## Independent mathematical checks

1. **Adaptive Gaussian argument (Section 2).** The unrevealed block formula follows from Gaussian conditioning on a new fixed query after each transcript. The remaining row/column block is independent in the orthogonal complements; adaptivity does not reveal extra information beyond the transcript. With fewer than k queries, the fresh row-answer support has dimension greater than the prior row-answer span. The two cases for whether X-perp lies in the proposed output space cover all outputs, including k close to n. In the second case, surjectivity of the projection of range(H) onto X forces range(H)+X-perp to be all of R^n. Both cases preclude success. Conditioning on a random seed and integration gives the randomized impossibility. Gaussian A-transpose samples establish the matching rank-promise upper bound.

2. **Upper bounds (Section 3).** Full column recovery takes n products. The stated right block Krylov space is obtained by applying the left-subspace result to A-transpose. Charging every frontier image and the final compression gives at most k(2t+2) products, rather than one charge per block. The logarithmic accuracy term is absorbed into log n in the branch where the faster bound is below n. Combining n/2 <= k with the rank lower bound proves the claimed Theta(n) regime.

3. **Angles and obstruction (Section 4).** The overlap estimate follows from the leading singular block and the projection identity. The displayed diagonal example attains residual 1+epsilon while its captured PCA fraction tends to zero. Therefore a direct unprocessed invocation of a PCA lower bound would be invalid; the manuscript does not make that inference.

4. **Warm-start theorem (Section 5).** Applying the residual inequality to (-F0-transpose y,y) gives precisely the weighted graph inequality. Both weighting matrices are positive when b>0. The contraction has squared Frobenius norm at most k. The trace-loss bound is valid for noncommuting head matrices: it uses positive-semidefinite trace inequalities and the fact that F-transpose F commutes with its own resolvent, not with the head eigenvalue matrix. The scalar coefficient is decreasing in the head eigenvalue. The tail split s>=epsilon versus s<=epsilon, Fejer bounds, arcosh bound and denominator estimate imply coefficient < epsilon*b/200. The good filtered graph lies in the constructed Krylov space even though the algorithm does not know b. Algebraic Ritz selection and retention of the final frontier images give the stated k*ceil(10/sqrt(epsilon)) query budget. The zero-tail case is valid separately; the leading eigenvalues are assumed positive. Replacing M by |M| can only increase the captured trace.

5. **Growing-rank deduction (Section 6).** Positive rank-k perturbation interlacing bounds all tail magnitudes by the GOE norm. On the stronger spectral event, b/a <= 1-19g/21. Both branches of g=min(4epsilon,1/2) imply the warm-start gap. The stronger event is contained in the conditioned event; unconditional probability at least 0.99 implies conditional probability at least 0.99. Since the approximation succeeds for every fixed matrix, the composition succeeds with probability at least 0.9801. The overhead subtraction is absorbed by log n>=44/c0. Enlarging a universal polynomial threshold includes this fixed condition. This argument retains, rather than discards, its essential dimension restriction.

6. **Padding (Section 7).** The top block C must have full row rank. For an orthonormal kernel basis N, DN is orthonormal and has the required dimension. Lifting each vector perpendicular to range(DN) to a vector perpendicular to range(Z) and using L>tau gives the residual inequality without loss. Each padded oracle call costs one underlying product. The bounded rank-one family permits the fixed padding scale 4 without estimating an unknown norm.
