## Mathematical audit

1. **Bounded lift and rank balance.** The equal-positive-column-sum functional bounds the orthant slice coordinatewise; removing zero summands is justified by minimality. A strictly positive average of the factor columns guarantees the stated affine dimension. The slack-affine-hull identity gives surjectivity onto the polygon. Applying the same construction to the transpose and Sylvester's rank inequality yields a lift of dimension at most four when the inner dimension is at most eight. The upper-bound-theorem capacities then exclude all lower-dimensional and fewer-facet cases for n >= 17.

2. **Finite geometry cover.** The sphere-generation moves check the required star/link and missing-simplex conditions, so they preserve a PL sphere. Starting from a simplex boundary and stellar subdivisions is sound. The exhaustive vertex-permutation canonicalization distinguishes the generated types. Completeness uses the external classification of 39 eight-vertex simplicial 3-spheres, including 37 polytopal ones. This count was corroborated against the published abstract of Bokowski and Guedes de Oliveira, “Nonconvex Embeddings of the Exceptional Simplicial 3-Spheres with 8 Vertices,” https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0097316501932285 . Keeping nonpolytopal or otherwise unrealizable types only enlarges the cover.

3. **Normal signs and shadow cycles.** In the generic configuration, strict preservation is equivalent to positive spanning of the restrictions of active normals to the projection kernel. A projective-line cut immediately before label zero, a common sign reversal, and all remaining permutations cover every generic rank-two normal configuration. The alternating-triple sign criterion is correct. The preserved edge graph of a genuine generic convex shadow is one cycle; retaining every single cycle is therefore a necessary cover. Cyclic shifts, reversal, and facet relabeling preserve the regular-polygon incidence question, justifying the canonicalizations.

4. **Degenerate lifts.** The limiting argument does not assume that perturbed shadows remain regular. Small generic facet perturbations preserve boundedness and the existing facets. Exposing directions for distinct original polygon vertices select distinct nearby shadow vertices in cyclic order. Finite combinatorial data permit a constant subsequence, and uniform boundedness permits convergent preimages. All four selected active facet equalities persist, while extra equalities in the limit cause no problem. Sectioning by the affine span of the selected points would otherwise produce an at-most-three-dimensional, at-most-eight-facet lift, contradicting vertex capacity. The required nonzero bivector therefore follows even for collapsed fibers and nonsimple original lifts.

5. **Incidence equations.** The three conic columns span the projected affine coordinate space, and every three-row minor is nonzero. Gauge elimination is therefore legitimate. The alternating signs in the five-minor expansion and the quadratic Plücker relations agree with the exterior-algebra model. They are used only as necessary conditions; positivity is not silently replaced by a sufficient complex feasibility test.

6. **Characteristic-zero specialization.** The modular lemma is sound. A nonzero specialized pivot minor gives a characteristic-zero relaxed kernel of exactly the modular nullity, containing the actual kernel. Its basis can be chosen over a localization with denominators nonzero at the chosen prime. The restricted Plücker coefficient matrix consequently specializes correctly, up to invertible basis changes. Independent complementary-pair vectors supply an upper bound on the quadratic row-space dimension, and the modular rank supplies the matching lower bound. Thus the entire space of quadratics vanishing on those coordinate axes is obtained. Over the complex numbers its common zero set is exactly their union (or the origin if there are no axes). This avoids the invalid inference from absence of finite-field points to absence of characteristic-zero points.

7. **Planar-section obstruction.** A decomposable vector on a complementary-pair axis forces the remaining n-2 lifted points into an affine plane. Because these are real points, equality of real and complex affine rank gives a real plane. Their distinct circle projections affinely span that plane, so projection is injective. The plane section of the original bounded eight-facet polytope has at most eight vertices, whereas all n-2 selected projected extreme points must remain extreme. This is the required contradiction.

8. **Upper certificates and arithmetic.** The polynomial quotient used for the exact product need not be a field: evaluating at the specified root proves the identity. Integer-angle bounds prove nonnegativity separately. Fixed-width arithmetic is safe for the supplied small primes and matrix sizes: modular products and their bounded sums are far below signed 64-bit overflow. The nullspace residual is explicitly checked.
