 Exact inverse-M interval solution hulls by 2n linear programs
                   Repository IV-05. Claimed resolution; independent review pending.



                                                 Abstract
            For a rational interval matrix whose every member is inverse-M, and an arbitrary rational
        interval right-hand side, we compute the exact solution hull using 2n linear programs with
        n variables and 2n inequalities each. A complementary-parts linear program solves the
        relevant absolute value equations. A sign argument using the inverse-M promise proves that
        their solutions are attained coordinatewise hull endpoints. All claims are in exact rational
        bit complexity; floating-point test code is supplementary only.


1    Problem and notation
Write
                  A = [C − R, C + R],       R ≥ 0,       b = [bc − br , bc + br ],   br ≥ 0.
Assume every A ∈ A is inverse-M: A ≥ 0, A is nonsingular, and A−1 has nonpositive off-diagonal
entries. Its inverse is then a nonsingular M-matrix and has positive diagonal entries. The
requested hull is the smallest coordinate box containing

                               Σ = {x : Ax = b for some A ∈ A, b ∈ b}.

This is the problem in IV-05 [1, 2]. The promise suffices for the algorithm; it can separately be
checked by the criterion proposed in the accompanying IV-03 manuscript.


2    A bijective absolute value map
For a fixed sign vector σ ∈ {−1, 1}n , define

                                         Hσ (x) = Cx − Dσ R|x|.

Every selection matrix C − Dσ RDs , and indeed every matrix C − Dσ RDd for d ∈ [−1, 1]n ,
belongs to A.
Lemma 1. If every matrix in A is nonsingular, then Hσ is a bijection of Rn onto Rn .

Proof. For x, y, write |x| − |y| = Dd (x − y), where di = (|xi | − |yi |)/(xi − yi ) when xi ̸= yi , and
di = 0 otherwise. Each |di | ≤ 1. Thus

                               Hσ (x) − Hσ (y) = (C − Dσ RDd )(x − y),

which proves injectivity.
Compactness of the regular interval family gives a uniform positive lower bound α on its
smallest singular values. Hence ∥Hσ (x)∥ ≥ α∥x∥. For any b, the continuous squared residual
∥Hσ (x) − b∥2 is coercive and has a global minimizer x.
Put r = Hσ (x) − b, and suppose r =  ̸ 0. Let Js range over the finitely many selection matrices
whose signs agree with all nonzero coordinates of x. Every convex combination of these matrices
lies in the regular interval family. Therefore 0 is not in the compact convex hull of the vectors
JsT r: an equality 0 = J¯T r would contradict nonsingularity of J. ¯ Let g ̸= 0 be the point in
that convex hull nearest to 0. The projection inequality gives g T JsT r ≥ ∥g∥2 for every adjacent

3    Solving the map by a linear program
Fix σ and a rational b. Put

                 P = C − Dσ R,        Q = C + Dσ R,           X = Q−1 ,    Y = P −1 .

The matrices P, Q are inverse-M. Thus X, Y have positive diagonal and nonpositive off-diagonal
entries. Consider the linear program in the free variable y ∈ Rn :

                       min 1T y    subject to Xy ≥ 0,            Y (y + b) ≥ 0.                  (1)

Lemma 2. The program (1) has an optimum. For every optimum, setting

                            v = Xy,        u = Y (y + b),       x=u−v

gives the unique solution of Hσ (x) = b.

Proof. Lemma 1 supplies a root x. With u = x+ and v = x− , one has P u − Qv = b. Thus
y = Qv is feasible in (1). For every feasible y, v = Xy ≥ 0 implies y = Qv ≥ 0. Consequently
the objective is bounded below, and its feasible sublevel sets are compact. An optimum exists.
At an optimum, write u = Y (y + b) ≥ 0, v = Xy ≥ 0. Suppose uk > 0 and vk > 0. Decrease yk
slightly. The kth entries of u, v decrease by the positive diagonal coefficients Ykk , Xkk and remain
nonnegative for a sufficiently small decrease. Every other entry weakly increases because the
off-diagonal coefficients of X, Y are nonpositive. This gives a feasible strict objective decrease,
a contradiction. Hence uk vk = 0 for every k.
It follows that x = u − v has |x| = u + v. Since P u − Qv = b, we obtain Cx − Dσ R|x| = b. The
root is unique by Lemma 1.


4    The exact hull endpoints
Theorem 3. For coordinate i, choose σi = 1 and σj = −1 for j ̸= i. The unique solution of

                                        Hσ (x) = bc + Dσ br

has xi = maxz∈Σ zi . Reversing all these signs gives xi = minz∈Σ zi . Consequently 2n instances
of (1) compute the exact hull.

Proof. Let bσ = bc + Dσ br , and let x solve the stated equation. For any true solution z with
A′ z = b′ , rowwise interval evaluation gives
                                                       
                                       Dσ Hσ (z) − bσ ≤ 0.

Indeed, a row with σj = 1 uses the minimum possible row product Cj,: z − Rj,: |z|, which is at
most b′j and hence at most the upper right-hand-side endpoint. A row with σj = −1 uses the
maximum possible row product, which is at least b′j and hence at least the lower endpoint.

Write w = Dσ (Hσ (z) − bσ ) ≤ 0. The secant identity from Lemma 1 yields

                                z − x = M −1 Dσ w,       M ∈ A.

For the upper-endpoint sign choice, row i of M −1 Dσ is entrywise nonnegative: its diagonal
entry is positive, and each off-diagonal entry is a nonpositive entry of M −1 multiplied by −1.
Thus zi ≤ xi . For the reversed sign choice that row is nonpositive, giving zi ≥ xi .
The bounds are attained. Choose any signs s consistent with the nonzero coordinates of x.
Then A∗ = C − Dσ RDs ∈ A, and

                                     A∗ x = Hσ (x) = bσ ∈ b.

Hence x ∈ Σ, proving both endpoint assertions.


5    Exact complexity and limitations
Each of the 2n programs has n variables and 2n inequalities. Exact inversion of the rational
endpoint matrices P, Q produces polynomial-bit rational coefficients. Polynomial-time rational
linear programming [3] and exact postprocessing therefore give polynomial bit complexity for
the hull. The interval family is compact and regular, so the solution set is nonempty and
bounded for every nonempty interval right-hand side.
The argument does not supply a polynomial algorithm for general regular absolute value
equations (AV-03). It uses the nonpositive off-diagonal signs of P −1 and Q−1 in the coordinate-
decrease proof of complementarity. General regularity alone does not provide those signs. The
bijectivity lemma alone is an existence result, not a general polynomial-time search algorithm.
