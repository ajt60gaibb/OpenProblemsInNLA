# Notes — SP-09

The key ingredient is a dimension-intersection obstruction: if the a-eigenspace
has dimension p and fewer than p eigenvalues of B are within t of a, there is
a nonzero vector simultaneously in that eigenspace and in B's spectral subspace
at distance strictly greater than t. This contradicts an operator-norm bound t.

For two target spectral points, the two capacity inequalities plus coverage are
also sufficient: assign eigenvalues close only to one point there, then fill
remaining capacities with eigenvalues close to both. This elementary sufficiency
is the special feature of two points. For three or more points, single-capacity
conditions need not imply all subset conditions for a matching, and even an
unrestricted spectral matching theorem would not automatically determine unitary
orbit distance. The original SP-07 constant-one conjecture is already false.

Do not infer the finite-k result from an infinite amplification example, and do
not remove normality: orthogonality of spectral subspaces is used essentially.
The exact squared-distance tests avoid irrational comparisons for a rational
complex grid. They verify the combinatorial threshold formula, not every unitary.
The proof supplies the norm lower bound for all unitaries.

The 2025 follow-up arXiv:2508.13834v1, Section 3.1 / Corollary 3.5, was checked
for the self-adjoint case. The current literature search is not exhaustive.
