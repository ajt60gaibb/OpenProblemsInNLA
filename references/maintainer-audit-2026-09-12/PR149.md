### Product-bounded reference estimate (Lemma 1, lines 43–89)

The supremum over all reference products defines a finite extremal norm v. The functions g_n are seminorms bounded by v, decrease after deleting a leftmost contraction, and have a common Lipschitz bound. Their limit p is therefore continuous and convergence on compact sets is uniform. The recursion passes through the compact maximum and gives p(x)=max_A p(Ax).

The kernel S of p is common invariant. For a fixed basis of S the maximal length-n product images tend to zero. Finite-dimensional norm equivalence makes the restricted operator norms tend uniformly to zero; one finite length therefore contracts strictly, giving restricted radius <1. Applying the same argument to the whole space would contradict the normalized radius one, so the quotient is nonzero. This establishes the stable kernel rather than assuming it. The quotient p-norm admits, at each vector, a reference generator preserving its norm exactly.

Use an adapted stable norm, so ||U_A||<=q<1, and an upper-right block bound K. With H=(K+1)/(1-q), L=H+1, a vector in a<=Hb has a matched perturbed image satisfying b'>=(1-Lt)b and a'<=(H-1+Lt)b, where t=c_0 d_H(M,N). The decisive cone condition is

`H(1-Lt) - (H-1+Lt) = 1-L^2 t >= 0`.

Taking t<=L^(-2) also ensures positive quotient growth. Matching the maximizing reference generator at each successive vector constructs legal words in N with growth at least (1-Lt)^n. A fixed vector-norm comparison disappears under nth roots. This proves the desired lower bound without assuming a maximizing periodic word or invariant blocks in N. If S=0, the first coordinate is absent and the same argument applies.

### Maximal critical exterior degree (Lemma 5, lines 184–239)

A maximal common invariant flag has irreducible quotient blocks. Blocks with radius below one are product bounded; blocks with radius one are product bounded by the compact irreducible extremal-norm theorem. Every tensor allocation of exterior degrees consequently has uniformly bounded products. The exterior representation is triangular in the allocation order: a strict original block transition moves an exterior factor toward an earlier block and cannot preserve an allocation. Hence its diagonal families are exactly the displayed paired allocation families.

All positive exterior radii are at most one. Choose the greatest degree k with radius one; k exists because degree one qualifies. Two distinct degree-k allocations a,b have coordinatewise maximum c of degree greater than k and minimum e. For each common word,

`||P_a|| ||P_b|| = ||P_c|| ||P_e|| <= (product K_i^(e_i)) ||P_c||`.

This is an exact reordering of two exterior-norm factors within each block. It does not assert a false multiplicative inequality between different exterior degrees. It also remains valid for singular matrices and zero factors, since there is no division; degree-zero exterior factors are identities. Taking maxima over common words and nth roots places every paired allocation tensor radius below the degree-|c| exterior radius, which is strictly less than one by maximality of k. Lemma 4 therefore proves product boundedness of the full critical exterior power. If k=d, its sole allocation is already bounded; the pairwise condition is vacuous. Fixed similarities cause no problem for boundedness or radius, including on exterior powers.

### Transfer (lines 243–278)

The degree-k exterior map is locally Lipschitz on a bounded matrix ball, by tensor telescoping and restriction to the antisymmetric subspace, with factor k L_0^(k-1). It carries nonempty compact families to nonempty compact families even when noninjective. Matching both Hausdorff directions gives the displayed set-distance bound. Shrinking the neighborhood to keep the right side positive and taking kth roots yields rho(N)>=(1-C delta)^(1/k)>=1-C delta. This also handles k=1 directly.
