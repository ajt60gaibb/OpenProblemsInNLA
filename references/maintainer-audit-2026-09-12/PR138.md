## Independent reconstruction of the proof

1. **Degree lower bound (lines 63–135).** Free linear combinations do not raise the current maximum degree, and one multiplication at most doubles it, regardless of reuse. Thus every comparison polynomial has degree at most 2^m. For a degree-D polynomial with sign error e < 1, its odd part has no larger error and can be squared into B(x^2), with B(0)=0 and degree(B) <= D. On [delta^2,1], B lies between (1-e)^2 and (1+e)^2. Consequently H=(1+e^2-B)/(2e) is bounded by one there. The displayed exterior Chebyshev inequality is proved by alternating signs at the D+1 Chebyshev extrema plus an additional exterior zero; strict c>1 avoids endpoint ambiguities. Applying it at the affine image of zero yields (e+1/e)/2 <= (r^D+r^(-D))/2, where r=(1-delta)/(1+delta). The function on the left decreases strictly for e in (0,1), giving e >= r^D. The e>=1 and e=0 cases are correctly separated. Therefore E_m >= r^(2^m)>0 and C_T >= r^(3^T)>0, without assuming either infimum is attained.

2. **Constructive cubic bound (lines 139–202).** For A=1+a+a^2, the derivative of x(A-x^2) vanishes only at sqrt(A/3) on [a,1]. This point lies strictly inside, both endpoints have value a(1+a), and the interior maximum is M=2A^(3/2)/(3sqrt(3)). The normalization is therefore exact. Squaring the positive quantities in the comparison phi(a)>=2a/(1+a^2) produces the displayed degree-six identity, whose remainder is (1-a)^2 times a polynomial with positive coefficients. Applying the decreasing function (1-v)/(1+v) then squares the interval ratio. Iteration gives an odd composition whose positive range is [a_T,1]. Centering by 2/(1+a_T) is absorbed into the final cubic's two coefficients and incurs no additional stage. Hence C_T <= r^(2^T) for T>=1. Coefficients may depend on m, T and delta, exactly as permitted by the target.

3. **Strict improvement at an infimum (lines 206–238).** This is necessary for the lower bound and is supplied, not assumed. Starting with any near-optimal T-stage composition with error e<1, division by 1+e puts its positive output in [(1-e)/(1+e),1]. One additional centered cubic yields error at most e^2. Taking a sequence approaching C_T proves C_(T+1)<=C_T^2<C_T; positivity comes from the degree bound. The T=0 transition uses the explicit bound C_1<=r^2<1-delta=C_0. Thus nonattainment of optimal coefficients is harmless.

4. **Budget comparison and endpoints (lines 242–270).** The constructive upper bound gives C_m<=r^(2^m)<=E_m for m>=1, so admissible stage counts exist and T_min<=m. Conversely every k-stage composition uses at most 2k nonscalar products. Taking k=floor(m/2) gives E_m<=C_k. Strict decrease excludes every T<k from reaching E_m. At m=0 and m=1, the optimal odd part is linear, and 2x/(1+delta) shows E_0=E_1=r. Since C_0>r and C_1<r, both exceptional stage counts equal one. Finally floor(m/2)>=(m+1)/4 for m>=2, giving the claimed global uniform Theta(m+1).

No matrix-size-specific identity, coefficient-cost charge, attainment assumption, asymptotic exchange, or limiting-gap assumption is hidden in these steps. The excluded gap endpoints delta=0 and delta=1 are not used; all estimates remain valid for arbitrary sequences of strictly interior gaps as m varies. The theorem does not calculate E_m or an optimal leading constant, and the manuscript does not say that it does.
