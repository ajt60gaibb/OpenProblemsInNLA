## Independent analytic verification

### 1. Deterministic data and the selected lower bounds

`solution.md:22–45,68–90`; TeX 40–62,80–97.

For 0<epsilon<1/4 the spectra of 2I, diag(epsilon,2epsilon), diag(0,1) are pairwise disjoint. Their extrema are a=0,c=2. In root ordering k=1, the transformed second block is an eligible term of chi_mono; its denominator is exactly ||Lambda_2||=2epsilon, which is nonzero. The first-block cross-gap is min(2-epsilon,2-2epsilon,2,1)=1. Hence the separate global maxima are bounded below by ||Bhat_2||/(2epsilon) and ||S_13^-1||^(1/2), respectively. Multiplying these lower bounds is valid because all factors are nonnegative; no requirement that the two maxima occur at the same ordering is imposed or needed. Here both lower bounds already use k=1.

### 2. The singular similarity transformation has a nonzero limit almost surely

`solution.md:95–186`; TeX 103–162.

Put X=Omega_2^-1 diag(1,2) Omega_2 and P=Omega_3^-1 diag(0,1) Omega_3. Then det X=2, P²=P, tr P=1, det P=0 and adj P=I-P. The recurrence at d=3 gives S_23=epsilon X-P and Bhat_2=S_23^-1 epsilon X S_23. For two-by-two matrices, direct determinant expansion gives

    det(epsilon X-P)=epsilon(2epsilon-kappa),
    kappa=tr((I-P)X).

Linearity of the two-by-two adjugate gives the exact displayed rational expression. Its limit when kappa!=0 is N=-(I-P)XP/kappa; the signs and cancelled epsilon factor are correct. Since P(I-P)=0, N²=0. This alone would not ensure nonzero N, so the separate nonvanishing argument is essential and is supplied.

Independently multiplying the displayed rational witness Omega_2=I, Omega_3=[[1,2],[3,5]] gives P=[[6,10],[-3,-5]], kappa=7 and Q=(I-P)XP=[[30,50],[-18,-30]]. Thus both the kappa numerator and Q_11 numerator are nonzero polynomials after clearing determinant denominators. A nonzero real polynomial has Lebesgue-null zero set, and the eight independent Gaussian entries have an everywhere absolutely continuous joint law. Together with invertibility of the Gaussian matrices, this proves kappa!=0 and Q_11!=0 almost surely. A nonzero nilpotent limit N therefore exists and ||Bhat_2(epsilon)|| tends to ||N||>0.

The exact optional witness formula at Markdown 188–204 also checks by direct multiplication: its numerator entries are 2epsilon²-7epsilon+30, 20epsilon+50, 3epsilon-18, and 4epsilon²-14epsilon-30. At the limit the squared Frobenius norm of the numerator of 7N is 4624=68²; rank one makes its spectral norm equal to 68, so ||N||=68/7. This is an independent algebra check, not reliance on an outcome of zero Gaussian probability or on submitted computations.

### 3. The coefficient factor cannot compensate for divergence

`solution.md:208–242`; TeX 181–202.

Because the first block is scalar, B_1=2I independently of Omega_1. The literal recurrence gives S_12=2I-Bhat_2 and S_13=(2I-Bhat_2)(2I-P), in that order. No commutation is used. When S_23 exists, Bhat_2 is similar to diag(epsilon,2epsilon), so

    det S_13=2(2-epsilon)(2-2epsilon),

which is strictly positive and below eight on the specified epsilon interval. The smallest singular value satisfies s_min²<=s_max s_min=|det S_13|; hence ||S_13^-1||^(1/2)>=|det S_13|^(-1/4)>=8^(-1/4). This lower bound is independent of both epsilon and the Gaussian matrices. The exponent is exactly 1/(d-1)=1/2. The proof does not mistake an upper determinant bound for an upper bound on the inverse norm.

### 4. All required random variables are defined

`solution.md:244–278`; TeX 205–217.

The convergence argument must not ignore singularities in other root orderings. For each fixed admissible epsilon, all recurrence entries are rational functions of the three Gaussian matrices. At the deterministic witness where all are identities, every transformed block stays diagonal and S_ij is the coordinatewise product of diagonal differences. Pairwise disjoint spectra make every factor nonzero, including in every reordered list. Induction through the finitely many inversions therefore supplies a nonzero numerator polynomial for each required determinant, and the union of the exceptional sets is null. This verifies all root orderings, not just k=1.

The sequence epsilon_m=1/(m+5), m>=1, is deterministic and lies in (0,1/4). Intersecting the full-measure events across this countable sequence is valid. Scalar-block endpoint 0/0 ratios may arise in other orderings, but the canonical convention defines them as one; none of the lower bounds uses such a ratio. Omega_1 is also included in the almost-sure inversion event, despite the first B block simplifying algebraically.

### 5. Correct marginal probability quantifiers

`solution.md:282–317`; TeX 222–240.

Using the same three Gaussian matrices at all sequence indices preserves each fixed input's prescribed marginal distribution, while allowing an almost-sure comparison across indices. On the full-measure event already constructed,

    chi_mono(epsilon_m) chi_coef(epsilon_m)
      >=8^(-1/4) ||Bhat_2(epsilon_m)||/(2epsilon_m) -> infinity.

For every fixed finite C, the indicators of products <=C converge almost surely to zero and are bounded by one. Dominated convergence therefore gives the exact marginal-probability limit stated in the theorem. If C(2,3,1/2) existed, all those probabilities at that same finite threshold would be at least 1/2, a contradiction. The deterministic spectrum never depends on the sample. The coupling does not replace the original question by a stronger simultaneous-for-all-spectra event. Negative or zero C presents no exception; the products are nonnegative and the same limit applies.

A counterexample for one fixed allowed pair b=2,d=3 refutes the universal catalog conjecture. It does not classify every other block size or depth, and the manuscript does not claim that stronger conclusion.
