## Independent reconstruction

1. **Hadamard property (Lemma 1, equation (3)).** The two rows assigned to one pair have inner product m-m=0. For different pairs i,j, their inner product is A_i dot A_j plus or minus B_i dot B_j, both zero separately. Every row has length squared 2m. This holds for every A,B in the stated domain, with no compatibility hypothesis and no reliance on their equivalence classes.
2. **Injection, the potentially decisive counting issue.** In every output C, equality of first-half row vectors recovers the matching uniquely. Two different rows of A cannot agree because their real inner product would be m, contradicting orthogonality. Each such vector appears exactly twice. Sorting each pair by its smaller row label and sorting pairs by that label recovers A in its original row order. The second half of the smaller-indexed row recovers B including its sign. Thus two different triples cannot produce the same C. Global permutations and automorphisms do not create uncounted fibers because C has fixed row and column labels and the inverse is explicit.
3. **Matching normalization (equation (2)).** Listing all 2m labels, grouping adjacent labels and forgetting pair orientation/order gives `(2m)!/(2^m m!)` matchings, exactly. The unrelated row-permutation implementation discussed later has fibers `2^m m!`; that implementation is not confused with the injective matching formulation.
4. **All cases.** For m where H(m)=0, the domain is empty and the inequality remains true. For m=1 it gives four distinct order-two outputs from the four ordered A,B choices, which is a legitimate subset. For the iteration, existence at powers of two follows from the construction itself, so every logarithm is defined. No all-admissible-orders existence assumption is introduced.
5. **Exponent and contradiction (Theorem 1).** With n=2m=2^k, division by 2m changes the doubled logarithm term exactly to `a_(k-1)`. The elementary estimate `(2m-1)!! >= m! >= (m/2)^(m/2)` gives increment at least `(k-2)/4`. Summing k=2 through K gives `(K-1)(K-2)/8`; dropping the nonnegative base value is valid. Therefore `log_2 H(2^K)/(2^K K)` grows at least linearly in K. This contradicts every fixed C in the exact canonical bound along orders divisible by four. It proves a negative resolution, not merely a stronger lower bound compatible with the conjecture.
