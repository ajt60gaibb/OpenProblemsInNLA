## Independent proof audit

1. **Singular counting inequality (`solution.md:45–51`; TeX 114–123).** Truncating the singular expansions above positive a and b leaves a sum of norm at most a+b and a removed matrix of rank at most n_X(a)+n_Y(b). The variational singular-value bound yields the asserted inequality with the strict `>` counting convention. The trace-norm Markov bound follows directly. Zero singular values and equality at the thresholds cause no exception.

2. **Unbounded Hermitian comparison lemma (`solution.md:53–76`; TeX 125–162).** For each fixed s>0, split the Hermitian difference into its >s spectral part of rank o(n) and its <=s part. Hermitian rank perturbations change each counting function by at most the rank; integration by parts against a compactly supported C1 test function gives the displayed total-variation bound. The small-norm part shifts ordered eigenvalues by at most s, so the normalized difference is at most the global modulus of continuity. Taking n to infinity first, then s down to zero, and uniformly approximating C_c functions by C_c^1 functions proves the lemma. The approximation changes each normalized trace by at most the uniform error, independently of n and spectral location. Complex-valued test functions follow by the same absolute-value estimate or separate real/imaginary parts. No tightness, moment bound or bound on either matrix norm is silently used.

3. **Schur reduction (`solution.md:80–99`; TeX 167–189).** Every complex finite matrix admits unitary Schur form; no diagonalizability is required. With G=U*HU and Z=U*EU, its imaginary Hermitian part K equals Im Z and has trace norm at most q=||E||_*. At strictly upper entries K_ij=N_ij/(2i), giving N=2i T_+(K). The published weak estimate therefore gives n_N(s)<=2Cq/s for every size and threshold.

4. **Real and imaginary control (`solution.md:101–121`; TeX 191–218).** Scaling the counting inequality for (N+N*)/2 gives n_ReN(s)<=2n_N(s), hence 4Cq/s. Applying the inequality at s/2 to D-G=Re Z-Re N gives exactly (2+8C)q/s. The diagonal absolute sum of the Hermitian K is at most its trace norm by duality against its diagonal sign matrix. Thus the sum of absolute imaginary parts of all eigenvalues is at most q. Signs, factors of two, strict thresholds and the case q=0 are consistent.

5. **All prescribed test functions (`solution.md:123–149`; TeX 223–259).** Since q_n/n tends to zero, the Hermitian lemma compares the real parts D_n to H_n for the restriction of F to the real axis. The at-most q_n/s eigenvalues with imaginary part exceeding s contribute at most 2||F||_infinity q_n/(ns); all others contribute at most omega_F(s). A continuous compactly supported function on C is uniformly continuous on the entire plane, so both passages are valid even when real parts escape to infinity. Taking n first, then s, and adding the assumed H_n limit proves the exact distribution statement. Schur diagonal entries preserve algebraic multiplicities. There is no unsupported exchange of limits or integrals.
