## Independent mathematical checks

1. The phase of P(0), the pure-imaginary boundary values of R, and strict interlacing all hold with the odd grid size and arbitrary allowed shifts. Continuous deformation from the uniform grid keeps each real partial-fraction residue positive. There are no cancellations or zero residues during that deformation. The residue identity for beta and the bound on its total mass are correct.
2. Differentiating the logarithmic product gives the stated kernel, including its factor 1/2. Its radial-difference integral is exactly twice the logarithm of the coth ratio. The monotonicity used to bound that ratio has the stated direction. The cell-variation error is absolute because h/t <= 2 pi. These facts preserve the exponent 2 alpha with a constant uniform as alpha tends to zero or one half.
3. Moving every cardinal-product factor to radius exp(-1/m) avoids division by a boundary zero even at an interpolation node. On each dyadic annulus the Poisson mass estimate cancels the radial product at that annulus's height. The geometric sum has the required uniform alpha dependence, and cos(pi alpha) >= 1 - 2 alpha supplies exactly the allowed denominator. Replacing m by 2N+1 is uniform for N >= 2.
4. The sector function's linear logarithmic term matches the discrete root perturbation, including the factor i and the cell width. The Taylor/cell quadrature error is bounded at radius exp(-1/m). Both fractional powers have nonnegative real part, and their variation on one grid cell is controlled by an absolute factor.
5. The positive-real weak estimate follows from Markov's inequality for the real part, periodic weak (1,1) for its harmonic conjugate, and the constant imaginary part. For a q-cell arc, the local/far split has bounded far variation. The disk automorphism after radial composition centers the estimate at height qh, and its harmonic-measure density is bounded below by a constant divided by qh on the required arc. Cell comparability consequently gives q times u^(-1/(2 alpha)) for both reciprocal counting functions. The explicit logarithmic kernel integral cuts them off at a constant times q^(2 alpha). Integrating to that cutoff yields q^(4 alpha), using precisely alpha > 1/4. The large-arc case follows with m comparable to q.
6. The cardinal matrix is F0 times F inverse with the same row convention as the original target. Its weighted entry bound follows from the individual Poisson residue bound and the radial product comparison. At each distance scale one may partition into consecutive blocks of length R with at most one shorter final block; each has a bounded number of neighbors. An interval containing an interacting pair permits the same local normalization in both moment estimates, which cancels. Cauchy–Schwarz bounds each masked block by a constant times R^(4 alpha - 1). Bounded incidence controls the full scale. The scale sum is geometric, so there is no additional logarithm when alpha > 1/4.
