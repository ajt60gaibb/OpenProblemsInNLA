## Independent reconstruction of the proof

### 1. The block determinant identity and all shifts

Locations: `solution.md:24–77`; `solution.tex:80–152`.

For positive definite Hermitian P,Q, their positive roots X,Y satisfy X*X=P and YY*=Q. Direct multiplication of A-zI yields exactly the displayed three-by-three block Gram matrix. For every t>0 and complex z, s=t+|z|² is positive, and K=s²I+tP is positive definite. Eliminating the first block leaves K/s in the middle position. The determinant factors s^r and det(K/s) multiply to det K; thus no scalar factor is omitted.

The last Schur complement is

    sI + Y*(I - |z|² s K^(-1))Y.

Using det(sI+BC)=det(sI+CB) places Q=YY* on the right. Multiplying the resulting matrix on the left by K gives

    sK + (K-|z|²sI)Q = s³I + stP + stQ + tPQ.

In particular, the PQ order is correct. No P,Q commutativity, inversion of Q, or unproved identity for determinants of block matrices with noncommuting entries is used.

Factoring t from this r-by-r determinant gives t^r F(s³/t,s). If two pairs have the same polynomial F, the Gram determinant polynomials in t agree for every positive real t, separately for every fixed complex z. Polynomial identity then makes their roots agree with multiplicities. The roots are the negatives of the squared singular values; sorting their nonnegative square roots gives every requested singular value. This covers arbitrary complex phases of z, z=0, repeated singular values, and zero singular values. The proof never substitutes t=0 into an expression with 1/t; the polynomial argument handles the endpoint.

### 2. Nine real data coordinates

Locations: `solution.md:81–101`; `solution.tex:158–187`.

Each matrix entry defining F is affine in the independent indeterminates u,s, so the determinant has total degree at most r. For r=3 there are exactly ten monomials of degree at most three, one of whose coefficients (u³) is fixed as one. To establish reality, for real u,s write the defining matrix as (P+sI)(Q+sI)+(u-s²)I. Adjoint reverses the two Hermitian factors, and the determinant identity for BC+cI and CB+cI gives the same determinant. Hence the polynomial is real on all real u,s; the imaginary parts of its coefficients vanish. There are therefore at most nine real, not nine complex, varying coefficients. No surjectivity, coefficient independence, or converse from shifted data back to F is needed.

### 3. A genuine ten-dimensional family of distinct unitary classes

Locations: `solution.md:105–156`; `solution.tex:193–267`.

The domain Ω is open in ten real parameters. Positive definiteness, ordered positive p_j, and a,b>0 are strict open constraints and impose no equation. It is nonempty: the stated Q has eigenvalues 6,3,3, so is positive definite. The c,d coordinates are unrestricted locally subject to this open condition; the argument does not accidentally discard the complex phase parameter.

Invertibility of X,Y gives ker A equal to the first coordinate block and ker A² equal to the first two blocks. A unitary intertwiner between any two members must preserve these same flags. Unitarity then preserves their orthogonal differences and forces the intertwiner to be block diagonal. Its middle unitary U₂ simultaneously conjugates X*X=P and YY*=Q to P',Q'. Ordered simple spectra force P'=P and U₂ diagonal. Since a,a',b,b' are strictly positive real, their two phase equations force both relative diagonal phases to be one. Thus U₂ is scalar and Q'=Q. Every parameter is therefore equal.

This checks arbitrary unitary intertwiners of the full nine-dimensional matrices; it does not simply assume the visible block decomposition is invariant. It also eliminates permutations through the strict ordering. A possible transpose or complex-conjugate partner is not silently identified: unless all parameters agree it fails the same necessary intertwiner criterion. There is no need to prove the reverse direction of simultaneous pair conjugacy.

### 4. An actual infinite fiber

Locations: `solution.md:160–174`; `solution.tex:272–312`.

The coefficient map Φ is polynomial in ten real parameters and maps into R^9. Its maximum attained derivative rank k exists because the nonempty set of ranks is a subset of the finite set {0,...,9}; Ω need not be compact. At a point attaining k>0 a nonzero minor remains nonzero locally, while maximality prevents a higher rank. The separately covered k=0 case also gives local constant rank. Thus the constant-rank theorem's required neighborhood hypothesis really holds, rather than being inferred from a rank deficit at only one point.

The local fiber has dimension 10-k≥1. A coordinate line inside a sufficiently small manifold chart gives an injective smooth curve on an open interval. Shrinking this interval keeps it inside Ω. Thus positivity, simple ordered spectrum and both phase gauges are maintained. Equal Φ gives equal F; Section 1 gives the same complete shifted data; Section 3 gives pairwise distinct unitary classes. Global connectedness, properness, generic surjectivity, or a regular value of a surjective map are not needed. The standard constant-rank theorem is applied in precisely its usual smooth local form; no numerical or symbolic diagnostic certifies this step.

The positive definite square-root map is smooth. This standard fact can also be verified directly here by applying the implicit function theorem to X²=P on Hermitian matrices: the linearization H↦XH+HX is invertible when X>0, since in an eigenbasis it multiplies entries by the positive numbers λ_i+λ_j. Consequently the matrix family is smooth. Finally A³=0 and A² has sole upper-right nonzero block XY, which is invertible. Kernel dimensions 3,6,9 give exactly three Jordan blocks, all of size three. These observations establish the entire displayed theorem.
