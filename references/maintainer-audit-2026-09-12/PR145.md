## Detailed independent reconstruction

### Stable roots and phase (lines 31–110)

For each nontrivial mth root of unity omega, the reciprocal quadratic 2-r-r^(-1)=omega(2-2cos(theta)) has no unit-circle root for 0<theta<=pi. Its right side is either nonreal or negative, whereas the left side on the unit circle is real and nonnegative. Reciprocal roots therefore give exactly one stable root. No double root occurs away from theta=0: the critical right-side values would be 0 or 4. For the negative-real omega case, the stable root is positive real; the others occur in conjugate pairs. In particular the product Q of the unstable roots is positive.

The local square-root factorization gives r_l=1-kappa_l theta+O(theta^2), where Re(kappa_l)>0, and supplies a smooth extension through zero. Together with compactness away from zero it proves |r_l|<=exp(-c theta), with c>0 depending only on m. Each factor 1-r_l exp(-i theta) has positive real part. Dividing its simple zero at zero by theta gives the nonzero limit kappa_l+i, whose argument is pi*l/(2m). This proves the phase endpoint eta(0)=(m-1)pi/2. Conjugate pairing at pi gives eta(pi)=pi. The derivative bounds and smooth extension needed later are justified; no principal-argument jump is introduced.

### Exact determinant and uniform remainder (lines 112–215)

The order-2m recurrence has nonzero extreme coefficients and the 2m distinct characteristic roots listed in the manuscript for 0<theta<pi. Ghost values at 1-m,...,0 and n+1,...,n+m implement precisely the finite Toeplitz eigenvalue equation. Restricting recurrence solutions to the interior is injective on the boundary kernel, because zero interior and ghost values contain enough consecutive zeros to force the entire recurrence solution to vanish. Conversely the finite eigenvector equation extends to such a recurrence solution. Thus nullities agree, not just determinant zero sets.

I independently expanded the two largest-modulus Laplace terms. With root order (R,z,z^(-1),O), the products of Vandermonde factors for S_+={z} union O and S_-={z^(-1)} union O have opposite signs and factors

`V(R)V(O) Q z^(-(m-1)) conjugate(f)^2`

and

`V(R)V(O) Q z^(m-1) f^2`.

Multiplication by (z Q)^(n+m) and (z^(-1) Q)^(n+m), respectively, gives the difference

`Q^(n+m+1) [z^(n+1) conjugate(f)^2 - z^(-n-1) f^2]`.

The phase is consequently exactly (n+1)theta-2psi=(n+2)theta-eta. There is no index or grid shift error. Every other subset loses at least one stable-root modulus relative to Q, giving its exponential factor.

The normalized coefficients remain smooth at zero: each numerator Vandermonde product vanishes to order 2*binomial(m,2)=m(m-1). In the denominator V(R)V(O) contributes (m-1)(m-2), and |f|^2 contributes 2(m-1), the same total. All first root derivatives are distinct, so the leading coefficients are nonzero. Division therefore has a genuine smooth extension, rather than merely a formal cancellation. At pi the denominator remains nonzero. The finitely many coefficient derivatives and logarithmic derivatives of the b_S factors are bounded, yielding |E_n|<=C exp(-cn theta) and |E_n'|<=C(n+1) exp(-cn theta).

Conjugation changes the sign of both the full determinant and its normalizer (the stable/unstable permutations contribute the same parity and the oscillatory pair swaps), so E_n is real. At pi the two oscillatory columns coincide, giving E_n(pi)=0. Integrating the derivative estimate produces the extra factor pi-theta required at the upper endpoint.

### Eigenvalue indexing and endpoints (lines 219–278)

The quadratic-form integral places every finite eigenvalue strictly inside (0,4^m), so g has a unique eigenangle for it. For large n, F_n is strictly increasing with derivative comparable to n+2. After choosing a fixed sufficiently large J, each phase window |F_n-k*pi|<=pi/4, J<=k<=n, contains exactly one root: endpoint signs are opposite and the sine derivative dominates E_n'. A simple determinant zero cannot come from a boundary nullity of two or more, since then every first determinant derivative is zero. Symmetry of the Toeplitz matrix identifies geometric and algebraic multiplicity.

No root lies in the gaps between these windows. The final window around (n+1)pi is treated separately; there |sin F_n| is bounded below by c(n+2)(pi-theta), whereas the endpoint-cancelled E_n is exponentially smaller. Thus the apparent root at pi is artificial and no eigenangle precedes it in that last window. Counting downward from the largest eigenvalue labels the root in window k by index k; this avoids any unsupported low-index phase formula. The resulting phase error is O(exp(-cj)/(n+2)).

The circulant of size N=n+2m has A_n as a principal block: bandwidth-m Fourier coefficients cannot wrap into that block. Its sorted eigenvalues g(2*pi*floor(j/2)/N) have the stated ordering for even and odd N. Interlacing therefore bounds every low eigenvalue from above and, for j>=2, from below by (4j/(3(n+2m)))^(2m). The exclusion of j=1 from the latter estimate is correct and is dealt with separately in the trace argument.

### Construction of all required orders (lines 52–66 and 282–308)

The finite-dimensional implicit-function theorem applies uniformly on the compact x interval to Y=x+h eta(Y); the derivative with respect to Y is one at h=0. Smooth extensions handle the interval endpoints. All Taylor coefficients depend only on endpoint/interior jets of g and eta, so the coefficients are independent of the chosen extensions and of n,j.

Because g has a zero of order 2m, the coefficient d_k(x) is O(x^(2m-k)) for k<=2m. For j>=J the phase error and g'(theta)=O(theta^(2m-1)) give eigenvalue error at most C h^(2m) j^(2m-1) exp(-cj). For the finitely many j<J, interlacing and the coefficient vanishing estimates give O(h^(2m)) directly. Uniform Taylor remainders yield order 2m-1 across all indices and every lower order by omitting bounded coefficients. Above ceiling(log(n+2)^2), the exponential error is uniformly O(h^(2m+1)); applying the next Taylor order proves the exact second assertion. All thresholds can depend on m and are eventually compatible with the cutoff.

### Failure at order 2m (lines 312–382)

The external input is the **essential-uniform kernel limit**, not merely operator-norm convergence. For the present symbol its hypotheses hold with the smooth positive auxiliary symbol b=1. The grid-constant approximants and continuity of the limiting kernel allow the L-infinity bound to control their diagonal; a measure-zero objection would be invalid because each diagonal cell value persists on an open square. Integration then yields the inverse-trace limit.

I independently evaluated the kernel diagonal. At y=x, the substitution u=1-x/t turns the integrand into x^(-1)u^(2m-2) du. Multiplication by the prefactor gives x^(2m-1)(1-x)^(2m-1)/((2m-1)((m-1)!)^2); reflection covers x<1/2. The beta integral is precisely the positive rational number in equation (29). Replacing n^(-2m) by (n+2)^(-2m) changes its limit by a factor tending to one.

If the same order-2m coefficient expansion held uniformly for every index, fixed j would instead have h^(-2m)lambda_(n,j) -> pi^(2m)(j+(m-1)/2)^(2m). The interlacing lower bound supplies an n-independent summable bound C_m j^(-2m) for inverse terms j>=2. The positive fixed-j limit handles j=1. Thus dominated convergence, including zero extension past j=n, is valid and gives equation (31).

For odd m>=3 the shifted series is zeta(2m) minus a nonzero finite rational sum. For even m>=4 it is (2^(2m)-1)zeta(2m) minus another nonzero finite rational sum. Euler's formula leaves a rational number minus a positive rational divided by pi^(2m), which is irrational by transcendence of pi. This contradicts the rational kernel trace. No individual extreme-eigenvalue asymptotic is assumed in deriving the first trace limit.

The canonical third assertion concerns the same constructed functions, so this argument meets its exact quantifier. Even the stronger claim excluding any other continuous coefficient choice follows from the usual uniqueness argument: compare the bulk expansions at grid points tending to an arbitrary interior x, identify coefficients successively after division by powers of h, and use continuity at the endpoints. That extra argument is not needed for the literal canonical statement and is not a missing premise here.
