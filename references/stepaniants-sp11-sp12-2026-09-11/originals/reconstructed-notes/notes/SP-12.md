# SP-12 — A chromatic lower bound for positive-semidefinite nullity with SAP

**Classification:** literature-dependent affirmative resolution note; eligibility audit incomplete.

## Statement

With S(G), SAP, and nu(G) defined as in the companion SP-11 note, the target in the repository [R12] is

    nu(G) >= chi(G) - 1

for every finite simple graph G with at least one vertex. The principal external input is Hall's stated bound nu(F) >= delta(F) for every finite simple graph F [H, Corollary 3.22]. The following proof supplies the remaining implication explicitly.

## Lemma: induced-subgraph monotonicity

If H is an induced subgraph of G, then nu(H) <= nu(G).

### Proof for addition of one vertex

It suffices to treat G obtained by adding one vertex v to H. Let H have h vertices. Choose A in S(H) positive semidefinite with SAP and rank r. We construct a positive-semidefinite SAP matrix in S(G) with rank r+1 and thus the same nullity as A.

Let M_r be the smooth manifold of h-by-h positive-semidefinite symmetric matrices of rank r, in a neighborhood of A. Let P send a symmetric matrix to its entries at unordered nonedges of H. We first show that the differential of P restricted to M_r is surjective at A.

In an orthonormal basis adapted to the range and kernel of A, tangent matrices to M_r have the block form

    [ S   T  ]
    [ T^t 0  ],

where S is an arbitrary symmetric r-by-r matrix and T is arbitrary. Consequently, its orthogonal complement in the space of symmetric matrices consists precisely of symmetric X with AX = 0. If the differential of P were not surjective, its adjoint would produce a nonzero X supported only on off-diagonal nonedges of H and normal to M_r. This would give AX = 0, zero diagonal, and zero entries on every edge of H, contradicting SAP. The differential is therefore surjective.

Write z_i = 1 when i is adjacent to v in G and z_i = 0 otherwise, and put b = epsilon z. By the submersion theorem there are matrices D_epsilon in M_r, converging to A as epsilon tends to zero, such that

    (D_epsilon)_{ij} = -b_i b_j

at every nonedge {i,j} of H. Define

    B_epsilon = [ 1      b^t                  ]
                [ b      D_epsilon + b b^t  ].

The Schur complement of its top-left entry is D_epsilon. Hence B_epsilon is positive semidefinite and has rank r+1. Its old nonedge entries are zero by construction. Its old edge entries remain nonzero for sufficiently small epsilon, since they converge to those of A. Taking epsilon nonzero makes its new edge entries precisely the nonzero coordinates of b. Thus B_epsilon belongs to S(G).

It remains to check SAP. Let Z_G be the fixed vector space of symmetric matrices with zero diagonal and zero entries on all edges of G. At

    B_0 = diag(1, A),

the map X -> B_0 X on Z_G is injective. Indeed, its first row forces the first row of X to vanish; symmetry forces the first column to vanish. The remaining block X_H then satisfies AX_H = 0 and is supported only on off-diagonal nonedges of H, so SAP of A forces X_H = 0.

Injectivity of a linear map between finite-dimensional spaces is open in its matrix entries: an injective map has a nonzero maximal-column-rank minor that stays nonzero under a sufficiently small perturbation. Therefore X -> B_epsilon X remains injective on Z_G for sufficiently small epsilon. Since B_epsilon has exactly the pattern G, this injectivity is precisely its SAP.

We have preserved nullity:

    nullity(B_epsilon) = (h+1) - (r+1) = h-r.

Choosing A with nullity nu(H), then adding omitted vertices one at a time, proves the lemma. The rank-zero case is covered by the same argument: M_0 is the zero-dimensional manifold {0}, and surjectivity is forced by SAP exactly as above.

## The chromatic bound

Set k = chi(G). Choose an induced subgraph H minimal in vertex count subject to chi(H) = k. For k >= 2, deleting any vertex of H leaves a graph colorable with at most k-1 colors. If any vertex had degree at most k-2, a (k-1)-coloring after its deletion would leave an unused color among its neighbors, extending to H. That is a contradiction. Therefore delta(H) >= k-1. When k = 1, the target nu(G) >= 0 is immediate.

For k >= 2, combine the lemma, Hall's theorem, and this degree inequality:

    nu(G) >= nu(H) >= delta(H) >= k-1 = chi(G)-1.

This proves the requested implication from Hall's Strong Delta Theorem without assuming Hadwiger's conjecture.

## Scope and attribution

Hall's all-graph bound is essential and is not independently proved here. The induced-subgraph argument above is a proof of the monotonicity needed for this deduction; no novelty is asserted for that standard property or for the chromatic implication. This is a literature-dependent resolution note, not a new independent solution of the central existence problem. It has not undergone external review.
