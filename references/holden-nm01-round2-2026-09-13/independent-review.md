## Proof findings

1. **Coordinate reduction, optimal value, and rationality.** With independent data columns B, normalization of the hidden coefficients supplies the row functional that gives e^T Y=e^T and e^T T=e^T. The determinant comparison correctly applies Hadamard followed by the arithmetic-geometric mean to rows of the inverse change of coordinates. Equality forces an orthogonal matrix and then the weak SSC permutation condition. The facet-support proof correctly shows that columns on each hidden coordinate facet span dimension r-1: a tangent perturbation of e_i would otherwise violate dual circular-cone containment. Consequently inverse-factor rows are scaled rational facet normals, and normalization determines rational positive scales. The very loose polynomial height bounds are compatible with cofactor, denominator-product and inversion bounds. No polynomial facet-enumeration claim is made in variable rank.

2. **Projective identity and strong-SSC search-to-decision reduction.** The transformed factors multiply to the projective data and preserve coefficient column sums. Positive diagonal perturbations keep coordinate rays fixed and move all other extreme rays within the strict dual-cone interior when the asserted uniform slack is used. Rational height bounds supply a positive gap with polynomial bit length; the stated safe probing grid respects that gap. The moment-curve directions have at most (r-1) binom(r,2) collisions in total. Forming Q(z)=z^r P(1/z) keeps degree r even for a zero projection. The derivative identity S_a(z_i)/(delta Q'(z_i)) correctly recovers entries, including zero roots. Query counts and interpolation degrees are polynomial. Rational reconstruction from threshold bisections uses a sufficiently narrow interval and bounded denominator; randomized answers can be amplified with a polynomial union bound. The stated all-input loop and bit caps address runtime on off-promise inputs. This is a conditional equivalence on the strong subclass, not an algorithm for the original weak promise.

3. **Weak SSC and diagonal rigidity.** The six displayed dual contacts all have unit norm and sum one; strict convexity excludes additional boundary directions. An orthogonal frame contained in this dual cone must consist of boundary contacts. The noncoordinate contacts are nonorthogonal to every possible distinct frame member, so only the coordinate frame works. The positive tangent balance 243 b_1 + 361 b_2 + 392 b_3=0, together with the Cauchy-Schwarz upper bounds, forces equality and hence scalar diagonal scaling. The proof correctly distinguishes weak from strong SSC.

4. **Entire-promise failure on the punctured interval.** A putative SSC factorization must select three independent extreme dual facets by the facet-support argument. Normalization forces the listed affine scales. Exact endpoint signs classify all 20 triples; exact Bernstein coefficients certify the five feasible nonidentity candidates are strictly worse than the feasible identity candidate throughout the entire closed interval. A promised generating factor is globally optimal by the determinant argument, so it must be the identity candidate; the rigidity theorem then excludes it for nonzero t. This does rule out every SSC factorization, rather than merely the inherited factorization. I independently reproduced these finite computations, as detailed below.

5. **Contact-count SOS obstruction.** At each contact every square-times-generator summand is nonnegative and therefore vanishes. A product of t linear generators covers at most 2t contacts. With M>2D, the remaining contacts exceed twice the degree of its square root polynomial. The circle zero-count lemma therefore forces every square root to be divisible by f, contradicting an identity for f since the right side is divisible by f squared. The degree accounting is valid for the full squarefree input preordering, including empty and maximal subsets.

6. **Strong-SSC perturbations with polynomial encoding.** At zero perturbation, each quotient-basis localizing matrix is strictly positive definite: more than 2k positive-weight contact evaluations separate all nonzero basis polynomials. Small positive perturbations preserve these finitely many strict inequalities and polygon incidences. The manuscript additionally supplies the necessary uniform polynomial height/degree bounds on the rational principal minors and orientation tests; thus the existence of polynomial-bit perturbation parameters does not rely on continuity alone. Exponentially many subsets do not invalidate the uniform coefficient-height bound. The expanded-circle functional is nonnegative on every truncated-preordering term and negative on f, as required. This validates the all-degree existence statement, not a polynomial-time verifier for all subsets.

7. **Dense hierarchy size.** Appending coordinate rays preserves the strong SSC dual-boundary characterization. Restricting the padded certificate to the original three coordinates gives the forbidden rank-three certificate. The monomial dimension at any sufficient dense level is exponential in rank for the constructed family, whose input encoding is polynomial in rank. The manuscript correctly limits this conclusion to standard dense levels and notes that the family has a known factorization and optimum one. It establishes no general computational hardness for NM-01.
