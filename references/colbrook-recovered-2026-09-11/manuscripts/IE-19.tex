\begin{abstract}
A $3\times3$ positive strictly diagonally dominant matrix refutes the displayed entrywise inverse-norm inequality. Under the stated entrywise upper bounds, the sharp inverse infinity-norm infimum is $1/(\alpha+m)$, rather than the value at $S=\alpha I+m\one\one^T$. The conclusion concerns entrywise ordering, not a stronger order also comparing diagonal-dominance margins.
\end{abstract}
\section{Counterexample}
Let $n\ge3$, $m>0$, $\alpha\ge(n-2)m$, and $S=\alpha I+m\one\one^T$. The target as recovered asks whether a symmetric, entrywise positive, diagonally dominant $J\le S$ entrywise must have $\norm{J^{-1}}_\infty\ge\norm{S^{-1}}_\infty$.

For $n=3$, $m=\alpha=1$, take
\[
S=\begin{pmatrix}2&1&1\\1&2&1\\1&1&2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
J=\begin{pmatrix}2&1/2&1/2\\1/2&2&1/2\\1/2&1/2&2\end{pmatrix}.
\]
All entries of $J$ are positive, $J\le S$ entrywise, and $J$ is strictly diagonally dominant. Yet
\[
S^{-1}=I-\tfrac14\one\one^T,\quad J^{-1}=\tfrac23I-\tfrac19\one\one^T,
\qquad\boxed{\norm{J^{-1}}_\infty=\tfrac79<\tfrac54=\norm{S^{-1}}_\infty.}
\]
\section{Sharp infimum}
\begin{theorem}
For every admissible $n,m,\alpha$, over the stated class,
\[\boxed{\inf_J\norm{J^{-1}}_\infty=\frac1{\alpha+m}.}\]
Strict positivity of all entries prevents attainment.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
All such $J$ are positive definite, because
\[
x^TJx=\sum_i\left(J_{ii}-\sum_{j\ne i}J_{ij}\right)x_i^2
+\sum_{i<j}J_{ij}(x_i+x_j)^2.
\]
Every term is nonnegative, and for $n\ge3$, vanishing of all $x_i+x_j$ forces $x=0$. Cauchy--Schwarz applied to $J^{1/2}e_i,J^{-1/2}e_i$ gives $J_{ii}(J^{-1})_{ii}\ge1$. Equality would require $e_i$ to be an eigenvector, impossible with positive off-diagonal entries. With $D=\alpha+m$,
\[
\norm{J^{-1}}_\infty\ge(J^{-1})_{ii}>1/J_{ii}\ge1/D.
\]
For $0<\varepsilon\le m$, let $J_\varepsilon=(D-\varepsilon)I+\varepsilon\one\one^T$. Its diagonal is $D$, its off-diagonal entries are $\varepsilon$, and $D\ge(n-1)m\ge(n-1)\varepsilon$, so it belongs to the class. Direct inversion yields
\[
\norm{J_\varepsilon^{-1}}_\infty=
\frac{D+(2n-3)\varepsilon}{(D-\varepsilon)(D+(n-1)\varepsilon)}\longrightarrow\frac1D.
\]
This proves both the value and nonattainment.
\end{proof}
The gap between the proposed value and the actual infimum is
\[
\norm{S^{-1}}_\infty-\frac1{\alpha+m}
=\frac{(n-1)m(\alpha+2m)}{\alpha(\alpha+m)(\alpha+nm)}>0.
\]
\section{Scope, checks, and sources}
The exact verifier checks both inverses and the class assumptions, and tests the parameterized inverse formula at rational parameters. The universal lower bound and limiting claim are analytic arguments. A stronger ordering involving dominance margins is a different question; this example is not claimed to satisfy it.
