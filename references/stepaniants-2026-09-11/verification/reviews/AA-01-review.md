## Audit of the substantive steps

1. **Signed-order geometry (Lemma 3.1).** Within an open signed-order chamber, every signed raw input is a signed nested tail sum of positive gaps. A sum or difference of two such inputs is identically zero or has a fixed sign and nonnegative gap coefficients after an overall sign change. Coordinatewise relative gap perturbations therefore preserve all raw/raw comparisons and bound nonzero additive results relatively by epsilon. The stated product bound follows by expanding two relative perturbations. Ties and zero coordinates are not asserted to lie in an open chamber; they are handled later by closure.

2. **Avoidance lemma (Lemma 4.1).** For each nonzero target raw value, a forbidden rounding interval has length less than `3 tau` when it intersects the allowed interval. There are at most `2n` targets. Thus their union has length less than `6n tau = 3u/16`, strictly smaller than the available interval's length `u/2`. The case of exact pre-rounding value zero is separately valid. The strict separation claimed by the lemma follows, including repeated or opposite-signed targets.

3. **Deterministic coupling (Lemma 4.2).** The raw/rounded provenance dichotomy is exhaustive in the canonical model. Copying, storage and exact negation do not introduce another numerical source. Maintaining equal rounded outputs in both executions makes every rounded/rounded arithmetic operation exact-match automatically. Raw/raw operations are controlled by chamber geometry. A mixed multiplication is either zero in both runs or relatively perturbed by at most epsilon. In a mixed addition/subtraction, the invariant separating every signed rounded value from every signed raw input gives `|a| > tau |r|`, hence relative center change less than `epsilon/tau = u/16`. All nonzero cases therefore have `a' = a(1+theta)` with `|theta| <= u/16`; zero cases remain zero in both runs.

   Choosing the first error in `[-u/4,u/4]` by the avoidance lemma and the second as `(1+delta)/(1+theta)-1` matches the numerical results. Its magnitude is at most `5u/(16-u) < u`. The same invariant is available at the next node. For comparisons, rounded/rounded values match; raw/raw ordering is preserved; and a mixed comparison cannot change sign because the raw perturbation is at most `epsilon |r| < tau |r|`. Equality cases are correctly covered: equal rounded values remain equal, raw equalities are fixed by the chamber, and mixed equality is excluded in these selected executions. Therefore the induction preserves actual control flow, including branches controlled by rounded intermediates.

   This proof assigns no new error when a stored value is reused. Selecting errors successively is legal because accuracy is required for every arbitrary independent error vector; the completed finite path extends to a full vector by arbitrary unused-node errors. The output is either matched rounded data or the same signed raw selection, giving the stated output inequality. The proof does not need a zero-error leaf to remain reachable or to represent the polynomial globally.

4. **Local bound and necessity (Proposition 5.1 and Theorem 2.1).** Accuracy at eta `1/4` applies to both coupled executions and yields `|p(Ty')| <= (5/3)(1+epsilon)|p(Ty)| <= 2|p(Ty)|`. This argument never divides by the target polynomial, so it also holds at its zeros. The chosen epsilon is uniform over inputs and chambers. Tensor-product Lagrange interpolation on a fixed relative box is valid for all polynomials of total degree at most d, because each individual variable's degree is at most d. It gives a finite coefficient-norm constant independent of y. Applying it to `q_T(t_1 y_1,...,t_n y_n)` yields precisely the coefficientwise absolute polynomial `W_T(y)` for positive gaps. Continuity extends the bound to every nonnegative gap vector. Thus the necessity direction includes both lower-dimensional boundaries and unbounded input scales.

5. **Termwise construction and sufficiency (Lemma 6.1 and Theorem 2.1).** Exact comparisons with negated inputs determine signs without numerical constants; exact comparisons of their absolute values determine an order. Each rounded gap is its true nonnegative value times its own relative error factor; zero gaps stay exactly zero. Each nonzero monomial starts with an input factor, and integer coefficients can be implemented by finitely many repeated additions and exact negation. No constant 0 or 1 input is needed. Expanding only these additions produces signed monomial occurrences with all shared error factors and their multiplicities retained. The total occurrence weight is exactly `W_T(y)`. A finite maximum exponent sum L bounds each error product between `(1-u)^L` and `(1+u)^L`, giving the asserted absolute error estimate. Coefficient domination then converts it to uniform relative error.

   There are finitely many chambers, so taking finite maxima of their constants and error exponents gives one precision choice for each requested eta. The evaluator itself does not depend on eta, u, or any nonconstructively chosen domination constant. It is exact at zero error. At target zeros the domination bound forces every contributing monomial to vanish, and the error estimate ensures exact output. The identically zero polynomial is evaluated by a single rounded subtraction of an input from itself. These facts cover input zeros, tied magnitudes, the origin, and cancellation zeros without exceptions.

6. **Halting decision algorithm (Corollary 7.1).** All chamber substitutions and coefficient collections are effective integer operations, with finitely many chambers. On nonnegative gaps, `W_T >= 0`; consequently the quantified squared inequality with `C > 0` is exactly equivalent to the unsquared domination condition, including polynomial zeros. It is a first-order real-arithmetic sentence with integer coefficients. Effective real quantifier elimination decides each sentence and terminates. A positive answer gives the finite evaluator directly, without a search over arbitrary programs or any bound on the size of an unknown evaluator.
