## AA-01

I read the complete `solution.tex`, not just its summary or submitted PASS
report. The coupling lemma handles the actual constant-free model, including
independent errors, comparisons on rounded quantities, stored-value reuse,
and output paths with no rounded operations.

Every numerical register has raw-input or rounded provenance. Signed-order
gap perturbations control sums/differences of two raw inputs even near a
small gap. Each fresh rounded value can be chosen outside all signed-raw
relative neighborhoods: the total forbidden length is less than the allowed
error interval. This invariant makes mixed raw/rounded subtraction and
comparisons stable under the selected perturbation. Two rounded operands
already match exactly. The matching-error formula stays strictly inside the
permitted error box; errors on unused nodes can be assigned afterward.
The dependence of the chosen errors on the execution is legitimate because
accuracy is required for every error assignment.

Using accuracy at eta=1/4 gives the uniform local bound without dividing by
the target polynomial, so zeros are included. Tensor-product interpolation
on a fixed box bounds the coefficient norm of q(t*y) by its supremum and
therefore yields the required W<=C|q|. Continuity reaches zero gaps and tied
magnitudes. This necessity step does not assume a zero-error-reachable leaf.

For sufficiency, exact signs can be determined by comparisons with negated
raw inputs; no constant input is introduced. Gaps are single rounded
subtractions, and termwise monomial evaluation retains all multiplicities
of shared errors. Integer coefficients are finite repeated sums; the zero
polynomial is one self-subtraction. The finite set of branches provides
uniform accuracy for one fixed tree. Squaring the nonnegative domination
inequality gives a first-order real sentence with integer coefficients.
[Effective real quantifier elimination](https://arxiv.org/abs/2209.10978v2)
is a valid terminating external input. This result claims neither practical
complexity nor a theorem for extra exact primitives or nonzero constants.
