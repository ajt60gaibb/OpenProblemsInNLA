# Sources and provenance

Prepared 12 September 2026. The source material was read through ordinary website access. No external repository was modified. The mathematical argument in this archive is developed here; the three references below provide the problem statement and the relevant prior context.

## 1. Canonical IE-12 entry

**Title:** Near-quadratic solution cost at a prescribed backward error.

Website:
https://github.com/ajt60gaibb/OpenProblemsInNLA/blob/main/linear-systems-and-elimination/IE-12/README.md

Plain-text form:
https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ajt60gaibb/OpenProblemsInNLA/main/linear-systems-and-elimination/IE-12/README.md

The fetched entry was marked “Partially resolved,” with its last-checked date of 2026-09-10. Its exact-real model permits scalar arithmetic, square roots, comparisons, and independent standard Gaussian or random-bit draws at unit cost. The matrix norm is a promise and all entries are accessible. It asks for an absolute-polynomial dependence on `1/epsilon` with a strictly quadratic dimension factor and 0.99 success in the A-only backward-error metric.

The PDF restates this mathematical target rather than relying on an informal interpretation of “near quadratic.” The status quoted here is what the website stated when read; it is not a claim that the repository has recognized or adopted this archive's argument.

## 2. Cited general-system and PSD bounds

Michał Dereziński, Yuji Nakatsukasa, and Elizaveta Rebrova.
**Towards Universal Convergence of Backward Error in Linear System Solvers.**
arXiv:2604.16075v2, 22 May 2026.

https://arxiv.org/html/2604.16075v2

Relevant portions: definition of backward error and model discussion in Section 3.1; Corollary 17 for the PSD cost bound; Corollary 25 for the general-system cost bound; the hard-instance discussion and Section 7 for the remaining dimension dependence.

The version used is explicitly v2, not the earlier version that lacked the revised general-system theorem. The new proof in this archive does not invoke that paper's convergence theorems as black boxes; it uses its own elementary augmented-kernel filter.

## 3. Repeated products with a finite alphabet

Edo Liberty and Steven W. Zucker.
**The Mailman algorithm: A note on matrix–vector multiplication.**
Information Processing Letters 109(3), 179–182 (2009).
DOI: 10.1016/j.ipl.2008.09.028.

Author manuscript:
https://www.cs.yale.edu/homes/el327/papers/mailmanAlgorithm.pdf

The author manuscript was read, including the construction and diagrams/equations on its first three pages. It gives logarithmic savings for repeated real-vector products with a matrix from a constant-size alphabet after preprocessing.

The present proof is related in using a catalogue of shared subcomputations, but its weighted, variable-length patterns and separately charged heavy coefficients are proved directly. The growing alphabet of the rounded matrix is not treated as constant.
