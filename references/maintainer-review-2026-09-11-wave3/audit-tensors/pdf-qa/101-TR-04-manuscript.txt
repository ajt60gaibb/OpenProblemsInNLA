metic/SVDmodelreturnsanapproximationwithsquarederrorstrictlysmallerthan(d−1)

5 Why this is not a uniform constant improvement
This distinction is material to the scope of the result, not a numerical precision issue. Consider
d = 3, format 3×3×2, ranks (2,1), and the tensor
A = αe ⊗e ⊗f +βe ⊗e ⊗f +γe ⊗e ⊗f , α > γ ≥ β > 0.
1 1 1 2 2 1 3 3 2
Its optimal error is E∗ = γ2. Indeed, a tensor feasible for ranks (2,1) is M⊗v with rankM ≤ 2.
For a unit vector v = (c,s), the contracted matrix is diag(αc,βc,γs). The maximum possible
sum of its two largest squared singular values, also maximizing over c2+s2 = 1, is
max{α2+β2,α2,β2,γ2} = α2+β2.
This proves the claimed optimum by orthogonal projection.
When γ > β, the best first-cut rank-two subspace is uniquely span(e ,e ). The remaining
1 3
rank-one truncation retains only the α term. The algorithm’s squared error is therefore β2+γ2,
and its ratio to optimum is
1+(β/γ)2 < 2.
Keeping α = 2, β = 1 and sending γ ↓ 1 shows that these ratios approach 2 = d−1 in a fixed
format. At γ = β, an unfortunate ordinary TT-SVD tie choice can attain ratio exactly two; the
finite tie handling removes that equality. No theorem here asserts a uniform ratio bounded by
2−δ for a fixed δ > 0.
6 Reproducibility and scope
The accompanying verify_tr04.py checks the projector averaging identity exactly, checks the
fixed-format family and rotated tie bases, and tests the numerical implementation for rank fea-
sibility and exact reconstruction up to floating-point error. The exact mathematical algorithm
and its guarantee are Theorem 3; a tolerance-based SVD is not claimed to detect exact ties in
finite precision. No ranks are enlarged. No oracle for E∗ or for an optimal Y is used: those
objects appear only in the proof. The assertion addressed is the strict pointwise target, not a
stronger uniform-factor or bit-complexity formulation.
