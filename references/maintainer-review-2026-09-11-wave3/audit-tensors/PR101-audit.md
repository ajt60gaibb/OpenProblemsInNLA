## TR-04: prescribed-rank tensor-train approximation

The canonical target is a strict inequality on each positive-optimum input in the exact arithmetic/SVD model. It does not require a common smaller constant below `d-1`. That distinction predates this PR and is accurately stated by both the manuscript and new resolution notice. The algorithm is valid for every dense real input, every requested format, and all positive rank bounds; it never asks an oracle for the optimum or enlarges ranks.

The completion estimate is correct. An orthogonal first-mode projection of rank at most `r1` gives a discarded error orthogonal to the lifted completion error. Compressing any feasible comparison tensor cannot increase any remaining unfolding rank. Applying [Oseledets, Theorem 2.2 and Corollary 2.4](https://www.researchgate.net/profile/Ivan-Oseledets/publication/220412263_Tensor-Train_Decomposition/links/5bbfb5c5299bf1004c5a56e3/Tensor-Train-Decomposition.pdf) to the remaining `d-2` cuts gives

`error^2 <= epsilon^2 + (d-2)||P(A-Y)||_F^2`.

Here `epsilon^2` is the best first-cut tail and is at most `E_*`. If it is strictly smaller, every tested completion gives the requested strict inequality. The equality case is handled rather than discarded as nongeneric. A best matrix rank-`r1` approximation at a positive boundary singular value has column space `H + E0`, where `H` contains all strictly larger singular directions and `E0` selects the remaining dimensions from the tied eigenspace. Thus its residual is orthogonal to `H`, and to the whole selected space when no tie straddles the boundary.

For a straddling tie of dimension `t` with `s<t` selected directions, the cyclic-window projectors have exact average `(s/t)P_E`. Their average captured squared residual is at most `(s/t)E_* < E_*`. Some tested completion therefore has error at most `[1+(d-2)s/t]E_* < (d-1)E_*`. Taking the candidate with smallest measured Frobenius error preserves that bound. The zero-optimum case has zero first-cut tail and zero residual and is reconstructed exactly. Zero tensors and rank bounds exceeding the actual first rank are explicitly covered.

There are at most `n1` completions, each polynomial in the dense input size and rank parameters. Exact tests for equality of singular values are permissible in the declared idealized SVD model; the diagnostic implementation's tolerance is not promoted to a certified finite-precision algorithm. The fixed `3x3x2` example also checks out: the optimum is `gamma^2`, the unique-first-cut algorithm error is `beta^2+gamma^2` when `gamma>beta`, and ratios approach two as `gamma` approaches `beta`. Hence the manuscript appropriately makes no uniform-factor improvement claim and does not settle the stronger general-tree question beyond this canonical tensor-train target.

## TR-13: all five generic Hankel ranks

The result matches the generic complex target in [Nie–Ye, Question 6.1 and Conjecture 6.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1706.03631). The manuscript proves the slightly broader odd-order range `m>=3`; the canonical remaining range `m>=5` is fully covered. Nothing is inferred for every exceptional Hankel tensor or for TR-14.

I checked the Koszul block matrix directly. For a rank-one three-factor tensor it is a Kronecker product with a `3x3` matrix of rank at most two. Polynomial minor equations therefore give the bound for ordinary border rank in the full tensor space, including unstructured approximating sequences. The stated invertible-first-slice formula follows from solving the first two kernel equations and substituting into the third; the noncommuting factor order is correct.

Grouping the original `2k+1` modes and selecting one multi-index for each possible index sum are fixed linear operations taking rank-one tensors to rank-one tensors or zero. They cannot increase ordinary border rank. With `ell=n-1`, `a=k ell+1`, `s=floor(ell/2)`, the selected middle indices `0,s,2s` are valid for `n>=3`. The two spikes at `a-1` and `2a+s-1` both lie in the generating-vector range because `s+1<=ell`. Also `a>=2s+1`, ensuring the displayed row and column blocks are distinct.

The slices satisfy `M0=J`, `JM1=L^s`, `JM2=L^(2s)+E`. Their commutator has exactly the specified `diag(I_s,-I_s)` minor and rank `2s`. The full Koszul rank is consequently `2a+2s=2r`, where `r=ceil((m(n-1)+1)/2)`. This one exact witness proves a nonzero polynomial minor and hence an ordinary-border-rank lower bound on a nonempty Zariski-open set. The separate binary case has an ordinary flattening containing an invertible reversal matrix and gives the same lower bound.

For the upper bound, the moment-map Jacobian has full row rank at distinct nodes and nonzero weights: an annihilating degree-`D` polynomial would have `r` distinct double roots, impossible unless zero because `2r>D`. The map is dominant, its constructible image contains a nonempty Zariski-open set, and every point of that image has an actual Vandermonde decomposition with at most `r` terms. Intersecting the two nonempty opens in the irreducible Hankel parameter space and using the standard rank inequalities forces all five ranks to equal `r`. The argument establishes actual structured rank, not merely structured border rank.

## TR-20: reduced nonisotropic discriminant degrees

The exact Segre embedding and bilinear denominator match [Borovik–Friedman–Hoşten–Pfeffer, Conjecture 3.18](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.06939v2#S3). I read every section, including the additional general coefficient formula, rather than treating successful arithmetic tests as evidence for the global geometry.

The parameter reduction is surjective because products of Segre coordinates span every bidegree-`(2,2)` monomial. Pullback simply adjoins kernel variables to the resulting polynomial, preserving its reducedness, irreducibility and degree, with the projective kernel included as the cone vertex.

On `X=P^(m-1) x P^(n-1)`, the two smooth quadric divisors meet transversely; disconnected zero-dimensional quadrics when a factor is `P^1` cause no problem. The section `dP-P dlog(Q)` transforms correctly as a section of `E=Omega^1_X(log D) tensor O(2,2)`. Bidegree `(2,2)` sections prescribe every two-jet. Evaluation has rank `k` off the crossing and `k-1` on it. The two boundary strata have incidence dimension `M-1`, too small to support components of the zero scheme of `k` equations in dimension `M+k`. Thus the universal incidence is the irreducible local complete intersection closing the interior incidence. Cohen–Macaulayness and generic reducedness make it reduced. The missing differential at a crossing is `p_u du-p_v dv`; its failure requires two further independent jet conditions, giving singular-locus dimension at most `M-3`.

A prescribed nondegenerate critical two-jet proves dominance and generic finiteness over parameter space. The positive-dimensional-fiber image has codimension at least two, since its inverse image is a proper subset of the irreducible incidence with fiber dimension at least one. A general parameter line avoids this and the singular image; characteristic-zero Bertini then supplies the smooth finite covering curve used for adjunction and Riemann–Hurwitz. Connectedness is unnecessary because both formulas add over components.

The total ramification formula is correct: the curve class is `c_k(E)+h c_(k-1)(E)`, adjunction gives `K_C=(K_X+c1(E)+(k-2)h)|C`, and adding twice the covering degree yields the stated total. At a smooth point of one boundary divisor, eliminating tangential equations gives a remaining equation `-B s + beta u^2/2 + higher terms=0` with both coefficients nonzero generically: exactly simple ramification. At a crossing, the normal `2x2` Jacobian block has determinant `-p_u p_v`, and the tangential Hessian is generically invertible, including the empty block for `k=2`. Crossings are therefore unramified. First-jet zeros on each boundary component count its simple ramification points; those lying at crossings or violating generic jet conditions have parameter image of codimension at least two and are absent from a general pencil.

The degree-one and reducedness argument resolves the main possible obstruction to using that count as a hypersurface degree. Interior Hessians are arbitrary symmetric matrices after criticality is imposed, so their singular locus is an irreducible divisor with general corank one and a mixed kernel. Mixed cubic sections vary the third derivative along that kernel while leaving its two-jet fixed, producing a general simple fold. The fold-image normal is `N_(xi,w)(Pdot)=d(Pdot/Q)_xi(w)`. Its flattening has rank two and row/column spaces spanned by a normalized rank-one symmetric matrix and its nonzero projective tangent. Each such span contains exactly one rank-one line: a combination with nonzero tangent coefficient has a `2x2` minor of determinant `-beta^2`. Thus the normal uniquely determines both projective factors of `xi`. Distinct ordinary folds cannot lie above a general smooth point of the same image hypersurface because they would have the same normal. Exceptional folds and boundary limits have smaller dimension. The generic map is therefore one-to-one with ramification multiplicity one. Boundary image normals have flattening rank one, also separating them from the nonisotropic hypersurface.

The logarithmic residue, conormal and first-jet sequences yield the manuscript's Chern classes and boundary subtraction. I independently simplified the coefficient extractions symbolically with arbitrary `n`; they give `4n(n^2-1)` for `m=2` and `12n^3(n-1)` for `m=3`, including `n=2,3`. These equal the two conjectured binomial formulas. The diagnostic critical-point count also matches [Salizzoni–Sodomaco–Weigert, Corollary 5.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.17760v1#S5), with their projective dimensions replaced by `m-1,n-1`. The source count is only a consistency check; the boundary and generic-degree arguments above establish the reduced degree.
