## RA-03: finite counterexample

Lines 160–188 already settle the literal RA-03 question without a limit or an eigensolver. For A = [[2,1],[1,2]], the singular values are 3 and 1. Each diagonal pivot has probability 2/5 and squared residual error 9/4. Each off-diagonal pivot has probability 1/10 and squared residual error 9. Their sum is 18/5, whereas twice the optimal rank-one squared error is 2.

I independently executed all four rational rank-one updates and recovered this value. For a general nonsingular dense 2-by-2 input, each weighted contribution is |det A|²/||A||F², so the manuscript's four-times-determinant formula is also correct. Zero-entry cases are explicitly outside this particular identity, and remain covered by the algorithm's zero-probability convention.

## General lower-bound proof

The family (lines 99–115) is A = L diag(1,epsilon,...,epsilon^r) Lᵀ, with n=r+1, epsilon=t^(n²), unit lower-triangular L, and Lij=t^j/(i+j) below the diagonal. It is positive definite because L is invertible and the diagonal scales are positive. Its first-column contribution makes every off-diagonal entry positive. Rational t gives rational entries.

**Prefix minors, lines 191–237.** For s selected rows S, scale only the missing prefix columns j by t^(−j). After grouping common and missing indices, the limiting matrix is block triangular with identity and a Cauchy block 1/(i+j). The latter has distinct positive index sets and nonzero Cauchy determinant. This yields the stated nonzero leading coefficient and exponent sum of missing j's. The inverse estimate O(t^(−s)) is valid: the scaled inverse is bounded, and undoing a diagonal column scaling costs only its largest inverse scale, not the product. Empty blocks and s=0 are covered. I additionally verified 124 prefix minors symbolically through dimension six, including their predicted leading powers.

**Scale separation, lines 239–269.** Cauchy–Binet has a unique lowest epsilon exponent, attained by the first s columns. All remaining minors are bounded. The leading product's t-exponent is at most s(s+1), strictly less than n² for s≤n−1, so its relative O(epsilon) remainder vanishes. For the full determinant there is one term. This proves simultaneous eventual nonvanishing of all square minors at each fixed dimension, including the nonprincipal minors needed for LU. No unjustified uniform-in-r estimate is used.

**Residual normalizers, lines 273–364.** The interpolation remainder w is L times a vector with coordinate s+1 equal to one, giving h=||w||²≥(1+||c||²)/4 eventually. If s+1 is unselected, the sampled next-column entries are O(t^(s+1)), so the inverse estimate gives c=O(t) and h→1. If s+1 is selected, its interpolation equation has an O(t) row and right side one, forcing ||c||≥constant/t and h→infinity. Consequently 1/h tends to the required binary indicator and stays bounded.

The bordered-minor formula then gives each uneliminated residual entry epsilon^s wI,i wJ,j times 1+o(1). The relevant leading components are nonzero by the prefix-minor lemma. There are only finitely many entries and sets for fixed r, so the maximum relative error tends to zero. Summing nonnegative diagonal terms or squared entries preserves this relative statement even for diverging w. This establishes both normalizers and their inverse-indicator limits without cancellation of a norm sum.

**History cancellation, lines 366–401.** A valid Cholesky history has probability equal to the selected principal determinant divided by the product of trace normalizers. The sole remaining diagonal entry after r pivots is det A divided by that same determinant. LU instead uses squared moduli of selected determinants and squared-norm normalizers. The corresponding factors cancel exactly when probability is multiplied by terminal error. LU row/column independence is never assumed. Every history in the small-t proof is valid because all its required minors are nonzero. I checked these identities independently on an unrelated rational positive-definite 3-by-3 matrix by comparing direct pivot recursion with the determinant-history sums.

**Optimal scale and count, lines 403–466.** Since L→I, epsilon^r A^(-1)→enenᵀ in norm. Thus lambda_min(A)/epsilon^r→1, and at order r+1 the trace and squared-Frobenius optimal tails are precisely lambda_min and lambda_min². Normalizing the exact history identities cancels all epsilon powers. Each history summand tends to one or zero, with bounded individual inverse factors.

The surviving condition is that prefix S_s does not contain label s+1 for 0≤s≤r−1. Equivalently, at position u the next label lies in {1,...,u,n} minus earlier labels. There are exactly two possibilities at each position; label n=r+1 has no later normalizer constraint. This gives 2^r Cholesky histories and 4^r ordered pairs of LU histories. These are finite sums at fixed r, so the limiting passage is valid. Direct enumeration independently confirms the counts for r=1,...,7.

It follows that the ratios tend to 2^r and 4^r. The existing universal upper bounds prove these are suprema, not necessarily attained maxima. Given any fixed polynomial factor, one first chooses r so that the exponential exceeds it, then chooses sufficiently small t. Lines 144–157 implement exactly that quantifier order. This rigorously refutes RA-02 and also excludes a polynomial multiple of 2^r for RPLU.

## Correlation-matrix extension and certificate details

The row-replication proof (lines 468–534) is sound. For rational A and suitably chosen rational c, the integers mi=Aii/c define an isometry P with mi copies of eiᵀ/sqrt(mi). The matrix C=PAPᵀ/c has diagonal one, positive entries and the nonzero eigenvalues of A/c. Aggregate group probabilities match both original pivot rules; substituting the rank-one updates preserves the residual form. Trace and squared-Frobenius errors, and their optimal denominators, scale together. An independent exact example with c=2 confirms both full pivot processes for one and two steps.

C is initially only semidefinite. Adding delta I and dividing by 1+delta produces a positive-definite correlation matrix. For every positive-probability history of C, all denominators stay nonzero nearby and its weighted terminal contribution converges. Finitely many such histories suffice; the remaining contributions are nonnegative. Therefore the liminf inequality is justified even though new LU histories can appear near zero pivot entries. The positive optimal denominator is continuous. This proves the supremum claim when dimension is unrestricted, without assuming continuity of the entire LU expectation.

For the finite certificates (lines 536–606), epsilon^r A^(-1) dominates vvᵀ for v=L^(−T)en, yielding the stated upper bound on lambda_min. Maxima over the permitted prefix sets bound every retained-history normalizer. The final selected blocks are also checked to be invertible; retaining only these histories gives a legitimate lower bound. The enumeration enclosure additionally uses the trace upper bound for the largest eigenvalue of epsilon^r A^(-1). These inequalities point in the correct direction. Exact rational arithmetic is essential for these highly separated scales.
