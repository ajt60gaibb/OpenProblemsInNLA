## Proof and primary inputs

The density identity `x f = k*f` follows by differentiating its Laplace transform. Differentiating the smooth convolution kernel gives the second identity without requiring a bounded derivative of the density at zero. At a positive global mode, the two identities give `M-z = integral k v` and `1 = integral (-k') v`, with `0 <= v <= 1`. Comparing the kernels pointwise proves the interval between mean minus maximum scale and mean minus minimum scale. Total shape greater than one supplies a positive interior mode and zero density at zero; all integrals are finite.

The essential transfer decreases a minimum positive fractional coefficient and increases another fractional coefficient until one reaches zero or one. The decreased coefficient remains the minimum positive scale of the augmented convolution. Its mean and extreme scales therefore put its mode between `rho-1` and `rho+1`. I directly checked that [Roosta-Khorasani and Székely, Appendix A, Theorem 4 and Lemma 5](https://arxiv.org/html/1601.04731v1) allow heterogeneous positive Gamma shapes and rates. Thus unimodality applies even after subdivision into shapes smaller than one.

Differentiating the two varying Laplace factors gives the CDF derivative `alpha(a-b) g'(x)` with the stated sign. Since the augmented density vanishes at zero, inversion produces no boundary term. Parameter differentiation is legitimate on compact intervals before a scale vanishes. The upper and lower tail directions are both correct. Weak continuity supplies the terminal zero-scale case; each step removes a fractional coefficient, so the path terminates after finitely many steps. No assertion about arbitrary majorization paths is used.

Repeating each weight N times while dividing the Gamma shape by N preserves the original law, cap and mean. The extreme sum has a Gamma component whose shape tends to rho and an independent nonnegative remainder with mean tending to zero. Weak convergence and continuity of the limiting Gamma CDF prove both final tail comparisons. This does not require uniform convergence of densities or a positive lower bound on the subdivided shapes.

Gaussian diagonalization gives exactly `rho=m*mu/2`; hence the required deviation condition is `rho*epsilon >= 1`. The resulting probability chain agrees with [Hallman, Theorem 6 and Conjecture 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.15454v1#S5). It covers equality at the threshold, m=1, fractional effective rank, zero eigenvalues and padded zeros. A nonpositive lower endpoint has zero lower tail. The proof establishes validity of the threshold, not its optimality. The separately credited auxiliary counterexamples to other Hallman conjectures remain valid and are not used as premises.
