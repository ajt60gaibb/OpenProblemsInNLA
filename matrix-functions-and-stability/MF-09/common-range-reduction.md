# A finite joint spectral radius reduction for generators with a common range constraint

Let $`\mathcal M=\mathcal B\cup\mathcal R`$ be a finite rational matrix family.
Use $`\mathcal B=\{0\}`$ if the first subfamily is empty. Every matrix
$`R\in\mathcal R`$ maps into a common rational subspace $`U`$ of dimension
$`r`$. The span of their images is one choice for $`U`$. Discard the case
$`r=0`$ or $`\mathcal R=\varnothing`$, when $`\mathcal R`$ adds no growth.
Write $`\sigma=\widehat\rho(\mathcal B)`$. For $`t>\sigma`$, form the set of
linear maps on $`U`$

```math
\mathcal C_t=\{0\}\cup
\left\{t^{-(|w|+1)}(RB_w)|_U:R\in\mathcal R,\ w\in\mathcal B^*\right\},
```

where $`B_w`$ denotes a word product, including the identity. Inserting
$`R`$ returns the state to $`U`$, so products of these maps represent
successive visits to $`U`$. We prove the following statements.

1. For $`t>\sigma`$, one has $`\widehat\rho(\mathcal M)<t`$ if and only if
   $`\widehat\rho(\mathcal C_t)<1`$. At any positive rational $`t>\sigma`$,
   an algorithm computes a finite rational subset of $`\mathcal C_t`$
   with the same joint spectral radius.
2. Suppose $`\sigma`$ is algebraic and all the unscaled maps
   $`(RB_w)|_U`$ are simultaneously triangularizable over $`\mathbb C`$
   or simultaneously similar over $`\mathbb C`$ to normal matrices.
   Then $`\widehat\rho(\mathcal M)`$ is algebraic. If it is above
   $`\sigma`$, some finite word in $`\mathcal M`$ attains it. Both conditions
   allow noncommuting $`\mathcal M`$ and generators of rank greater than one.

## Comparing the two strict thresholds

By submultiplicativity, products in $`\mathcal B/t`$ have a bound
$`K\beta^{|w|}`$ with $`K\ge1`$ and $`\beta<1`$. In particular $`\mathcal C_t`$
is compact. Fix a norm given by rational coordinates on $`U`$. A product
of $`k`$ nonzero factors from $`\mathcal C_t`$ is the restriction of a
scaled word from $`\mathcal M`$ of length at least $`k`$. Thus if
$`\widehat\rho(\mathcal M)<t`$, the same geometric norm bound for the full
family implies $`\widehat\rho(\mathcal C_t)<1`$.

Conversely, if $`\widehat\rho(\mathcal C_t)<1`$, then for some fixed
$`k`$ the supremum of the norms of products of length $`k`$ in
$`\mathcal C_t`$ is below one. The family $`\mathcal C_s`$ is continuous in
Hausdorff distance for $`s>\sigma`$. On a small closed interval about $`t`$,
the maps corresponding to long words tend uniformly to zero by the
geometric bound at the left endpoint. The remaining maps form a finite
list continuous in $`s`$. The norm supremum for products of fixed length
is therefore continuous. Choose $`\sigma<s<t`$ sufficiently close to
$`t`$ that $`\mathcal C_s`$ still has joint spectral radius less than one.

Any word in $`\mathcal M/s`$ either uses only the first subfamily or
decomposes into an initial first-subfamily word, its first $`R/s`$, a
product of maps from $`\mathcal C_s`$, and a terminal first-subfamily
word. The first and last words are uniformly bounded, the first
$`R/s`$ is uniformly bounded and maps into $`U`$, and the middle product
is uniformly bounded. Fixed coordinate maps between $`U`$ and the ambient
space change this bound by a constant. All full scaled words are thus
uniformly bounded, giving $`\widehat\rho(\mathcal M)\le s<t`$.

## An exact finite cut

Choose a vector-space basis of the real span of $`\mathcal C_t`$ from
among its actual members, ignoring zero. This is a finite-dimensional
space of matrices. For some constant $`K_1`$, the sum of absolute values
of the coordinates of any member in this basis is at most $`K_1`$ times
its norm. Since the maps tend uniformly to zero with word length, all
maps past some length are in the absolutely convex hull of this basis.
Hence the absolutely convex hull of $`\mathcal C_t`$ is generated by a
finite subset $`F_t\subseteq\mathcal C_t`$, including the basis and all
short-word maps. In the zero-span case take $`F_t=\{0\}`$. Passing to an
absolutely convex hull preserves joint spectral radius. Expand any product
multilinearly and bound the sum of absolute values of its coefficients by
one to see this at each fixed product length.

For positive rational $`t>\sigma`$, find this cut by rational arithmetic.
Compute a basis of the span of products $`B_w`$ by starting with the
identity and closing under multiplication by each generator. Retain a
new word only when it enlarges the span, and stop once each generator
maps the span into itself. Applying $`B\mapsto(RB)|_U`$ for each $`R`$,
rescaling the corresponding word, and retaining an independent subset
gives a word basis for $`\mathcal C_t`$. Coordinates in this basis are
obtained by inverting a rational nonsingular minor, which gives a
rational upper bound for $`K_1`$. In the rational induced infinity norm,
enumerate lengths until the maximum norm of a block of length $`\ell`$
from $`\mathcal B/t`$ is $`q<1`$. This terminates by submultiplicativity.
Splitting a word into blocks of length $`\ell`$ and a short remainder
gives an explicit bound $`K_2 q^{\lfloor |w|/\ell\rfloor}`$ for its
restricted maps, with rational $`K_2`$ obtained using finitely many
norms and the coordinate maps. When $`q=0`$, all words of length at least
$`\ell`$ vanish. Otherwise choose a cutoff for which the bound times
$`K_1`$ is at most one. Enumerating shorter words now computes $`F_t`$.

### Strict-stability consequence

Given a decider for strict stability on the class containing
$`\mathcal B`$, first test $`\sigma<1`$. If it fails then the full family
is unstable at the strict threshold. Otherwise compute $`F_1`$ and decide
its strict stability in dimension $`r`$. For instance, require that
$`\mathcal B`$ is simultaneously triangularizable and that the compressed
maps $`(RB_w)|_U`$ are either simultaneously triangularizable or are
simultaneously similar to normal matrices. In either compressed class
the joint spectral radius is the maximum of the
individual radii. For the triangular class, diagonal entries determine
this value and off-diagonal entries add only polynomial growth. In the
common normal basis the spectral norm of each generator equals its
radius. The first-subfamily assumption can likewise be replaced by any
class with a strict-stability decider. Consequently the output is exact
even at the threshold $`1`$.

In this example the split of an input list need not be supplied. Try all
partitions. Simultaneous triangularizability of the first subfamily is
decidable by real quantifier elimination for the complex entries of an
invertible change of basis. For a triangularizable first subfamily with
radius at least one, return no immediately. Otherwise compute $`F_1`$ and
check either simultaneous condition on that finite set by real quantifier
elimination. At any passing partition return the exact strict comparison.
Every step terminates, and a family in the example class has at least one
passing partition or an unstable first subfamily. If no partition passes,
the algorithm may return an arbitrary answer outside the promise.

### Algebraicity consequence

If $`t_*:=\widehat\rho(\mathcal M)=\sigma`$, algebraicity follows by
assumption. If $`t_*>\sigma`$, the comparison gives
$`\widehat\rho(\mathcal C_{t_*})\ge1`$. Its finite subset $`F_{t_*}`$
consists of scaled actual maps, and the radius of every individual map
is at most one. Indeed the ambient product $`RB_w`$ has all its nonzero
eigenvalues on $`U`$, and its normalized spectral radius is at most
one by the definition of $`t_*`$. Scaling individual matrices preserves
both simultaneous conditions stated above. Thus the joint spectral
radius of $`F_{t_*}`$ is the maximum of its individual radii and some
map has radius exactly one. Its original rational product has radius
$`t_*^{|w|+1}`$, giving the finite-word formula and algebraicity.

For a rational matrix, any complex eigenvalue and its conjugate are
algebraic, hence so is its modulus. When the first subfamily is
simultaneously triangularizable its joint spectral radius is also the
maximum of its individual radii, so the algebraicity hypothesis on
$`\sigma`$ automatically holds. The triangularity assumption on the
compressed maps can be checked on their finite unscaled word basis,
using existence of one invertible complex change of basis as a real
polynomial condition. The common normal basis condition concerns all
original compressed maps. At a rational threshold, the strict test only
needs the common normal basis condition on the computed finite subset.

For a noncommuting rational example, take $`d=4`$ and
$`B=\tfrac12 I_2\otimes\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)`$.
Take $`R_i=S_i\otimes e_1e_2^T`$, where
$`S_1=\operatorname{diag}(2,1)`$ and
$`S_2=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)`$.
The common range is $`\mathbb R^2\otimes\operatorname{span}(e_1)`$ and
both $`R_i`$ have rank two. The unscaled maps from $`R_i B^k`$ are zero
for even $`k`$ and $`2^{-k}S_i`$ for odd $`k`$. In particular they are
normal in the same basis. At $`t=1`$, the largest map radius is one,
attained by $`R_1B`$ with $`k=1`$. For every $`t>1`$ this largest radius
is less than one. The strict comparison therefore gives
$`\widehat\rho(\{B,R_1,R_2\})=1`$ exactly.

Morris gives a different, more general finiteness theorem for rank-one
generators, without requiring their images to share one line. The finite
cut here addresses common range dimensions above one, with arbitrary
words from the first subfamily between returns to that range.
[Morris, *Rank one matrices do not contribute to the failure of the finiteness property*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1109.4648).
