\subsection{Negative resolution claimed - 19 November
2026}\label{negative-resolution-claimed---19-november-2026}

Pedro Martins,
\href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24237543}{\emph{Marginal Growth
Rates of Matrix Products Need Not Be Weakly Increasing or Regularly
Varying}}, published as a preprint on \textbf{15 November 2026}, reports
a negative answer to both questions. \textbf{Theorem 1.3} (p.~2;
construction and proof in Sections 2-5, pp.~4-13) gives a pair of real
\(20\times20\) matrices
\(\mathcal M_{\theta_\star}=\{F_0(\theta_\star),F_1(\theta_\star)\}\).
Writing \(g=g_{\mathcal M_{\theta_\star}}\), the theorem asserts

\[n\le g(n)\le6n^3\qquad(n\ge1),\]

which implies \(\widehat\rho(\mathcal M_{\theta_\star})=1\). For
\(p_1=4\), \(p_{j+1}=4^{p_j}\), \(\theta_\star=\sum_{j\ge1}p_j^{-1}\)
and \(N_j=16^{p_j}\), it also asserts, for every \(j\ge2\) and every
integer \(\ell\ge2\),

\[\frac{g(N_j/2)}{g(N_j)}\ge\frac{2^{p_j}}{4\cdot10^5p_j^4},
\qquad
\frac{g(\ell N_j)}{g(N_j)}\ge
\frac{(\ell-1)2^{p_j}}{4\cdot10^5p_j^4}.\]

The factors on the right tend to infinity. The first ratio contradicts a
fixed weak-increase constant by taking \(k=N_j/2\) and the time
increment \(N_j/2\) in the original statement. The second contradicts
weak upper regular variation already for the fixed multiplier
\(\ell=2\). A pair of real matrices is compact and nonempty, and the
theorem uses the same Euclidean operator norm and maximum over all words
as this entry. Thus the reported result covers both complete universal
targets, rather than only growth along a selected switching word. The
original definitions, questions and earlier literature checks are
retained below.

\subsection{Context and notation}\label{context-and-notation}

The joint spectral radius of a nonempty compact set
\(\mathcal M\subset\mathbb C^{d\times d}\) is

\[\widehat\rho(\mathcal M)=\lim_{k\to\infty}
\max_{A_1,\ldots,A_k\in\mathcal M}\|A_k\cdots A_1\|_2^{1/k}.\]

This definition also applies to finite real matrix sets. The ordinary
spectral radius of one matrix is written \(\rho(A)\).

For a compact nonempty \(\mathcal M\subset\mathbb R^{d\times d}\) with
\(\widehat\rho(\mathcal M)=1\), define its maximal product norm at
length \(k\) by

\[g_{\mathcal M}(k)=\max_{A_1,\ldots,A_k\in\mathcal M}\|A_k\cdots A_1\|_2.\]

This problem concerns growth within one family over time.
\href{https://github.com/ajt60gaibb/OpenProblemsInNLA/blob/main/matrix-functions-and-stability/MF-07/README.md}{MF-07}
instead requests a dimension-dependent bound uniform across families.

\subsection{Problem statement}\label{problem-statement}

Does every family in this notation satisfy both

\[\exists c>0\ \forall k,\ell\ge1:\quad
g_{\mathcal M}(k+\ell)\ge c\,g_{\mathcal M}(k)\]

and

\[\forall \ell\ge1\ \exists C_\ell>0\ \forall k\ge1:\quad
g_{\mathcal M}(\ell k)\le C_\ell\,g_{\mathcal M}(k)?\]

Constants may depend on \(\mathcal M\).

\subsection{Reference and status
evidence}\label{reference-and-status-evidence}

growth rates of matrix products}, Linear Algebra Appl. 709 (2025),
132--163, \href{https://doi.org/10.1016/j.laa.2025.01.013}{DOI}, §4,
Definition 4.1 and §7, Question 1. The source names these properties
weak increase and weak upper regular variation. Searches for both
property names, the authors, and subsequent matrix-product growth papers
located no resolution.
