## Complete original target

For every finite-dimensional complex vector space `V`, every irreducible reduced nondegenerate complex projective variety `X ⊆ P(V)`, and every projective point `p`, the proposed implication is

```math
\underline R_X(p)<R_X(p)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
R_{X\times X}(p\otimes p)<R_X(p)^2.
```

Here `R` is the minimum number of points whose projective span contains the given point; border rank uses the Zariski closure of each corresponding rank-at-most locus. The product is the Segre image in `P(V ⊗_ℂ V)`, with separate factors and unrestricted complex coefficients. Nondegenerate means that the variety spans its full ambient projective space.

The proposed result is the negation of this complete universal statement, using one fully admissible example. A proof for a finite subset of points, a Euclidean closure with no Zariski bridge, symmetric decompositions only, or a coordinate identification without a tensor-product bridge would not suffice.

## Fixed exact data

Set `E = ℂ^13`, indexed by integers `0,…,12`. Let `e_j` be its coordinate basis and

```math
c(t)=(1,t,t^2,\ldots,t^{12})\quad(t\in\mathbb C),
\qquad c(\infty)=e_{12}.
```

The degree-twelve rational normal curve consists of the projective points represented by these vectors. Set

```math
S_j=1+2^j+3^j,\qquad
z=e_1-c(1)-c(2)-c(3).
```

Use `J = {0,2,3,…,12}` in this increasing order and `V = ℂ^J`, of complex dimension twelve. Define the complex-linear map

```math
(Pw)_j=5w_j-S_jw_1\qquad(j\in J).
```

Its columns indexed by `J` equal `5I`, while `z_1=-5`; the required exact facts are surjectivity and `ker P = ℂz`.

For finite parameters and infinity put

```math
g(t)=P(c(t))=(5t^j-S_jt)_{j\in J},
\qquad g(\infty)=5e_{12},
\qquad v=P(e_1)=(-S_j)_{j\in J}.
```

All displayed integers and powers are exact. In increasing `J` order,

```text
v = (-3, -14, -36, -98, -276, -794,
     -2316, -6818, -20196, -60074, -179196, -535538).
```

Define `X` to be the complete projective image of this parametrization, with its reduced algebraic structure. Equivalently it is the projective image of the homogeneous degree-twelve map

```math
[s:t]\longmapsto
\left[(5s^{12-j}t^j-S_js^{11}t)_{j\in J}\right].
```

Its base-point freedom must be proved. For example, if `s=0`, the last coordinate is `5t^12 ≠ 0`; on `s=1`, the first coordinate forces `t=5/3`, where the `j=2` coordinate is `−85/9 ≠ 0`. This addresses every parameter, including infinity, without a numerical search.

## Semantic definitions and bridges

An elementary cone presentation is acceptable if every bridge below is proved. Let

```math
C=\{0\}\cup\{a\,g(t):a\in\mathbb C^*,\ t\in\mathbb C\cup\{\infty\}\}.
```

For any cone `C` in a complex vector space, define the rank-at-most-`r` predicate by the existence of `r` representatives in `C` and `r` unrestricted complex coefficients whose linear combination is the vector. Zero terms and zero coefficients may be allowed for padding. This is equivalent to projective span by at most `r` points at every nonzero vector, and this equivalence must be explicit. The minimum is meaningful because nondegeneracy ensures finite spanning expressions; a default value from a failed `Nat.find` must never encode the result.

The affine Zariski closure must be the common zero set of **all** complex multivariate polynomials vanishing on the specified rank-at-most cone. A Euclidean limit may be used only after proving its inclusion in this closure. The cone formulation must be bridged to the projective closure of the rank-at-most locus, using homogeneous scaling invariance. Representative choice and nonzero scalar rescaling must preserve both ranks.

The Segre cone is exactly the cone generated by `x ⊗ y` for `x,y ∈ C`, with both factors independent. Its rank predicate permits arbitrary lists of such pairs and arbitrary complex coefficients. If coordinate matrices `(x_i y_j)` are used operationally, prove the standard linear isomorphism from `V ⊗_ℂ V` and show that it carries every allowed summand and the target tensor to those coordinates.

For algebraic admissibility, one suitable statement is the existence of a proper homogeneous prime ideal `I ⊆ ℂ[X_j : j∈J]` such that `zeroLocus(I)=C`, together with the projective point/cone correspondence. Primality supplies irreducibility and a reduced coordinate ring. The result must also prove that the span of `C` is all of `V`. Equivalent standard Mathlib scheme or algebraic-set formulations are acceptable if they establish the same complete conditions.

**Closed-image requirement:** defining `X` or `C` as the closure of the displayed image is not enough. Prove that the whole nonzero closed image consists of the displayed finite-parameter and infinity points, or give an equally complete characterization on which the same independence theorem is proved. Otherwise extra closure points could enter rank decompositions and invalidate the lower bound.

## Minimum sufficient final obligations

The final Challenge must expose the original-target negation and enough supporting declarations for review to trace its meaning. The following are the mathematical obligations expressed by this draft; exact declaration names are mapped below.

| Obligation | Exact required statement |
|---|---|
| Data and quotient | `P` is surjective; its kernel is precisely `ℂz`; all parametrized projective representatives are nonzero. |
| Algebraic image | `X` is projective, irreducible, reduced and nondegenerate, and its entire point set equals the specified projective parametrization. |
| Independence | Every set of at most eight distinct points of `X` has linearly independent nonzero representatives over `ℂ`. Any representative choice is allowed. |
| Three-term expression | `v ≠ 0` and `g(1)+g(2)+g(3)=v`. |
| Rank lower bound | There is no expression of `v` using at most two points of the entire `X` with arbitrary complex coefficients. Consequently `R_X([v])=3`. |
| Border upper bound | `[v]` lies in the Zariski closure of the rank-at-most-two locus. Consequently its border rank is at most two and strictly below three. |
| Nine-term expression | `v⊗v = Σ_{a=1}^3 Σ_{b=1}^3 g(a)⊗g(b)`, with the usual tensor product over `ℂ`. |
| Square lower bound | There is no expression of `v⊗v` using at most eight Segre points of `X×X`, permitting arbitrary complex coefficients, repetition and unrelated points in the two factors. Consequently its rank is nine. |
| Canonical conclusion | An admissible `V,X,p` exists with border rank below rank and square rank at least the square of rank; hence the original universal implication is false. |

The two lower bounds must not be hypotheses of the final exported canonical result. The algebraic-image conditions must not be hypotheses supplied without proofs for this particular example. The trusted Challenge may have intentional placeholders, but the Solution must import none of those placeholders and must supply every advertised result with permitted axioms only.

Exact border rank two can also be exported at little conceptual additional cost: the rank-one projective locus is precisely the closed variety `X`, and `[v] ∉ X`. This requires the same closed-image bridge. For the original negative answer, the upper bound two together with rank three is already sufficient.

## Proof-route design to be checked before implementation

These are independently examined source reductions, not substitute proofs of the future Lean boundary.

1. For any at most eleven distinct rational-normal-curve parameters, the polynomial `T ∏(T−a)`, over their finite nonzero values, annihilates their curve vectors while its coefficient of `T` is nonzero. If infinity is included, its degree is at most eleven, so it also annihilates `e₁₂`. This gives only the tangent-rank lower bound needed here. The roots-of-unity upper formula for tangent rank may be omitted.
2. An expression of `z` by at most eight curve points would express `e₁` by at most eleven, a contradiction. A dependence of at most eight distinct projected points either produces such a center expression or contradicts ordinary Vandermonde independence.
3. Combining the displayed three-term expression with any expression using at most two points produces a dependence in a union of at most five projective points. Independence forces all three original nonzero coefficients to persist.
4. Define the polynomial curve `h(t)` by `h(t)=(g(t)−g(0))/t` for nonzero `t`, extended polynomially by `h(0)=v`. Its `j=0` coordinate is constantly `−3`; for `j≥2`, its coordinate is `5t^(j−1)−S_j`. Thus it never vanishes. For every nonzero `t` it has a two-term expression. If a polynomial vanishes on the rank-at-most-two cone, composition with `h` vanishes at infinitely many complex parameters and hence at zero. This directly supplies the Zariski border bound with no interval subdivision or analytic limit.
5. In any hypothesized square decomposition with `L≤8` summands, collect distinct representatives on the left and on the right separately. They are independent by obligation 3. Contractions place `v` in their spans. In the respective independent bases, `v` has at least three nonzero coefficients. In the product basis `v⊗v` therefore has at least nine nonzero coordinates. Each summand occupies one coordinate, so `L≤8` is impossible. Repetition and cancellation only reduce the supported coordinates of the proposed short sum.
6. Prove the image is algebraically closed and irreducible using either a fully connected projective morphism/proper-image API or exact homogeneous coordinate algebra. A parameter-image declaration alone does not discharge this step. Nondegeneracy follows from the spanning of the original rational normal curve and surjectivity of `P`.
