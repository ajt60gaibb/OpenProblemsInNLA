\textbf{Rating rationale:} Challenging because a dimension comparison of
Gaussian critical-point expectations needs control beyond the known
integral formula; specialist importance concerns a particular statistic
of rank-one approximation landscapes.

\subsection{Statement}\label{statement}

For integers \(p\ge3\) and \(2\le n_1\le\cdots\le n_p\), let
\(G\in\bigotimes_{i=1}^p\mathbb R^{n_i}\) have independent standard
normal entries. Let \(X\) be the smooth manifold of nonzero real
rank-one tensors in this space, and let \(N(G)\) be the number of
critical points on \(X\) of

\[Z\longmapsto\|G-Z\|_F^2.\]

Each critical tensor is counted once, regardless of how its vector
factors are scaled. Define \(a(n_1,\ldots,n_p)=\mathbb E[N(G)]\); the
critical-point count is finite almost surely.

If

\[n_p-1>\sum_{i=1}^{p-1}(n_i-1),\]

is it always true that

\[a(n_1,\ldots,n_{p-1},n_p)
\le a(n_1,\ldots,n_{p-1},n_p-1)?\]

The expectation on the right uses a standard Gaussian tensor in the
smaller space.

\subsection{Relevance}\label{relevance}

Rank-one approximation algorithms navigate a landscape of stationary
points. This conjecture predicts how the average number of such points
changes when one mode becomes much larger than the others.

\subsection{Status check --- 2026-09-10}\label{status-check-2026-09-10}

Rechecked \href{https://arxiv.org/pdf/1408.3507}{Draisma--Horobeţ,
Conjecture 1.3}, including the direction of the inequality, and searched
by the authors, critical-rank-one counts and monotonicity. No proof or
counterexample to the real Gaussian statement was located. The matrix
case is excluded here, and stabilization of complex critical-point
counts does not settle this expectation. No recent explicit primary
reaffirmation was found.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Strict monotonicity on the large
\(2\times2\times m\) slice ---
2026-11-28}{Strict monotonicity on the large 2\textbackslash times2\textbackslash times m slice --- 2026-11-28}}\label{strict-monotonicity-on-the-large-2times2times-m-slice-2026-11-28}

Carptopus proves \(a(2,2,m)< a(2,2,m-1)\) for every integer
\(m\ge3\cdot10^{103}+3\). These comparisons are inside the Gaussian
model and the dimensional threshold of the displayed conjecture. The
conclusion covers this tail of one format family. The comparison between
\(m=3\) and \(m=4\), the other values below the proved tail, and other
format families remain open.
\href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23353313}{Carptopus, Theorem 1.1} ·
\href{https://github.com/Carptopus/MathExplore-Notes/blob/12ba9e48fd64e6721588b4db4f1972b8af118674/research/effective-eventual-monotonicity-22m-tensor-slice/manuscript.md}{Pinned
manuscript}.

The proof specializes the Draisma--Horobeţ determinant formula to block
sizes \(1,1,m-1\). Set \(r=m-2\) and let \(I_r\) be the expected
absolute determinant of

\[W-U I_2+\sqrt U\,Z\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{pmatrix},\]

where \(W\) is the sum of \(r+1\) independent standard Gaussian rank-one
outer products in dimension two, \(U\) has the \(\chi_r^2\)
distribution, and \(Z\) is standard normal, all independent. Schur
complementation and tilting the scalar normal by \(|t|^{r-1}\) give
\(a(2,2,m)=\pi I_r/(2r)\). The normalized determinant expectation has an
Edgeworth expansion \(I_r/r=L+C/r+D/r^2+e_r\). The verification bounds
\(C>0.0853526992029\), \(|D|<3753\) and
\(|e_r|<5.78\cdot10^{49}r^{-5/2}\) for \(r\ge2^{128}\). The positive
\(C/r\) correction then dominates the difference of the other terms
between \(r\) and \(r+1\) at the stated threshold.

The audit checked the Gaussian tilt and normalization and replayed the
frozen symbolic expansion, the outward interval bound for \(C\), and the
arithmetic ledgers for the Fourier remainder and final comparison. The
replay omitted the unused SciPy quadrature import from the shared
symbolic generator. The author identifies those quadrature probes as
numerical controls.
