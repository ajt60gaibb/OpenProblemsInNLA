# Pre-proof boundary: finite complete addition fibers imply exact rank

The exact proposed definitions and six universal propositions are NLA/TR06/FiniteFiberExactnessBoundary.lean. It imports only the established ClosedFibers module. It contains no theorem bodies, sorry/admit, new axioms or assertion that the proposed propositions have been proved. Frozen Definitions, Challenge and prior proof contributions are unchanged.

Final assertion ExactRankStatement is over every RCLike scalar field. Its hypotheses are 0 < d, all mode sizes n_j > 0, rank r >= 2, and finiteness plus nonemptiness of the ENTIRE existing closedAdditionFiber r A. Its conclusion is exactly the frozen ExactRank r A. It assumes no identifiability, chosen decomposition, genericity, finite Nat.card or new analytic premise. Set.Finite and Set.Infinite are the actual predicates throughout.

Definitions fix the concrete families in advance. splitTuple places t*a_0 and (1-t)*a_0 in positions 0 and 1; positions 2 through m contain a_1 through a_(m-1); all remaining positions are zero. cancelTuple places t*B and -(t*B) in positions 0 and 1 and zeros elsewhere. unitTensor is the actual all-ones pureTensor. No family is an orbit of arbitrary factor scalings: its entries are the actual tensors compared by the existing closed fiber.

1. RankBoundStatement: any nonempty closed fiber supplies an actual decomposition of some length m <= r. It has no format positivity or rank restrictions. Apply the established exists_decomposes_remove_zeros to a chosen tuple, then use its actual sum equality. This covers empty tuples and the zero tensor.
2. SplitFamilyStatement: if 0 < d, 0 < m < r, and a is an actual length-m decomposition of A, then the explicitly displayed splitTuple family is injective on the whole scalar field and every member belongs to the complete length-r fiber. A nonzero a_0 makes t -> t*a_0 injective. Scalar multiples are zero or rank one because a scalar can be absorbed into mode 0; at t = 0 or t = 1, zero entries are retained. The sum identity is t*a_0 + (1-t)*a_0 = a_0 together with the other summands and zero padding. No positive mode-size hypothesis is necessary in this branch: the actual nonzero summand supplies the requisite witness.
3. UnitTensorStatement: for positive mode sizes, the all-ones pureTensor is rank one and nonzero. Choose the coordinate consisting of index 0 in every mode and evaluate there to get 1. This statement intentionally includes d = 0; no scalar absorption is needed to exhibit this particular tensor.
4. CancelFamilyStatement: for 0 < d and r >= 2, every rank-one nonzero B supplies an injective cancelTuple family in the complete zero fiber. First-position equality again identifies the scalar. Opposite entries cancel, scalar closure gives zero-or-rank-one entries, and no scalars are discarded.
5. ShorterInfiniteStatement: under the final format assumptions, split on m = 0. For m > 0 use SplitFamilyStatement and infinitude of the RCLike field. For m = 0, the actual empty decomposition proves A = 0; UnitTensorStatement and CancelFamilyStatement supply the infinite family. In each case, use an injective scalar-indexed range contained in the complete fiber to conclude Set.Infinite. No Nat.card inference is made.
6. ExactRankStatement: RankBoundStatement gives m <= r with an actual decomposition. If m < r, ShorterInfiniteStatement contradicts the finite-fiber hypothesis; hence m = r and a length-r decomposition exists. Every hypothetical shorter decomposition separately forces an infinite fiber, contradicting finiteness. These are exactly the two clauses of frozen ExactRank.

The final restrictions are substantive, not cosmetic. When r = 1 the complete fiber over zero is the singleton zero tuple, so finite/nonempty does not imply exact rank 1. If a mode size is zero, the tensor space is zero and the same counterexample occurs at every positive r. When d = 0, frozen pureTensor has empty product 1, so rank-one tensors are only the unit tensor and arbitrary scalar absorption is invalid; finite closed fibers can then occur below the displayed length. The boundary therefore keeps d > 0 and does not extrapolate the final theorem to those cases.

This is a supporting exactness bridge for the later generic localization certificate. It does not prove that a particular localization fiber is finite/nonempty, identifiability, real versus complex rank equality, derivative injectivity, properness, smooth-locus correspondence or any integral bound. A later application to real fibers must separately establish its finite and nonempty premises, potentially from complex fibers and an actual real decomposition.
