\subsection{Resolution --- 2 November
2026}\label{resolution-2-november-2026}

\textbf{Resolved affirmatively.} Wenqi Zhu (University of Oxford),
Chunlin Liu (University of Science and Technology of China), Kai Zou
(King's College London) and Ping Nie (University of Waterloo).

For every \(n\ge2\) and every tuple \(\mathbf d=(d_1,\ldots,d_n)\) of
positive integers, with \(k=d_1+\cdots+d_n\) and \(H\) the base-two
entropy of the problem statement below,

\[\widetilde Q(D_{\mathbf d})=2^{H(d_1/k,\ldots,d_n/k)},
\qquad
Q(D_{\mathbf d}^{\boxtimes N})\le k\,2^{N H(d_1/k,\ldots,d_n/k)}\quad(N\ge1).\]

This is Theorem 1.1, stated on p.~2 of the 13-page paper
\href{https://github.com/erenup/dicke-tensor-subrank/blob/e344c05618a8a76941fc6dc166b22ed54384f6cf/paper/main.pdf}{\emph{The
Entropy Law for the Asymptotic Subrank of Dicke Tensors}} at the
immutable
\href{https://github.com/erenup/dicke-tensor-subrank/tree/e344c05618a8a76941fc6dc166b22ed54384f6cf}{revision
\textit{e344c05}}, dated 2 November 2026. Lemma 3.1 (p.~3) proves that
the limit defining \(\widetilde Q(D_{\mathbf d})\) exists, and Section 3
settles the two-factor case. For \(k\ge3\) the lower bound is obtained
by coordinate restrictions in Sections 4--9 and completed at the end of
Section 9 (p.~10). The upper bound, for arbitrary linear maps and every
power \(N\), is Proposition 10.3 (p.~11), proved by a slice-rank cover.

\textbf{Comparison with the target.} The paper's \(D_{\mathbf d}\) is
the displayed sum of basis tensors, with no scalar factor. Its subrank
\(Q(T)\) is the largest \(q\) for which complex linear maps
\(A_i:V_i\to\mathbb C^q\), chosen independently for the \(k\) factors,
satisfy \((A_1\otimes\cdots\otimes A_k)T=\sum_{j=1}^q e_j^{\otimes k}\),
with no symmetry or coordinate constraint, and
\(\widetilde Q(T)=\lim_{N\to\infty}Q(T^{\boxtimes N})^{1/N}\) for the
power that groups corresponding factors and retains exactly \(k\)
factors. These are the definitions of the problem statement. The paper
writes the value as \(\exp H(p)\) with \(p_a=d_a/k\) and natural
logarithms; this equals \(2^{H(d_1/k,\ldots,d_n/k)}\) in the base-two
notation used here. The theorem holds over \(\mathbb C\) for every
\(n\ge2\) and every tuple of positive integers, so it covers the full
range of the target, including the previously known cases \(n=2\) and
\(d_1=\cdots=d_n=1\). All \(k\) original modes remain separate. The
limit is pointwise in each fixed \(\mathbf d\); the theorem gives no
rate uniform in \(\mathbf d\), no finite-subrank equality, and no
statement about tensor rank, border rank or tensors with merged modes,
none of which the target asks for.

\textbf{Coordinate restrictions and Question 1.58 of reference 3.}
Theorem 1.1 also proves that
\(\widetilde Q_{\mathrm M}(D_{\mathbf d})\), defined in the same way
using only deletion of local basis vectors followed by independent
relabelling and nonzero rescaling, has the same value. Since
\(\widetilde Q_{\mathrm M}\le\widetilde Q\), this strengthens the lower
bound. Question 1.58 of reference 3 concerns a monomial rate defined by
monomial degeneration (Definition 1.18 there; Remark 1.12 there
distinguishes it from the rate defined by monomial restriction), not by
this coordinate-restriction limit. Equation (2.1) of the paper places
that rate between \(\log_2\widetilde Q_{\mathrm M}(D_{\mathbf d})\) and
\(\log_2\widetilde Q(D_{\mathbf d})\), using the equivalence of
restriction and degeneration for the ordinary rate (Theorem 1.11 of
reference 3), so the theorem also answers that question; no general
equivalence of the two monomial notions is used. The catalogue target
concerns ordinary subrank only and does not depend on this comparison.

\textbf{Proof and verification scope.} Theorem 1.1 is proved in the
written paper. A Lean formalization at the same revision proves only ten
universal finite algebraic and combinatorial targets, recorded in its
\href{https://github.com/erenup/dicke-tensor-subrank/blob/e344c05618a8a76941fc6dc166b22ed54384f6cf/lean/SCOPE.md}{formal
scope}: three partner-count identities, exact-span partner containment
and its count bound, a finite-field kernel count, the isolation count,
exact coordinate extraction, finite uniform isolation, and an abstract
dimension bound for invariant supermodular functions. The entropy
inequality, including the supermodularity of quotient entropy and its
application to the permutation action, the multinomial and average-free
estimates, the transfer of ranks to prime fields, the hashing
probabilities, the tensor interpretation of isolation, the existence of
the limits, the asymptotic lower bound and the slice-rank upper bound
are proved in the text only. No Lean theorem states the entropy formula,
so this is not a Lean verification of the target. The
\href{https://github.com/erenup/dicke-tensor-subrank/blob/e344c05618a8a76941fc6dc166b22ed54384f6cf/VERIFICATION.md}{verification
record} also describes exact checks of finite instances, which do not
establish the theorem.

\textbf{Review.} Solved by two independent informal AI-agent audits of

\subsection{Problem statement}\label{problem-statement}

Let \(n\geq2\) and let \(d_1,\ldots,d_n\) be positive integers with
\(k=d_1+\cdots+d_n\). In \((\mathbb C^n)^{\otimes k}\), define the Dicke
tensor

\[D_{\mathbf d}=
\sum_{\substack{(i_1,\ldots,i_k)\in\{1,\ldots,n\}^{k}\\
|\{j:i_j=a\}|=d_a\ \text{for all }a}}
e_{i_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_k}.\]

For any tensor \(T\in V_1\otimes\cdots\otimes V_k\), its subrank
\(Q(T)\) is the largest integer \(s\) for which there are linear maps
\(L_j:V_j\to\mathbb C^s\) satisfying

\[(L_1\otimes\cdots\otimes L_k)T=\sum_{a=1}^{s}e_a^{\otimes k}.\]

Let \(\widetilde Q(T)=\lim_{m\to\infty}Q(T^{\boxtimes m})^{1/m}\), where
powers group corresponding modes.

Does every Dicke tensor satisfy

\[\widetilde Q(D_{\mathbf d})
=2^{H(d_1/k,\ldots,d_n/k)},\qquad
H(p_1,\ldots,p_n)=-\sum_{a=1}^np_a\log_2p_a?\]

The maps in the subrank definition are arbitrary complex linear maps,
independently chosen in each mode. No symmetry or coordinate-selection
constraint is imposed. Multiplying \(D_{\mathbf d}\) by a nonzero scalar
does not change the question, so this agrees with the symmetric-tensor
convention for the monomial \(x_1^{d_1}\cdots x_n^{d_n}\).

\subsection{Why it matters}\label{why-it-matters}

Subrank measures how many independent scalar computations a tensor can
perform after linear transformations. The conjecture predicts the
exponential rate obtainable from repeated copies of an explicit
structured tensor solely from its multiplicity distribution.

\subsubsection{Status check ---
2026-09-10}\label{status-check-2026-09-10}

Checked the source formulation and the explicit 2025 restatement,
Conjecture 4.26, with targeted Dicke-tensor/subrank/entropy searches
through 2026. The formula is established for \(n=2\) and for
\(d_1=\cdots=d_n=1\), substantive parameter families within this target.
The all-multiplicity statement remains conjectural in the survey, and no
full later resolution was located. Asymptotic-rank computations and
lower bounds on subrank do not by themselves establish the requested
equality.
