\textbf{Rating rationale:} Controlling irreducibility for every
tensor-power cost sublevel is a foundational geometric question stronger
than finiteness of the attainable costs. Its immediate audience is
specialists in the algebraic geometry of tensor complexity. These
ratings are historical and refer to the original universal question,
which is resolved negatively below. They do not rate the remaining
order-three question.

\subsection{Resolution --- 3 November
2026}\label{resolution-3-november-2026}

\textbf{Resolved negatively.} Wenqi Zhu (University of Oxford), Qiyuan
Chen (Academy of Mathematics and Systems Science, Chinese Academy of
Sciences) and Ping Nie (University of Waterloo).

The answer to the universal question is \textbf{no}. Over \(\mathbb C\),
in the \(54\)-dimensional space
\(V=\mathbb C^3\otimes\mathbb C^3\otimes\mathbb C^3\otimes\mathbb C^2\)
of order four, the sublevel

\[V_{\leq a}=\{T\in V:\widetilde R(T)\leq a\},\qquad a=\sqrt[3]{62},\]

is the union of two proper relatively Zariski-closed subsets, so it is
not irreducible. This is Theorem 1.1, stated on p.~1 of the 16-page
paper
\href{https://github.com/erenup/asymptotic-rank-sublevels/blob/5e35de12ef4addb8ea26486e365de00146efdfce/paper/main.pdf}{\emph{Asymptotic
Tensor-Rank Sublevels Need Not Be Irreducible}} at the immutable
\href{https://github.com/erenup/asymptotic-rank-sublevels/tree/5e35de12ef4addb8ea26486e365de00146efdfce}{revision
\textit{5e35de1}}, made public on 3 November 2026.

The two witnesses are \(x=P\otimes d_0\), where \(P\) is the sum of the
six tensors \(e_i\otimes e_j\otimes e_k\) with \((i,j,k)\) a permutation
of \((0,1,2)\), and the rank-two tensor
\(y=e_0\otimes e_0\otimes e_0\otimes d_0+e_1\otimes e_1\otimes e_1\otimes d_1\)
(equation (1.3), p.~2). Section 3 (pp.~4--6) proves
\(\widetilde R(P)^3\leq74-3\sqrt[3]{81}<62\) by a finite Fourier
degeneration and Strassen's asymptotic-spectrum and tight-support
theorems for three-mode tensors. Corollary 7.2 of Alman and Li
(arXiv:2605.21738v1; Corollary 34 of the conference version), cited in
Section 1.1, applies to this tensor (their \(\mathrm{cw}_2'\)) and
already gives \(\widetilde R(P)\leq\gamma_2'\approx3.9309\) (their Table
1), below \(\sqrt[3]{62}\) and below the bound of Section 3. Section 3
gives a self-contained exact derivation; the paper makes no priority
claim for its tensor constructions or rank estimates. Section 4
(pp.~6--8) constructs a Zariski-open subset \(U\) of all of \(V\)
containing \(x\) on which rank at most three for the three balanced
flattenings forces fourth-mode flattening rank at most one (Proposition
4.3, p.~8). With \(X\) the closed locus of fourth-mode flattening rank
at most one, the proof of Theorem 1.1 at the end of Section 5 (p.~8)
gives the cover

\[V_{\leq a}=\bigl(V_{\leq a}\cap X\bigr)\cup\bigl(V_{\leq a}\cap(V\setminus U)\bigr),\]

in which \(y\) lies outside the first piece and \(x\) outside the
second.

\textbf{Comparison with the target.} The target asserts irreducibility
for every algebraically closed field \(\mathbb F\), every order
\(k\geq3\), all positive integers \(d_1,\ldots,d_k\) and every real
\(a\geq0\), so one reducible sublevel refutes it. Theorem 1.1 is the
admissible instance \(\mathbb F=\mathbb C\), \(k=4\),
\((d_1,d_2,d_3,d_4)=(3,3,3,2)\) and \(a=\sqrt[3]{62}\). The paper's rank
is the tensor rank with respect to the four indicated factors, and its
asymptotic rank is \(\lim_{m\to\infty}R(T^{\boxtimes m})^{1/m}\) with
corresponding modes grouped and \(\widetilde R(0)=0\) (equation (1.1),
p.~1); these are the definitions of the problem statement. The reducible
set is the full sublevel in the whole \(54\)-dimensional space, with no
linear section and no restriction to a family of tensors. Its two
covering pieces are relatively closed and each omits a point of the
sublevel, which is exactly the failure of irreducibility described
below. The proof of reducibility does not use the closedness theorem of
reference 1; that theorem makes \(V_{\leq a}\) an algebraic variety,
which is therefore reducible. The counterexample refutes only the
universal assertion. It does not decide the question for order three
(\(k=3\)), for algebraically closed fields other than \(\mathbb C\), or
for other formats and thresholds, apart from the further complex cases
of order at least four below.

\textbf{Extensions.} For integers \(n\geq3\) and \(k\geq4\), all
\(d_1,\ldots,d_k\geq2\), and \(t_n=\#\{i:d_i\geq n\}\), Theorem 6.5
(p.~12) proves that the complex sublevel in format \((d_1,\ldots,d_k)\)
has at least \(\binom{t_n}{3}+1\) irreducible components for every real
\(B_n\leq a< n+1\), where \(n< B_n< n+1\) is the explicit threshold of
Proposition 6.4 (p.~11); the same bound with \(t_3\) holds for
\(\sqrt[3]{62}\leq a<4\). The sublevel is therefore reducible whenever
\(t_n\geq3\). These are lower bounds, not a classification of
components. The counts grow only as the format varies, so they do not
settle the fixed-format finiteness question of
\href{https://github.com/ajt60gaibb/OpenProblemsInNLA/blob/main/tensor-computations/TR-22/README.md}{TR-22}.
In the other direction, Proposition 6.6 (p.~13) shows that for every
\(3\leq a<4\) the full three-mode sublevel in format \((9,3,2)\),
obtained from \((3,3,3,2)\) by grouping two three-dimensional modes, is
the irreducible variety of \(9\times6\) matrices of rank at most three.
This one irreducible three-mode case does not settle the order-three
question.

\textbf{What remains open.} Whether every asymptotic-rank sublevel of
order three is irreducible, over every algebraically closed field, in
every format and at every threshold, remains open. The argument uses
three balanced flattenings and the simultaneous slices of a fourth
factor, and the grouped example above shows that these constraints do
not transfer to three modes (Section 6.5, p.~13). The paper determines
neither a complete list of components nor the exact value of
\(\widetilde R(P)\).

\textbf{Proof and verification scope.} Theorem 1.1 and the extensions
are proved in the written paper. The cited inputs are Strassen's
spectral and tight-support theorems in the formulation of Christandl,
Vrana and Zuiddam; the closedness theorem of reference 1, for the
interpretation as a variety and in Section 6; and, for the
component-growth theorem, a speedup theorem of Alman and Li. A Lean
formalization at the same revision proves only a finite certificate
layer, recorded in its
\href{https://github.com/erenup/asymptotic-rank-sublevels/blob/5e35de12ef4addb8ea26486e365de00146efdfce/lean/README.md}{Lean
guide}, including the complete enumeration of the appended-minor
equations, the rank-26 matrix \(L(P)\) with kernel \(\mathbb CP\), the
witness flattening ranks, the finite Fourier exchange with all its
Laurent coefficient identities, the tight integer weights and marginal
counts, the ten-term identity for the small Coppersmith--Winograd tensor
and the integer cutoffs. It does not formalize asymptotic or border
rank, the spectral and tight-support theorems, or the passage from
entropy to asymptotic subrank. The bound on \(\widetilde R(P)\), the
basis change between the paired and diagonal Coppersmith--Winograd
tensors, the balanced-rank-to-kernel equivalence, the ambient open set,
the relatively closed cover and every result of Section 6 are proved in
the text only. No Lean theorem states the reducibility, so this is not a
Lean verification of the target. The
\href{https://github.com/erenup/asymptotic-rank-sublevels/blob/5e35de12ef4addb8ea26486e365de00146efdfce/VERIFICATION.md}{verification
record} also describes exact checks of finite instances, which do not
establish the theorem.

\textbf{Review.} Solved by independent informal AI-agent audits, two of
the main theorem and two of the component-growth results, together with
audits of the Lean statement and of the paper; none requested a
mathematical correction. The complete argument was also checked by
coauthor Wenqi Zhu (University of Oxford). Qiyuan Chen (Academy of
Mathematics and Systems Science, Chinese Academy of Sciences), a
coauthor, discussed the proof and agreed with it. The
\href{https://github.com/erenup/asymptotic-rank-sublevels/blob/5e35de12ef4addb8ea26486e365de00146efdfce/verification/reviews/README.md}{review
record} contains the audit reports. The paper discloses its use of AI
tools. The catalogue did not rerun the Lean or exact checks.

The retained status check below predates this resolution.

\subsection{Problem statement}\label{problem-statement}

Let \(\mathbb F\) be an algebraically closed field, let \(k\geq3\), and
let \(d_1,\ldots,d_k\) be positive integers. Write
\(V=\mathbb F^{d_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb F^{d_k}\). For
\(T\in V\), let \(R(T)\) be its tensor rank, and define its asymptotic
rank by

\[\widetilde R(T)=\lim_{m\to\infty}R(T^{\boxtimes m})^{1/m},\]

where \(\boxtimes\) groups corresponding modes of tensor powers; put
\(\widetilde R(0)=0\).

For every real \(a\geq0\), is the set

\[V_{\leq a}=\{T\in V:\widetilde R(T)\leq a\}\]

an irreducible algebraic variety?

The source proves that these sets are Zariski closed. The open assertion
is that none can be written as the union of two proper Zariski-closed
subsets. All fields, formats, and thresholds above are quantified;
\(0\in V_{\leq a}\) avoids the empty-set convention for irreducibility.

\subsection{Why it matters}\label{why-it-matters}

For matrices, bounded rank defines an irreducible determinantal variety.
The question asks whether a comparable geometric unity survives when
tensor rank is replaced by its asymptotic computational cost. A positive
answer would constrain the number of costs possible in a format and the
ways low-cost tensor families can degenerate.

This is stronger than merely asking for a finite set of asymptotic-rank
values. The source lists the two questions separately and records the
implication from irreducibility to discreteness; no converse is claimed.

\subsubsection{Status check ---
2026-09-10}\label{status-check-2026-09-10}

Checked the June 2026 arXiv revision, §5 second bullet, and the STOC
2025 irreducibility discussion, with targeted 2025--2026 searches. The
sources leave irreducibility open for tensors of order at least three.
Closedness is proved, but neither it nor the excluded matrix case
supplies a nontrivial resolved part of this universal irreducibility
target. Conditional implications from the full asymptotic-rank
conjecture are not unconditional solutions. No general proof or
counterexample was located.
