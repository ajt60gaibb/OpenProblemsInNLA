\textbf{Rating rationale:} An all-format theorem would overcome
longstanding obstructions in the local algebra of secant varieties and
encompass other major Cohen--Macaulay conjectures. The depth and
resolution questions chiefly concern specialists in tensor algebraic
geometry. These ratings are historical and refer to the original
universal question, which is resolved negatively below. They do not rate
the questions that remain open.

\subsection{Resolution --- 4 November
2026}\label{resolution-4-november-2026}

\textbf{Resolved negatively.} Wenqi Zhu (University of Oxford) and Ping
Nie (University of Waterloo).

The answer is \textbf{no}. For \(k=3\), \((n_1,n_2,n_3)=(3,4,5)\) and
\(r=5\), the variety
\(X_5\subseteq\mathbb C^3\otimes\mathbb C^4\otimes\mathbb C^5\) has
dimension \(50\), and its coordinate ring \(A_5=S/I(X_5)\) satisfies

\[\mathop{\mathrm{depth}}\nolimits(A_5)_{\mathfrak m}
<50=\dim(A_5)_{\mathfrak m},\]

so \(A_5\) is not Cohen--Macaulay. This is the case \(X_5(3,4,5)\) of
Corollary 1.2, stated on p.~2 of the 42-page paper
\href{https://github.com/erenup/segre-secant-cohen-macaulay/blob/3d8b1586b4e6e7ee7164116711aced9f7a23a057/paper/main.pdf}{\emph{Secant
Varieties of Segre Products Need Not Be Cohen--Macaulay}} at the
immutable
\href{https://github.com/erenup/segre-secant-cohen-macaulay/tree/3d8b1586b4e6e7ee7164116711aced9f7a23a057}{revision
\textit{3d8b158}}, made public on 4 November 2026. Up to the order of
the factors, it is the smallest format in the range of Theorem 1.1
(p.~1): for all integers

\[r\geq5,\qquad a,b\geq3,\qquad a+b\geq r+2,\qquad c\geq r,\]

and after any permutation of the three factors, the reduced affine
variety
\(X_r(a,b,c)\subseteq\mathbb C^a\otimes\mathbb C^b\otimes\mathbb C^c\)
of tensors of border rank at most \(r\) has dimension \(D=r(a+b+c-2)\),
fails Serre's condition \(S_2\), is not Cohen--Macaulay, and has depth
less than \(D\) at its homogeneous maximal ideal. Corollary 1.2 also
lists \(X_5(6,6,6)\), of dimension \(80\), the varieties
\(X_r(3,r-1,r)\) of dimension \(2r^2\), and the unit-tensor orbit
closures
\(X_r(r,r,r)=\overline{\mathrm{GL}_r(\mathbb C)^3\cdot\Delta_r}\), with
\(\Delta_r=\sum_{i=1}^re_i\otimes e_i\otimes e_i\), for every
\(r\geq5\).

In format \(3\times4\times5\) the witness is the integer tensor
\(T=\sum_{i=1}^5Q_i\otimes f_i\) of equation (4.5) (p.~10), padded with
zeros from \(3\times3\times5\). Here \(Q_1,\ldots,Q_6\) are the rank-one
\(3\times3\) matrices formed from the rows and the negated columns of
\(M=(j^{i-1})_{1\leq i,j\leq3}\), and their only linear relation is
\(Q_1+\cdots+Q_6=0\) (Lemma 4.1, p.~10). That relation gives a second
five-term decomposition (4.7). By Proposition 4.6 (p.~13), \(T\) has
rank and border rank five and both decompositions have injective
addition differentials. Section 2 (pp.~3--8) constructs a smooth
birational space of decompositions with an open subset that maps into
the smooth locus and has a complement of codimension at least two
(Proposition 2.5, p.~7). The two-point conductor obstruction of
Proposition 3.3 (p.~9) then shows that the local ring at \(T\) is not
\(S_2\): the two decompositions give two distinct normalization charts
over \(T\), and \(S_2\) would force a conductor divisor that the
codimension bound excludes. Section 5 (pp.~14--15) passes to the vertex:
if \((A_r)_{\mathfrak m}\) were Cohen--Macaulay, a graded free
resolution and the Auslander--Buchsbaum formula would make every local
ring Cohen--Macaulay.

\textbf{Comparison with the target.} The target asserts
Cohen--Macaulayness for every \(k\geq3\), all \(n_1,\ldots,n_k\geq1\)
and every \(r\geq1\), so one non-Cohen--Macaulay instance refutes it.
The paper's \(X_r(a,b,c)\) (equation (1.1), p.~1) is the Zariski closure
of the sums of \(r\) pure tensors in
\(\mathbb C^a\otimes\mathbb C^b\otimes\mathbb C^c\) with its reduced
structure, so it is the affine cone and contains the zero tensor. Its
coordinate ring is \(S/I(X_r)\), and \(\mathfrak m\) is its ideal of
positive-degree elements. These are the definitions of the problem
statement with \(k=3\) and \((n_1,n_2,n_3)=(a,b,c)\), over the same
field \(\mathbb C\). Since Theorem 1.1 holds after permuting the
factors, the order of \((3,4,5)\) is immaterial. Corollary 1.3 (p.~2)
gives the projective statement:
\(\sigma_r(\mathrm{Seg}(\mathbb P^{a-1}\times\mathbb P^{b-1}\times\mathbb P^{c-1}))\)
has a non-Cohen--Macaulay local ring at the class of the witness, so it
is neither locally nor arithmetically Cohen--Macaulay. Section 1.1
(p.~2) notes that Conjecture 1.1 of reference 1 and Conjecture 3.32 of
reference 2 concern the ordinary reduced secant varieties, with neither
a conciseness condition nor a restriction to balanced formats, and that
Theorem 1.1 refutes them with the same field, reduced structure and
projective embedding.

The unit-tensor orbit closures \(X_r(r,r,r)\) were already known to be
nonnormal for every \(r\geq5\), by Bürgisser and Ikenmeyer (Corollary
7.2(2) of arXiv:1011.1350v1 and Corollary 5.26 of arXiv:1511.02927v2),
as cited in Section 1.2 (pp.~2--3). Nonnormality does not imply failure
of \(S_2\) or of Cohen--Macaulayness; the paper's example is the
Cohen--Macaulay, nonnormal domain \(\mathbb C[t^2,t^3]\). As the paper
states, Theorem 1.1 strengthens the known nonnormality of these orbit
closures to failure of \(S_2\) and of Cohen--Macaulayness, and the proof
does not use the nonnormality results. In reference 2, implications run
from the universal conjecture to Cohen--Macaulayness assertions for
surface Quot schemes and smoothable Hilbert scheme components. Section
6.4 (pp.~18--19) notes that its failure gives no counterexample to these
assertions or to Haiman's isospectral Hilbert scheme conjecture;
Propositions 6.3 and 6.4 (pp.~17--18) show that the mechanism cannot
occur on invertible-slice charts and that the witness lifts to smooth
points of the concise secant. The counterexample refutes only the
universal assertion. It does not decide Cohen--Macaulayness in formats
and ranks outside Theorem 1.1, apart from the extensions below.

\textbf{Extensions.} In the other direction, Proposition 6.2 (p.~16)
shows that \(X_5(3,3,c)\) is Cohen--Macaulay for every \(c\geq1\): it is
the whole space for \(c\leq5\) and the variety of \(9\times c\) matrices
of rank at most five for \(c\geq5\). Section 7 (pp.~19--40) describes
the normalization near explicit witnesses. Corollary 7.3 (p.~20) gives
exactly six reduced, smooth normalization points over the rank-five
witness for all \(a,b\geq3\), \(a+b\geq7\) and \(c\geq5\), and Theorems
7.6, 7.8 and 7.9 (pp.~22--26) give exact smooth fibres of six, seven and
\(r+1\) points over higher-rank witnesses. Theorem 7.10 (p.~27) presents
the completed local ring at the rank-five witness, with dimension
\(5(a+b+c-2)\) and depth \(3a+3b+5c+3\); in format \(3\times4\times5\)
these are \(50\) and \(49\) (Remark 7.11, p.~30). Theorems 7.12--7.14
(pp.~31--32) give the normalization fibres and the local models of their
boundary points for one-sided extensions and for two specified mixed
extensions. These results concern the displayed witnesses.

\textbf{What remains open.} The paper determines neither the depth at
the homogeneous vertex nor, for \(r\geq6\), the depth at the witness
(p.~2; Remark 7.11, p.~30). It makes no minimal-format assertion (pp.~3
and 17), and formats outside the theorem, such as \(X_5(3,4,4)\) and
\(X_5(4,4,4)\), in which no factor has dimension at least five, are not
decided. Theorem 6.1 (p.~15) shows that no three-factor tensor has two
distinct immersive \(r\)-term decompositions when \(r\leq4\), so the
mechanism first occurs at rank five; this does not decide
Cohen--Macaulayness for \(r\leq4\) (p.~16). The paper gives no
classification of the Cohen--Macaulay border-rank varieties, none in
order \(k\geq4\), and no Cohen--Macaulay or rational-singularity
assertion about their normalizations (pp.~3 and 19).

\textbf{Proof and verification scope.} Theorem 1.1, its corollaries and
the extensions are proved in the written paper. A Lean formalization at
the same revision proves exactly two finite certificate layers, in two
pinned projects using only the standard axioms.
\textit{TR25.finiteCertificate}, recorded in its
\href{https://github.com/erenup/segre-secant-cohen-macaulay/blob/3d8b1586b4e6e7ee7164116711aced9f7a23a057/lean/witness-666/README.md}{Lean
guide}, covers the \(3\times3\times5\) integer witness and its zero
padding to \(6\times6\times6\): both decompositions with nonzero,
pairwise nonproportional summands and different directions, third
flattening rank five, the six rational determinant values in (4.11) and
(4.12), and coordinate Jacobian ranks \(45\) and \(80\).
\textit{TR25Family.finiteCertificate}, recorded in its
\href{https://github.com/erenup/segre-secant-cohen-macaulay/blob/3d8b1586b4e6e7ee7164116711aced9f7a23a057/lean/family-345/README.md}{Lean
guide}, covers the witness in format \(3\times4\times5\): both
decompositions, flattening ranks \(3,3,5\), the pair-product and
third-factor ranks, the six determinant values and both coordinate
Jacobian ranks \(50\). Neither formalizes border rank, the dimension of
the secant variety, the geometric tangent spaces, normalization, the
conductor argument, \(S_2\), Cohen--Macaulayness, any depth statement
(vertex or local) or the family for all ranks and formats. No numbered
result of the paper is a Lean theorem in full, and no Lean theorem
states the failure of Cohen--Macaulayness, so this is not a Lean
verification of the target. The
\href{https://github.com/erenup/segre-secant-cohen-macaulay/blob/3d8b1586b4e6e7ee7164116711aced9f7a23a057/VERIFICATION.md}{verification
record} also describes exact checks of the witness calculations, of the
finite cores of the Section 7 results and of 31 pairs of family
decompositions, which certify finite calculations and do not establish
the theorem.

\subsection{Problem statement}\label{problem-statement}

Fix integers \(k\geq3\), \(n_1,\ldots,n_k\geq1\), and \(r\geq1\). In
\(V=\mathbb C^{n_1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathbb C^{n_k}\), let

\[X_r=\overline{\left\{
\sum_{\ell=1}^{r}v_{1,\ell}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{k,\ell}:
v_{i,\ell}\in\mathbb C^{n_i}
\right\}}^{\,\mathrm{Zar}}.\]

Let \(S=\mathbb C[V]\) be the polynomial ring in the tensor entries, let
\(I(X_r)\) be its vanishing ideal, and let \(A_r=S/I(X_r)\).

Is \(A_r\) Cohen--Macaulay for every such format and rank threshold?

Explicitly, if \(\mathfrak m\) is the ideal of positive-degree elements
of \(A_r\), the requested equality is

\[\mathop{\mathrm{depth}}\nolimits(A_r)_{\mathfrak m}
=\dim(A_r)_{\mathfrak m}.\]

Depth is the maximum length of a regular sequence in the maximal ideal:
each successive element is a non-zero-divisor after quotienting by its
predecessors. Dimension is Krull dimension. This is Oeding's conjecture
that all secant varieties of Segre products are arithmetically
Cohen--Macaulay. The zero tensor is retained by using the affine cone.

\subsection{Why it matters}\label{why-it-matters}

These are the algebraic sets underlying low-border-rank tensor
approximation. Cohen--Macaulayness constrains their equations and free
resolutions, supporting the determination of generators and the analysis
of degeneracies. The conjecture does not assert that exact low-rank
tensor approximation is computationally easy or always attained.

\subsubsection{Status check ---
2026-09-10}\label{status-check-2026-09-10}

Checked Oeding v2, including Theorems 3.1--3.2 and Proposition 3.5, and
Jagiełła--Jelisiejew v2, Conjecture 3.32, with targeted later-resolution
searches. Oeding proves substantive tensor cases, including the fourth
secant of \(\mathbb P^2\times\mathbb P^2\times\mathbb P^n\) for
\(n\ge3\), rather than only the matrix or rank-one cases. The June 2026
source still treats the all-format conjecture as open. Inheritance
results require extra hypotheses and do not establish it universally. No
full proof or counterexample was located.
