## Problem statement

Let $`V=\mathbb C^4\otimes\mathbb C^4\otimes\mathbb C^4`$, with coordinates $`t_{ijk}`$, and let

```math
X=\overline{\left\{
\sum_{\ell=1}^{4}a_\ell\otimes b_\ell\otimes c_\ell:
a_\ell,b_\ell,c_\ell\in\mathbb C^4
\right\}}^{\,\mathrm{Zar}}\subset V.
```

Thus $`X`$ is the affine cone over the fourth secant variety of the Segre embedding of $`\mathbb P^3\times\mathbb P^3\times\mathbb P^3`$. Its points are exactly the tensors of complex border rank at most four.

Let

```math
I=I(X)=\{f\in\mathbb C[t_{ijk}]: f(T)=0\text{ for every }T\in X\}
```

be its homogeneous prime ideal. For each integer $`a\geq0`$, let $`I_a`$ be the vector space of homogeneous degree-$`a`$ polynomials in $`I`$. Is

```math
I=\langle I_5\cup I_6\cup I_9\rangle
```

as an ideal of $`\mathbb C[t_{ijk}]`$?

The angle brackets mean all finite polynomial combinations of elements of these three homogeneous spaces. This is the revised Salmon conjecture in the degree-generation form explicitly stated by Friedland–Gross, §1. It asks for equality of ideals, not merely equality of zero sets or equality after taking radicals. It does not prescribe an unverified number of generators or identify the full homogeneous pieces with particular proposed representation modules.

## Why it matters

The question asks for the tensor counterpart of determinantal equations for low-rank matrices. Exact generators describe algebraic constraints in small tensor decompositions and statistical mixture models, and support symbolic and numerical methods for testing and studying these constraints.

### Status check — 2026-09-10

Checked Friedland–Gross §1 against the displayed ideal equality and Jagiełła–Jelisiejew v2, §1.5.3, together with targeted 2025–2026 Salmon-ideal searches. The 2026 source explicitly retains the ideal question. The proved equality of zero sets only identifies the radical of the proposed ideal; it does not settle a portion of this fixed ideal-equality assertion. Numerical or local vanishing and dimension certificates likewise do not prove ideal equality. No complete proof was located, so the status remains Open.

### Scheme-theoretic generation on the cyclic open set — 2026-11-28

An invertible matrix slice permits a localized ideal calculation. Write the
four slices in one mode as $`A_0,A_1,A_2,A_3`$, and work on
$`\det A_0\ne0`$. Put $`B_i=A_iA_0^{-1}`$ for $`i=1,2,3`$.
A joint cyclic vector is a vector $`v`$ for which the ordered monomials in
$`B_1,B_2,B_3`$ applied to $`v`$ span $`\mathbb C^4`$. Existence of such a
vector defines an open condition, by taking the union of the loci where
four such vectors form a basis for some fixed $`v`$. On this open set,
the ideal sheaf of $`X`$ is generated by the restrictions of the degree-five
polynomials

```math
\big[A_i\mathop{\mathrm{adj}}\nolimits(A_0)A_j-A_j\mathop{\mathrm{adj}}\nolimits(A_0)A_i\big]_{ab}
\qquad (1\le i< j\le3,\ 1\le a,b\le4).
```

These polynomials are in $`I_5`$. On a sum of four pure tensors with
$`A_0`$ invertible, each slice has the form $`U D_i W^{\mathsf T}`$ for
diagonal $`D_i`$ and invertible $`U,W`$, with $`D_0`$ invertible. Thus all
$`B_i=U D_iD_0^{-1}U^{-1}`$ commute, and vanishing extends from this dense
set to $`X`$. After inverting $`\det A_0`$, the entries above generate the
same ideal as the entries of $`[B_i,B_j]`$. The change of coordinates
$`(A_0,A_i)\leftrightarrow(A_0,B_i)`$ identifies the scheme defined by
these polynomials with $`\mathop{\mathrm{GL}}\nolimits_4`$ times the scheme of
commuting triples, restricted to the cyclic open set.

To check reducedness on that open set, include a choice of joint cyclic
vector $`v`$. The resulting scheme is the frame bundle of the rank-four
tautological bundle on $`\mathop{\mathrm{Hilb}}\nolimits^4(\mathbb A^3_{\mathbb C})`$.
Indeed, a framed rank-four quotient of $`\mathbb C[x_1,x_2,x_3]`$ gives the
three multiplication matrices and the image of $`1`$. Conversely,
$`f\mapsto f(B_1,B_2,B_3)v`$ is surjective by cyclicity and its kernel is
an ideal by commutativity. These constructions also work over a base ring
on the open sets where a cyclic determinant is invertible and hence
identify the schemes. The Hilbert scheme is reduced and irreducible, so
its frame bundle is reduced. Forgetting $`v`$ is a smooth surjection
onto the cyclic open subset of the commuting scheme, since it is the
restriction of an affine-space projection to the open set of cyclic
vectors. Reducedness descends under that map.
[Jelisiejew–Šivic, §3.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.13137) ·
[Hauenstein–Manivel–Szendrői, §2.1](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~jhauenst/preprints/hmsHilbertSchemes.pdf).

The locus of four distinct points is dense in the Hilbert scheme. On
that locus the multiplication matrices are simultaneously diagonalizable,
which gives a sum of four pure tensors after restoring $`A_0`$. Thus the
underlying zero set of the localized equations is exactly $`X`$ on this
open set. Since they define a reduced scheme there, they generate the
ideal of $`X`$ itself. Transposing the slices proves the corresponding
result when the transposed tuple has a joint cyclic vector.

This calculation includes degenerate points. For example, the tuple
$`(B_1,B_2,B_3)=(E_{21},E_{31},E_{41})`$ has cyclic vector $`e_1`$ and
represents the length-four scheme at the origin with ideal
$`(x_1,x_2,x_3)^2`$, which is a singular point of the Hilbert scheme.
The global ideal equality still requires controlling points outside the
open sets proved here, including those without an invertible slice.
