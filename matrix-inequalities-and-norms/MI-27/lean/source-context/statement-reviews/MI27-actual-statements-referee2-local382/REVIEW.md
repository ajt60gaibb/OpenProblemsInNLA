## Original target and transparent meanings

I read the canonical README and all of Holden's supplied analytic solution, including the later sharpness and consequence sections. The original question asks whether the displayed upper bound holds for every complex positive definite pair, every integer dimension at least one, and complex trace normalization `trace(A+B)=1`, with coefficient precisely one. C20 has exactly these hypotheses and conclusion. It does not specialize to real matrices, commuting matrices, dimension two, a conditioned subset, or an unspecified dimension-free constant. Section 5's additional optimality construction and Section 6's extra consequences are not claimed formalized by these twenty contracts; they are not additional requirements of the original yes/no upper-bound target.

`Mat n` is the concrete complex square matrix type. `logM` is Mathlib's `CFC.log`, whose pinned definition is `cfc Real.log`; on the positive spectrum this is the natural logarithm. `traceNorm X` is literally the real trace of `CFC.rpow (Xᴴ * X) (1/2 : ℝ)`. The Gram matrix is positive semidefinite, so this is the ordinary full trace norm, with no factor one-half. `opNorm` uses `Matrix.toEuclideanCLM`, not an entrywise norm. The open `MatrixOrder` scope gives `A ≤ B` exactly when `B-A` is positive semidefinite. `posM` is spectral `max t 0`.

Entropy and relative entropy are ordinary matrix trace/log expressions. Neither is defined by the desired integral identity. `StrictDensity` means exactly positive definiteness and complex trace one; it includes no commutation, differentiability, spectral-gap oracle, or entropy identity. The normalization from original inputs is itself a conclusion of C03. The sign, trace, scalar powers, division and interval endpoints were checked against the definitions rather than inferred from theorem names.

## Primary identity and prospective proof route

I independently opened [Hirche–Tomamichel v3, §2.3, Corollary 2.3 (2.22)](https://arxiv.org/html/2306.12343v3#S2.SS3) and [Frenkel v4, Theorem 6](https://arxiv.org/html/2208.12194v4). The former states the required ordinary Umegaki identity with weights 1/γ and 1/γ². Its extended hockey-stick definition subtracts a positive trace part, which vanishes for the trace-one states and γ≥1 used here. Frenkel's extra trace-difference term likewise vanishes for equal trace. This source check supports C11's meaning, not a Lean proof of it.

The supplemental overlap/Taylor route for C16 respects noncommutativity: the quadratic remainder is controlled by the doubly stochastic overlap sum and tr((Y-X)²), without differentiating eigenvectors. The suggested C11 layer-cake/congruence route distinguishes inertia from eigenvalues and does not assume trace invariance under congruence. I checked its scalar c>0, -1<c<0 and c=0 integral cases and the cancellation of the equal-trace term at infinity. Its complex-Hermitian inertia, measurability/Tonelli, log-determinant and improper-integral lemmas are still substantial missing implementation prerequisites. Approval of these final reference types is not a claim that this proposed route is already formalized or inexpensive.
