# MI-10 — Audit of the all-orders Chollet inequality

**Sources.** Zhouding Liu, [*Gram variance and permutation information in Chollet's permanent inequality*](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24423034), version 2, Theorem 1.1, Sections 2–3, Section 4.1 and the limit argument on page 45. Yi Yu, [*An independent proof of the key inequality behind Chollet's conjecture*](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24562562), version 1, Theorem 3.1 and Remark 8.1. Both disclose AI assistance. This independent informal Codex audit checks the proof using Liu's reduction and Yu's certificate for nonnegativity.

## From a matrix pair to scalar edge budgets

- The Cauchy–Schwarz step uses the complex conjugate of each permutation monomial in its squared magnitude. Thus it bounds $|\operatorname{per}(A\circ B)|$ by the geometric mean of $\operatorname{per}(A\circ\overline A)$ and $\operatorname{per}(B\circ\overline B)$. Positive diagonal scaling reduces each self-comparison to correlation matrices. A zero diagonal of a positive semidefinite matrix forces a zero row, giving the original assertion directly in that case. Write $P=\operatorname{per}(A)$ and $Q=\operatorname{per}(A\circ\overline A)$ for the normalized self-comparison. These permanents are positive by the block-permanent lower bound applied to singletons.
- Liu's Section 2 first assigns a positive response $t_i$ to each vertex of the entrywise squared-magnitude matrix. Upper and lower constant vectors give a fixed point by monotone iteration. The largest ratio between two fixed points gives uniqueness by strict increase of the load function. The response bounds from Lemma 2.2 and Proposition 2.3 apply to the normalized cofactors as well as their marginal matrix. For the positive definite case with nonzero off-diagonals the cofactor marginal matrix is real, symmetric, nonnegative, positive semidefinite and row-stochastic. Positive semidefiniteness follows from the Gram representation of the products of linear forms with one factor omitted, followed by a Schur product. This gives the cofactor and diagonal bounds and the squared-distance inequality used in Yu's domain.
- Proposition 2.4 increases the off-diagonal marginals while retaining the matching-polytope constraints. The vertex caps sum to each vertex's unused degree. For an odd set, the unused edge sum is half its centered Gram-vector variance plus a nonnegative outgoing mass term. The harmonic-mean cap spends at most half the variance by summing the two-endpoint squared-norm inequality over the edges. Edmonds' matching-polytope theorem then represents the augmented weights as a mixture of genuine matchings.
- For the upper bound on $\log Q$, the random-priority exposure keeps one off-diagonal column explicit. Conditioned on the chosen permutation and the current row's priority, the remaining owners have independent priorities. The reference-weight entropy inequality is applied before choosing how to retain the off-diagonal column in the bound. When the row uses its diagonal, sample that column from its conditional off-diagonal marginal. When the row uses an off-diagonal column, keep its assigned column. The two cases yield the same marginal weights, and the row choice of reference scale gives the binary entropy and conserved-mass remainder in Lemma 2.5. The diagonal conjugation leaves each permutation's product unchanged and makes the directed fractions the same ones used in the matching argument.
- For the lower bound on $2\log P$, apply Lieb's block inequality to the retained group and each fully omitted matched pair. Average the retention policies and use the complex Gaussian moment representation of a permanent. Jensen's inequality under the common Gaussian covariance yields the singleton terms, determinant correction and matched-pair gains. The Gibbs identity in Theorem 2.6 aligns the singleton terms with the entropy bound. Its relative-entropy terms enter with a positive sign, and the row allocation fractions sum to one. The compressed resolvent identity gives the determinant contribution for each edge with those same fractions. The pair gain uses the joint product of squared Gaussian radii. Its moment formula gives the phase-average representation by the Stieltjes Carleman criterion, since the moment bound $(n!)^2 4^n$ makes the reciprocal-root series diverge.
- The edge comparison starts with a complex overlap in the disk obtained by subtracting the two vertices' contributions from the frame operator. Taking its real part decreases both the source term and the coherent gain. If the resulting real overlap is less than $-\sqrt p$, moving it up to $-\sqrt p$ remains in the disk and further decreases both terms. The independent gain lower bound is unaffected. This gives a real admissible point in Yu's domain, and all coefficients of the decreased terms are nonnegative. The squared-distance and cofactor constraints on the remaining variables follow from the marginal Gram matrix and the response bounds. Equation (4.6) follows by summing the edge source, gain, charge, and row terms with weights $p_{ij}=|a_{ij}|^2$.

Yu's Lemma 4.1 reduces the sign of each scalar edge budget to that of a four-variable function. The augmentation is bounded below using the diagonal lower bounds and the cofactor upper bound. The entropy and conserved-mass remainders are nonnegative. The remaining positive terms can be scaled by $q/q^*\in[0,1]$, which includes the case $q=0$. Yu's end lemmas cover the small lower load and large upper load. The remaining compact box is covered by the certificate below.

Thus every summand in Liu's inequality $2\log P-\log Q\ge\sum_{i<j}p_{ij}B_{ij}$ is nonnegative. For a general correlation matrix, $(A+\epsilon I+\epsilon^2J)/(1+\epsilon+\epsilon^2)$ is positive definite and has nonzero off-diagonals except at finitely many positive $\epsilon$. Permanents are polynomials, so the inequality passes to the limit $\epsilon\to0$ and then to the original PSD pairs by the first step.
