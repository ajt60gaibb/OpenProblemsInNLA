# MI-14 — Audit of the complex Lu–Wenzel proof

**Source.** Qiyue Tang, [*The Lu–Wenzel Conjecture*](https://hexagonmath.org/2611.00179v2), version 2, [Theorem 1, Sections 2–5 and Appendix A](https://hexagonmath.org/pdf/2611.00179v2). The author discloses substantial AI assistance. This is an independent informal Codex review of the proof of Theorem 1.

## Exact correspondence

Set $D_X(Y)=[X,Y]$ on the complex Frobenius space. Then $D_X^*(Y)=[X^*,Y]$, so the preprint's $T_X=D_X^*D_X$ is the operator in [MI-14](README.md). The commutator is unchanged by subtracting $\operatorname{tr}(X)I/n$. Theorem 1 gives the bound with multiplier $\|X-\operatorname{tr}(X)I/n\|_F^2$, at most one for the unit matrices in MI-14. It covers every $n\ge2$ and all the requested cutoffs with complex multiplicities.

## The proof

1. On the traceless unit sphere, write $G(X)=\operatorname{tr}(T_X-I)_+$. Every partial sum of $T_X$ minus its length is at most $G(X)$. The adjoint map $Y\mapsto Y^*$ composed with $D_X$ is conjugate-linear and squares to $-T_X$. On a positive eigenvalue $\alpha$, dividing by $\sqrt{\alpha}$ gives an antiunitary with square $-I$. Pairing a unit vector with its image gives an orthonormal pair, and its orthogonal complement is invariant. In particular, if $P$ is a separated positive spectral projection and $W=\operatorname{ran}P$, then $D_X W=W^*$. For an orthonormal basis $q_i$ of $W$, the tensor $\sum_i D_X q_i\otimes q_i^*$ is skew under interchange, pair by pair.

2. Suppose $G$ has maximum greater than two. At a maximizing matrix use the spectral cutoff at the eigenvalues strictly above one. The selected eigenvalues $\alpha_i$ are separated from all omitted eigenvalues $\beta_j$, including omitted eigenvalues equal to one. Keep the cutoff index fixed on a neighborhood. There the separated sum $F=\operatorname{tr}(PT_X)$ is smooth by the resolvent formula, and $F-r\le G$ for $r=\operatorname{rank}P$, with equality at the chosen matrix. Hence its local maximum set at this level lies inside the maximum set of $G$.

3. In the separated Hessian, differentiating $PT_X=T_XP$ gives the off-diagonal projection derivative with denominator $\alpha_i-\beta_j$. Combining the directions $Y,iY$ cancels the mixed terms and gives the coefficient $(\alpha_i+\beta_j)/(\alpha_i-\beta_j)$ in (11). This remains valid at $\beta_j=0$, where $D_Xq_j=0$. For the contraction in Section 4, put $u_{aij}=\operatorname{tr}(D_Xq_a[q_i,q_j])$. Expanding the skew-tensor contraction in (13) leaves two equal terms of squared norm, with the other two cancelling by skewness and cyclicity. Parseval gives (15). The derivation rule gives $u_{aij}-u_{iaj}+u_{jai}=0$. On triples of selected indices the three squared-modulus sums agree by permutation, which gives (17). On the remaining triples the contraction in (21) is the sum of $|u_{aij}-u_{iaj}|^2$ and the two terms with coefficients $\beta_j/(\alpha_i-\beta_j)$ and $\beta_j/(\alpha_a-\beta_j)$. These coefficients are nonnegative throughout the gap neighborhood.

4. At each local maximum the gradient vanishes and the Hessian is nonpositive, so (24) forces the sum in (21) to vanish. For an omitted positive eigenvalue all three $u$ terms vanish. Thus $[W,W]$ is orthogonal to the omitted positive input eigenspaces. By stationarity, $F X$ is a sum of $[D_Xq_i,q_i^*]$, so $X$ lies in $[W^*,W^*]$ and is orthogonal to the corresponding omitted output spaces. Applying $D_X^*$ removes the zero output space and gives $C=[X^*,X]\in W$. This uses the distinction between the input and output eigenspaces.

5. Among the global maximizers of $G$, choose one minimizing $E=\|C\|_F^2$. In the gap neighborhood let $\Pi$ be the complex orthogonal projection away from $X$ and the scalars. Equation (24) makes $F$ a subsolution for the sum of squares of the real fields $\Pi D_XP e_a$ and $i\Pi D_XP e_a$, with a smooth drift. The covariant derivative terms in the drift convert the sphere Hessians into squares of fields. Appendix A gives the necessary local maximum propagation. Its exponential barrier shows that each diffusion field is tangent to every exterior supporting ball of the maximum set. The squared distance along the resulting flow satisfies $f'\le 2Lf$. For this step one can intersect the maximum set with a closed ball strictly inside the coordinate chart. Closest points for the short flow lie in the interior of that ball by the distance bound to the initial point, so the supporting-ball argument applies locally. This also works when the diffusion covariance is singular.

6. Apply propagation to the smooth field $-\Pi D_X P C$, a real linear combination of the diffusion fields. On the maximum set it equals $-D_X C+ E X$, using $PC=C$ and $\langle X,D_X C\rangle=E$. The derivative of $E$ along the field is $-4\|D_X C-EX\|_F^2$. The minimizer therefore satisfies $D_X C=EX$. If $C\ne0$, then $T_X C=EC$ and $C\in W$ give $E>1$. Diagonalize $C$ with eigenvalues $c_i$. Each nonzero entry $X_{ij}$ has $c_j-c_i=E$. For $s=\operatorname{rank} X$, a nonzero term of a nonzero $s$-by-$s$ minor matches $s$ distinct rows and columns. Summing the equations over the matching gives $sE$, a signed sum of the $c_i$ with at most $2s$ nonzero coefficients, each of modulus one. Cauchy–Schwarz gives $(sE)^2\le 2sE$, forcing $s=1$.

7. If $C=0$, $X$ is normal. With diagonal entries $x_i$ after unitary diagonalization, form the graph whose $m$ edges are the unordered pairs with $|x_i-x_j|^2>1$. For its oriented incidence matrix $A$, $G/2=\|A^*x\|^2-m$. The absolute row sum of $A^*A$ for edge $ij$ is $\deg i+\deg j\le m+1$, so $G\le2$. If $C\ne0$, the traceless rank-one unit matrix is unitarily similar to $E_{12}$. The nonzero eigenvalues of its $T_X$ are two copies of two and $2n-4$ copies of one, as follows by applying $D_X$ to matrix units and to the difference of the first two diagonal matrix units. Thus this case also gives $G=2$. Both contradict the assumed maximum.

The argument yields the full complex inequality. The witness $E_{12}$ also attains equality at cutoffs $1\le k\le n-1$.
