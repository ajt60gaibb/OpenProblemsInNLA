\subsection{Structural non-attainment and exact 2×2 collapse --- 27 November
2026}\label{structural-non-attainment-and-exact-2x2-collapse-27-november-2026}

\textbf{Author:} Marcio Diaz (Space AI).
\href{https://github.com/ajt60gaibb/OpenProblemsInNLA/blob/main/references/diaz-mi20-2026-11-27/mi20_subquadratic_lee_whitepaper.pdf}{Preprint
PDF} ·
\href{https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.24591861}{Zenodo DOI
10.5281/zenodo.24591861} ·
\href{https://github.com/ajt60gaibb/OpenProblemsInNLA/blob/main/references/diaz-mi20-2026-11-27/README.md}{Submission
and reproduction scripts}.

The manuscript proves four structural results for every \(1 < p < 2\)
and every \(m \ge 2\): (i) the finite-dimensional optimal constants form
a strictly increasing infinite sequence
\(1 = C_{p,m}(1) < C_{p,m}(2) < \cdots < C_{p,m}(n) < C_{p,m}(n+1) < \cdots < C_{p,m}(\infty)\)
over both \(\mathbb{C}\) and \(\mathbb{R}\) (Theorem~1, settling Qiu's
dimensional-growth conjecture and transferring via Xing Li's Theorem~1.1
to concave-function Lee constants in Corollary~1); (ii) on any
infinite-dimensional separable Hilbert space \(\mathcal{H}\), no tuple
in \((\mathcal{S}_p(\mathcal{H}))^m\) containing any finite-rank
operator can attain \(C_{p,m}(\infty)\) (Theorem~2); (iii) universal
\(2\times 2\) collapse
\(C_{p,m}(2) = C_{p,m}^{\mathbb{R}}(2) = C_{p,2}(2)\) for all
\(m \ge 2\) and all \(p > 1\), with exact closed form
\(C_{3/2,m}(2) = ((107 + 51\sqrt{17})/256)^{1/6}\) and monic cubic
evaluation \(z^3 - z^2 - 4z + 2 = 0\) at \(p = 4/3\) giving
\(C_{4/3,m}(2) \approx 1.0183023754\) (Theorem~3); and (iv) explicit
small-integer \(2\times 3\) pairs at \(p \in \{3/2, 4/3, 5/4\}\)
strictly surpassing \(C_{p,m}(2)\), verified without floating-point
arithmetic by pure Python standard-library rational certificates
(Theorem~4). Proposition~5 shows that kernel lifting extends to all
\(p \ge 2\), forcing \(\ker(S) \cap \operatorname{ran}(H) \neq \{0\}\)
at every superquadratic finite-dimensional maximizer and pinpointing
Lemma~2 as the mechanism of the \(p = 2\) phase transition.

Status remains \textbf{Open}: these theorems resolve finite-dimensional
and finite-rank non-attainment but do not determine an explicit formula
for \(C_p(m) = C_{p,m}(\infty)\) across \(1 < p < 2\) and \(m \ge 2\).
Author submission with exact zero-float rational and symbolic
verification scripts and informal AI-assisted proof audit; no external
human peer review or Lean formalization was performed.

\subsection{Problem statement}\label{problem-statement}

For a complex square matrix \(X\), write \(|X|=(X^*X)^{1/2}\) and
\(\|X\|_p=(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits|X|^p)^{1/p}\). For every
integer \(m\ge2\) and real \(1< p<2\), determine exactly the
dimension-independent constant

\[C_p(m)=\sup_{n\ge1}\ \sup_{A_1,\ldots,A_m\in\mathbb C^{n\times n},\,\sum_j|A_j|\ne0}
\frac{\|\sum_{j=1}^m A_j\|_p}{\|\sum_{j=1}^m|A_j|\|_p}.\]

Thus the requested answer is the optimal constant, as a function of both
\(p\) and \(m\), in the corresponding Schatten norm inequality. An upper
bound alone does not settle the problem. All matrix orders are included
in the same supremum.

These constants quantify the loss when replacing a matrix sum by the sum
of its positive semidefinite moduli, an operation common in norm
estimates for matrix computations. This is distinct from constants
comparing the arithmetic and symmetric moduli of one matrix (catalog
MI-09).
