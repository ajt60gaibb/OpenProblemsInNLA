## Exact target match

RA-08 asks about every ordered PSD pair, every continuous nonnegative nondecreasing concave function, and the source's fixed eigenvalue-truncation convention. The manuscript supplies an admissible instance with `n=6`, `k=3`, `f(x)=min(x,1)`, and `rank(Ahat)=3`. Consequently `Ahat_k=Ahat` and, because `f(0)=0`, `f(Ahat)_k=f(Ahat)`. These equalities dispose of the potentially significant distinction between applying a function to an approximation and truncating its function. Choices inside repeated eigenspaces do not change either equality. The failure at input excess zero directly negates the entire universal implication in RA-08. No restriction in that canonical statement excludes a piecewise linear function or an exact Nyström approximation.

The cited primary paper identifies the ordered concave spectral case as open and distinguishes it from the operator-monotone class. Its discussion of constant loss is broader than the canonical nuclear specialization. I checked Table 1 and the preceding constant-loss discussion directly in [Persson–Meyer–Musco, arXiv:2311.14023v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.14023v2).

**RA-10 is not resolved.** Its function must be operator monotone. The growing nuclear example uses the larger scalar-concave class. In fact, `min(x,1)` is not operator monotone: its divided-difference matrix at one point below 1 and one point above 1 has the form `[[1,c],[c,0]]`, with `c>0`, hence negative determinant. Applying the derivative to the PSD all-ones rank-one perturbation produces an indefinite derivative; sufficiently small positive perturbations therefore violate matrix monotonicity. Being ordered does not repair this failed function hypothesis. The last section of the manuscript correctly discloses this distinction.

## Proof audit

### Derivative lemma

PASS. On separate open neighborhoods of the spectral clusters below and above 1, use respectively the analytic functions `z` and `1`. The contour formula then gives the divided differences, including repeated eigenvalues, and the fixed-contour second resolvent expansion gives a quadratic remainder. The needed constants may depend on dimension and on the distance to 1. All applications keep dimension fixed before taking the perturbation small, so the proof never requires a dimension-uniform differentiability estimate.

Minor presentation clarification, not a gap in any application: if the lemma is read for arbitrary symmetric perturbations at a zero eigenvalue, the displayed function must be locally extended by `f(z)=z` below the lower spectral cluster, or the statement restricted to perturbations that keep the matrix PSD. The proof already constructs this local analytic extension, and the actual perturbations are PSD for positive parameter.

### Finite rational spectral construction

PASS, with the following independently checked steps.

1. The two columns of `U` have squared integer lengths 81 and dot product zero. Hence `U^T U=I_2`, `E=UU^T` is a rank-two projector, and the first five coordinates of `F` equal the projector generated by `[8U; I_2]/sqrt(65)`. The last coordinate is an orthogonal unit projector. Thus `F` is PSD, has rank three, and has spectral norm one.
2. Direct exact rational multiplication gives `F Omega=0`; the upper identity block gives full column rank of `Omega`. Therefore `A Omega=[B;0]`, `Omega^T A Omega=B`, and the Nyström formula gives exactly `Ahat`. Replacing the sketch by any orthonormal basis of its range preserves this formula by invertible change of coordinates.
3. The ranges of the two PSD summands span all six coordinates. Equivalently, a vector simultaneously annihilated by `Ahat` and `F` must vanish first on the top three coordinates, then on the next two and on the sixth. Thus `A` is positive definite.
4. The residual is exactly `tF`. The rank-three candidate bounds the optimal spectral error by `t`; the first three eigenvalues of `A` are at least `1/2`; and the separate sixth-coordinate eigenvalue is exactly `t`. There are consequently at least four eigenvalues at least `t`, so `lambda_4(A)=t`. Monotonicity of the scalar function and `t<1` give transformed optimal tail exactly `t` too. This does not assume that `Ahat` is the particular spectral truncation of `A`.
5. I independently recomputed the derivative block from `B` and `E`: it is `D=(1/729)[[585,144,224],[144,585,-28],[224,-28,0]]`, before multiplying by `64/65`. The witness numerator for `(4,3,1)` is 19705 and its squared length contributes 26, giving `v^T D v=19705/18954` exactly.
6. The circle centered at `17/16` with radius `9/256` lies wholly above 1 and is distance at least that radius from the unperturbed spectrum. The adjacent eigenvalue gap is precisely twice the radius. Since `t=1/65536<r/2` and the next eigenvalue stays below 1, the contour isolates exactly the required eigenvalue for both matrices. The normal-matrix resolvent bound and perturbation bound give `||R||<=1/r` and `||R_t||<=2/r`.
7. The exact remainder has the ordered resolvent product `t^2 R F R F R_t`. Integrating `(z-1)` gives coefficient `2(d+r)/r^2=12800/81`. This is a finite-parameter estimate, not an appeal to an unspecified asymptotic remainder.
8. Exact rational arithmetic yields `(64/65)(19705/18954)-(12800/81)/65536-1=334583/15769728>0`. A Rayleigh quotient lower-bounds the spectral norm even though the transformed difference need not be PSD. Dividing by the optimal transformed tail `t` proves the displayed strict certificate.

The input excess is exactly zero. It therefore defeats every proposed output factor `1+C epsilon` with finite `C`, without any limiting argument in epsilon.

### Nuclear derivative divergence

PASS. The displayed off-diagonal divided difference is `h_i/(h_i+h_j)=1/(1+rho^(j-i))`. Conjugation by `diag(I,-I)` extracts the off-diagonal block and is nuclear-norm contractive after averaging; the off-diagonal matrix has twice the singular-value sum of `K`, giving `||D_m||_* >= ||K_m||_*/m`.

The upper triangular orientation of `T_m` is correct for this `K_m`. The diagonal difference from `T_m-I/2` vanishes, and the two off-diagonal bands at distance `d` have equal absolute error `rho^d/(1+rho^d)`. Summing their squared entries proves the displayed Frobenius estimate. The conversion `||X||_*<=sqrt(m)||X||_F` and `||I/2||_*=m/2` give exactly the claimed constants.

The inverse of `T_m^T` is the lower bidiagonal difference matrix. Its column Gram matrix has diagonal `2,...,2,1` and off-diagonal `-1`. The recurrence with `x_0=0`, `x_{m+1}=x_m` yields angles `(2j-1)pi/(2m+1)`. Inverting square roots of its eigenvalues proves all the displayed singular values, including `m=1`. Finally `sin x<=x` and `(2m+1)/(m(2j-1))>=1/j` justify the harmonic lower bound. At `rho=1/4`, the residual constant is exactly `sqrt(2/15)`. The conclusion is unbounded growth, even though the explicit lower bound is negative at some small dimensions.

### Nuclear Nyström realization and quantifiers

PASS. The proposed vector `v` is unit length and annihilates `Omega^T`; the identity top block again makes the sketch full rank. The same range-spanning argument gives strict positive definiteness for every `t>0`. The residual has nuclear norm `t`.

For `0<t<1/4`, interlacing/order puts the leading `d` eigenvalues above `3/4`, while the final eigenvalue `a(t)` lies in `(0,t]`. The Schur complement inverse is positive definite with norm less than 2, since its minimum eigenvalue exceeds `3/4-t>1/2`. The displayed negative correction is therefore valid and gives `a(t)=(1-eta)t+O(t^2)`. With `k=d`, there is exactly one discarded eigenvalue; its transformed value is still `a(t)`. Both optimal nuclear tails are consequently equal to `a(t)`.

At fixed dimension, differentiability also holds in nuclear norm by norm equivalence. Pinching the derivative into the leading block and the final scalar gives the lower bound `eta||D_m||_*+1-eta`. Direct substitution into the excess ratio yields

`liminf epsilon_f(t)/epsilon_A(t) >= ||D_m||_*`,

because the numerator after division by `t` loses `1-eta`, and the denominator `1-a(t)/t` tends to `eta>0`. This verifies the cancellation, rather than inferring it merely from a lower bound on the output ratio.

The choice order is legitimate: choose `eta` so that `eta/(1-eta)<epsilon_0`; choose a finite dimension with derivative nuclear norm strictly above `C`; then choose sufficiently small positive `t`. Strict margins make both final inequalities hold. The exponentially small distance of `B_m` to the kink creates no quantifier gap.

### Strictly increasing extension

PASS. Adding `gamma x` replaces the leading derivative by `D_m+gamma uu^T`, with nuclear norm at least `||D_m||_*-gamma`, while the optimal transformed small tail becomes `(1+gamma)a(t)`. The claimed excess-ratio lower bound follows. For the fixed finite spectral example, both the error and optimal transformed tail vary continuously in gamma, so its strict violation survives for sufficiently small fixed positive gamma. This extension remains outside the operator-monotone hypothesis needed by RA-10.

## Independent arithmetic and diagnostic checks

Independent scratch computation used Python's `fractions.Fraction`, NumPy, and ordinary bisection; it did not import or run the submitted verification code. Exact checks of `U^T U`, `F^2=F`, `F Omega=0`, all entries of `D`, the witness, and the remainder certificate passed. Numerical evaluation gave spectral ratio approximately `1.0386889112160018`, fourth eigenvalue divided by `t` equal to 1, and minimum eigenvalue approximately `2.34744e-7`. These numerical values corroborate the proof but are not its certificate.

The triangular singular-value formula passed independent numerical checks for `m=1,2,4,10,30`. The corresponding derivative nuclear norms were approximately `0.707107, 0.804186, 0.954035, 1.202476, 1.532834`, all above the analytic lower bound. No large-dimensional finite-difference experiment was used to justify divergence.
