\textbf{Rating rationale:} Extreme because explicit superlinear tensor
lower bounds remain far below generic quadratic behavior; broad
importance is the construction of hard instances for bilinear
computation and circuit lower bounds.\\
\textbf{Topic:} explicit lower bounds for tensor decompositions

\subsection{Context and notation}\label{context-and-notation}

Over \(\mathbb C\), the tensor rank \(R(T)\) is the minimum number of
pure tensors in an exact sum for \(T\). The border rank
\(\underline R_{\mathbb C}(T)\) is the least \(r\) such that \(T\) is a
Euclidean limit of complex tensors of rank at most \(r\).

\subsection{Problem statement}\label{problem-statement}

Construct a deterministic algorithm that, on input the positive integer
\(n\), outputs the rational entries of a tensor
\(T_n\in\mathbb Q^{n\times n\times n}\) in time polynomial in \(n\), and
prove that there are constants \(c>0,n_0\) such that
\(\underline R_{\mathbb C}(T_n)\ge c n^2\) for all \(n\ge n_0\). Output
rationals are encoded by binary integer numerators and denominators;
thus the time bound also limits their bit lengths. Border rank is over
\(\mathbb C\), as defined in the notation.

\subsection{Why it matters}\label{why-it-matters}

Explicit hard tensors would supply lower bounds for bilinear computation
that are much stronger than the currently available examples. Generic
existence does not provide the required efficient construction.

\subsection{Progress --- explicit superlinear border
rank}\label{progress-explicit-superlinear-border-rank}

There is a polynomial-time family of \(0\)--\(1\) tensors in
\(\mathbb Q^{n\times n\times n}\) with complex border rank at least
\(\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor^{9/4}=(1-O(n^{-1/2}))n^{9/8}\). Moreover, for
every \(\varepsilon>0\), sums of rectangular multiplication blocks on
disjoint coordinates with all three total factor dimensions at most
\(n\) have border rank \(O_\varepsilon(n^{9/8+\varepsilon})\). Thus
\(9/8\) is the optimal exponent for this class of constructions.

Let \(M_{a,b,c}\) be the matrix multiplication tensor with factor
dimensions \(ab,bc,ca\), using the cyclic form

\[M_{a,b,c}=\sum_{i=1}^a\sum_{j=1}^b\sum_{k=1}^c
  e_{ij}\otimes e_{jk}\otimes e_{ki}.\]

Corollary 1.2 of OpenAI's November preprint gives
\(\underline R_{\mathbb C}(M_{q,q,q})\ge q^{9/4}\) for \(q\ge2\) and
\(\widetilde R(M_{q,q,q})=q^{9/4}\), where \(\widetilde R\) is
asymptotic rank over \(\mathbb C\). The rank-at-most-\(r\) locus is the
image of the polynomial map that sums \(r\) pure tensors. It is
constructible, so its Zariski and Euclidean closures over \(\mathbb C\)
coincide.
\href{https://github.com/openai/math/blob/92b516ef8e2abf66c3db7aa31438d3010bc7956f/preprints/The-matrix-multiplication-exponent-is-nine-fourths-November-24-2026/paper.pdf}{Preprint,
Corollary 1.2} · \href{https://carlostoledo.co/omega/}{Carlos Toledo's
formal audit, G3c and G3d}.

\subsubsection{Construction}\label{construction}

Given \(n\), set \(q=\lfloor\sqrt n\rfloor\). Identify each first
\(q^2\)-dimensional coordinate block with the pairs in
\(\{1,\ldots,q\}^2\), and put \(M_{q,q,q}\) in the product of those
three blocks. On the remaining coordinates, add
\(e_\ell\otimes e_\ell\otimes e_\ell\) for each \(q^2<\ell\le n\), using
\(e_\ell\) for the individual coordinate vectors. All other entries are
zero. To test a coefficient, decode the coordinate pairs and compare the
repeated indices in the cyclic formula, or test for a diagonal entry on
the remaining coordinates. This takes
\(O(n^3\mathop{\mathrm{polylog}}\nolimits(n+1))\) bit operations for the
dense output, with numerator--denominator pairs \((0,1)\) and \((1,1)\).

Each added diagonal term is a scalar multiplication block, so the family
belongs to the class in the upper bound below. Each matrix flattening
has rank \(n\), since its rows have nonempty disjoint supports.
Projection onto the first \(q^2\) coordinates in each factor recovers
\(M_{q,q,q}\). Linear maps on the factors preserve limits and send a
rank-one term to a term of rank at most one. This proves the bound,
including \(q=1\), when the square block has rank one.

\subsubsection{Upper bound for rectangular block
sums}\label{upper-bound-for-rectangular-block-sums}

Fix \(\varepsilon>0\). There is a power \(d=2^h\), \(h\ge1\), with
\(R(M_{d,d,d})\le d^{9/4+2\varepsilon}\). The \(h\)-th Kronecker power
of \(M_{2,2,2}\) is \(M_{2^h,2^h,2^h}\) up to reindexing, and its rank
to the power \(1/h\) tends to \(2^{9/4}\). For any integer \(c\ge1\),
take the least \(j\ge0\) with \(d^j\ge c\). Restriction from size
\(d^j\) and submultiplicativity of rank under Kronecker products give
\(R(M_{c,c,c})\le K_\varepsilon c^{9/4+2\varepsilon}\), with
\(K_\varepsilon=d^{9/4+2\varepsilon}\).

Permuting the factors and, when needed, reversing the coordinate pairs
in the bases lets us assume \(a\ge b\ge c\). Partition the first two
index sets into groups of size at most \(c\). Each pair of groups
contributes a restriction of one copy of \(M_{c,c,c}\), so

\[R(M_{a,b,c})
\le \lceil a/c\rceil\lceil b/c\rceil R(M_{c,c,c})
\le 4K_\varepsilon ab\,c^{1/4+2\varepsilon}
\le 4K_\varepsilon (ab)^{9/8+\varepsilon}.\]

The last inequality uses \(c^2\le ab\). For a direct sum of such blocks,
let \(s_j\) be the largest factor dimension of block \(j\). The three
aggregate dimension bounds imply \(\sum_j s_j\le3n\). Summing the rank
bounds gives at most \(4K_\varepsilon(3n)^{9/8+\varepsilon}\) since the
exponent is greater than one. Factorwise linear images of these sums in
the \(n\times n\times n\) format inherit the bound. Their limits also
inherit it because each border-rank sublevel set is closed.

The original target is still a quadratic lower bound for a
polynomial-time rational family.

\subsection{References and status}\label{references-and-status}

M. Michałek and J. Landsberg,
\href{https://www.theoryofcomputing.org/articles/v021a013/v021a013.pdf}{\emph{Towards
Finding Hay in a Haystack: Explicit Tensors of Border Rank Greater Than
2.02m}}, Theory of Computing 21(13), 2025, §1 and Definition 1.1,
distinguishes polynomial-time explicitness from weaker notions and
obtains a linear bound. The quadratic target and rational-output
encoding are an editorial quantitative formulation of the paper's
explicit-versus-generic challenge, not a numbered conjecture quoted from
it.

Benjamin Lovitz's October preprint gives a deterministic polynomial-time
integer construction with border rank at least \(3n-11n^{3/4}\).
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/2610.08504v1}{Preprint, Theorem 1.1}.
