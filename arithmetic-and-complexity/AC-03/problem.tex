\subsection{Problem statement}\label{problem-statement}

In \(\mathbb C^9\otimes\mathbb C^9\otimes\mathbb C^9\), let

\[M_3=\sum_{i,j,k=1}^{3}e_{ij}\otimes e_{jk}\otimes e_{ki}.\]

The tensor rank \(R(T)\) is the minimum number of pure tensors in an
exact sum for \(T\). The border rank \(\underline R(T)\) is the least
\(r\) such that \(T\) is a Euclidean limit of tensors of rank at most
\(r\). Determine \(\underline R(M_3)\). The use of limits makes this a
different invariant from
\href{https://github.com/ajt60gaibb/OpenProblemsInNLA/blob/main/arithmetic-and-complexity/AC-02/README.md}{AC-02}.

\subsection{Why it matters}\label{why-it-matters}

Degenerating bilinear algorithms underlie asymptotic improvements in
matrix multiplication.

\subsection{References and status}\label{references-and-status}

A. Conner, A. Harper, J. M. Landsberg,
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.07981}{\emph{New lower bounds for
matrix multiplication and det\textsubscript{3}}}, Theorem 1.1, proves
\(\underline R(M_3)\ge17\). Schönhage's 21-term degeneration gives
\(\underline R(M_3)\le21\) as detailed below. J. Alman and B. Li,
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.21738}{\emph{Asymptotic Rank Speedup
Theorems, Revisited}} (2026), §1, identifies the exact border rank as
open. The remaining values are 17, 18, 19, 20 and 21.

\subsection{Verified bounds ---
2026-11-27}\label{verified-bounds-2026-11-27}

Schönhage's Example 2.3 gives a 21-term degeneration for the full
\(3\times3\) product. This is a classical upper bound for the displayed
complex border rank. \href{https://doi.org/10.1137/0210032}{Schönhage,
\emph{Partial and Total Matrix Multiplication}} ·
\href{https://people.tamu.edu/~jml/msurvey0407.pdf}{Landsberg's survey,
§8.1}.

Write \(x_i=a_{i1},\ y_i=a_{i2},\ z_i=a_{i3}\) and
\(u_i=b_{1i},\ v_i=b_{2i},\ w_i=b_{3i}\). For the third matrix
\(C=(c_{ij})\), the sum of products can be grouped as

\[\begin{aligned}
F(t)={}&\sum_i(x_i+t^2y_i)(v_i+t^2u_i)c_{ii}
 -\sum_i x_i(v_i+w_i)\sum_j c_{ij}\\
 &+\sum_{i\ne j}(x_i+t^2y_j)(v_i-tu_j)c_{ij}\\
 &+\sum_{i\ne j}(x_i+t^2z_j)(w_i+tu_j)(c_{ij}+tc_{ji})\\
 &+\sum_i (x_i+t^2z_i)w_i\left(c_{ii}-t\sum_{j\ne i}c_{ji}\right),
\end{aligned}\]

where all indices run from 1 to 3. The five sums consist of
\(3+3+6+6+3=21\) products of linear forms, one form from each of
\(A,B,C\). Exact coefficient expansion verifies that the constant and
linear terms vanish and the quadratic coefficient is
\(\sum_{i,j}(x_ju_i+y_jv_i+z_jw_i)c_{ij}=\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits(ABC)\).
For each \(t\ne0\), the tensor of \(t^{-2}F(t)\) has rank at most 21.
Its limit at zero is the tensor of
\(\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits(ABC)\), which is \(M_3\) in the
displayed bases. The index pattern in the fourth sum uses \(b_{11}\) at
\((i,j)=(3,1)\), correcting \(b_{12}\) in that factor in the thesis
transcription.
\href{https://www.maths.ed.ac.uk/sites/default/files/atoms/files/stothers.pdf}{Stothers,
\emph{On the Complexity of Matrix Multiplication}, pp.~22--23}.

The resulting interval is \(17\le\underline R(M_3)\le21\). Andreini et
al.'s November revision reports numerical searches in this range. An
exact degeneration or a lower-bound certificate would be needed to
improve one of the endpoints.
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/2602.21797v2\#S6}{Andreini et al.,
arXiv:2602.21797v2, §6}.
