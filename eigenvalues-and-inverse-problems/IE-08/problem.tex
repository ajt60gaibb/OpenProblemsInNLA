\subsection{Stronger resolution and Lean verification ---
2026-11-24}\label{stronger-resolution-and-lean-verification-2026-11-24}

\textbf{Affirmative resolution by Matthew J. Colbrook}, Department of
Applied Mathematics and Theoretical Physics, University of Cambridge.
\textbf{Theorem 1} of
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29923}{\emph{Schur decomposition with
logarithmic precision in cubic time up to logarithmic factors}} gives
the stronger bound

\[O(n^3\log^2(n/(\delta p)))\quad\text{arithmetic operations and}\quad
O(\log(n/(\delta p)))\quad\text{mantissa bits},\]

for every requested failure probability \(0< p<1/2\). It returns an
exactly upper triangular \(T\) on every execution, and both original
residual bounds hold jointly with probability at least \(1-p\). The work
bound holds on every execution. Taking \(p=0.01\) settles the original
target with \(c=2\), without any separation or diagonalizability
assumption. The original ID, statement, September resolution and
historical ratings are retained.

The complete main theorem is registered as
\href{https://palomar-registry.org/entry?id=PALOMAR-2026-11-23-000003&version=1}{Palomar
PALOMAR-2026-11-23-000003, version 1}. The evidence below supports
\textbf{Lean verified} for the full target.

\subsection{Problem statement}\label{problem-statement}

Does a randomized floating-point algorithm exist with the following
guarantee? Given \(A\in\mathbb C^{n\times n}\), \(\|A\|_2\leq1\), and
\(0<\delta<1/2\), it uses at most

\[O(n^3\log^c(n/\delta))\quad\text{arithmetic operations and}\quad
O(\log(n/\delta))\quad\text{mantissa bits}\]

and, with probability at least \(0.99\), returns an upper triangular
\(T\) and \(Q\) satisfying

\[\|A-QTQ^*\|_2\leq\delta,
\qquad \|Q^*Q-I\|_2\leq\delta?\]

The constants and exponent \(c\) must be universal, with no additional
eigenvalue-separation or diagonalizability assumption. Use the usual
relative-error floating-point model, with sufficient exponent range to
avoid overflow and underflow. This is a statement about the precision
needed for a rigorous algorithm, not whether ordinary QR implementations
usually work well.
