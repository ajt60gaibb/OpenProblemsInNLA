\textbf{Rating rationale:} Challenging reflects a sharp perturbation
threshold without geometric separation assumptions beyond a spectral
gap; community impact is the stability of spectral subspaces.

Let \(A\) be a self-adjoint, possibly unbounded operator on a separable
complex Hilbert space. Suppose \(\sigma(A)=\sigma\cup\Sigma\), where the
two nonempty closed sets satisfy
\(d=\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}\nolimits(\sigma,\Sigma)>0\). For a bounded
self-adjoint perturbation \(V\), set

\[P=E_A(\sigma),\qquad Q=E_{A+V}(O_{d/2}(\sigma)),\qquad
O_r(S)=\{x\in\mathbb R:\mathop{\mathrm{dist}}\nolimits(x,S)< r\},\]

where \(E_B\) denotes the spectral projection measure of \(B\).

Does \(\|V\|< d/2\) always imply \(\|P-Q\|<1\)? All norms are operator
norms. Equivalently, is the optimal universal constant
\(c_{\mathrm{opt}}=1/2\) for guaranteeing that the maximal angle
\(\arcsin\|P-Q\|\) is strictly below \(\pi/2\)?

The two spectral sets may interlace: no ordering or disjoint-convex-hull
hypothesis is imposed. The source's operator formulation is retained;
Hermitian matrix invariant-subspace perturbation is its
finite-dimensional setting. This problem concerns subspace orientation
after perturbation, beyond preservation of a spectral gap.

\subsection{Sharp threshold for rank-two perturbations ---
2026-11-28}\label{sharp-threshold-for-rank-two-perturbations-2026-11-28}

\textbf{Partial result (AI-derived).} The half-gap guarantee holds for
every self-adjoint perturbation of rank at most two, including
indefinite perturbations and unbounded \(A\). More generally, under
\(\|V\|< d/2\) it holds whenever

\[\|V\|_{\mathrm{HS}}<\sqrt{2}\,(d-\|V\|),\]

where \(\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\) is the Hilbert--Schmidt norm. No
placement condition on the spectral sets is required. The unrestricted
threshold remains open. The earlier rank-one result for bounded \(A\)
uses a determinant identity.
\href{https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.02796}{Gebert, Theorem 2.10}.

\textbf{Hilbert--Schmidt projection estimate.} Suppose self-adjoint
\(S,T\) differ by a bounded Hilbert--Schmidt operator \(W=T-S\). Let
\(E,F\) be their respective spectral projections onto selected
components. Assume each selected component has distance at least \(g>0\)
from the other operator's complementary component. Then

\[\|E-F\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
=\|E(I-F)\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2+\|(I-E)F\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
\le \frac{\|W\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2}{g^2}.\]

Indeed, \(E(I-F)\), regarded as an operator from
\(\mathop{\mathrm{ran}}\nolimits(I-F)\) to
\(\mathop{\mathrm{ran}}\nolimits E\), solves the weak Sylvester equation
with left operator \(S|_{\mathop{\mathrm{ran}}\nolimits E}\), right
operator \(T|_{\mathop{\mathrm{ran}}\nolimits(I-F)}\), and forcing
\(-EW(I-F)\). The analogous forcing for \((I-E)F\) is \(-(I-E)WF\). On
the Hilbert space of Hilbert--Schmidt operators the joint spectral
calculus for left and right multiplication solves each equation by
applying the multiplier \((s-t)^{-1}\) to its forcing. The joint
spectral projections on rectangles act by compression with the left and
right spectral projections. The multiplier is bounded by \(1/g\) and the
resulting operator satisfies the weak equation by testing on the two
domains. A bounded homogeneous weak solution intertwines both resolvents
and therefore both spectral measures. Disjoint left and right spectra
force that solution to vanish. This identifies the Hilbert--Schmidt
solutions with the displayed projection products. The two forcing blocks
have orthogonal left and right supports, so their squared norms sum to
at most \(\|W\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\).

\textbf{First possible right angle.} Write \(v=\|V\|\) and use the path
\(Q_t=E_{A+tV}(O_{d/2}(\sigma))\) for \(0\le t\le1\). The usual spectral
inclusion for bounded self-adjoint perturbations places the two
components within distance \(tv\) of \(\sigma,\Sigma\), respectively.
Thus the cross distances for times \(s,t\) are at least
\(d-(s+t)v\ge d-2v\). The estimate applied to \((t-s)V\) shows that
\(t\mapsto Q_t\) is continuous in Hilbert--Schmidt differences. Applied
from time zero it gives
\(\|P-Q_t\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le t\|V\|_{\mathrm{HS}}/(d-tv)<\sqrt2\).

Whenever \(\|P-Q_t\|<1\), the two directed gaps \(\|P(I-Q_t)\|\) and
\(\|(I-P)Q_t\|\) coincide. In fact,
\(P|_{\mathop{\mathrm{ran}}\nolimits Q_t}\) is invertible onto
\(\mathop{\mathrm{ran}}\nolimits P\) because both it and its adjoint are
bounded below. Its polar decomposition identifies the two squared gap
operators. Consequently, at a first time with \(\|P-Q_t\|=1\),
continuity would make both directed gaps equal to one. Their squared
Hilbert--Schmidt norms would sum to at least two, a contradiction.

For rank at most two, \(\|V\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\le\sqrt2\,v\) verifies the
sufficient condition whenever \(v< d/2\). Sharpness in this class
follows from \(A=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}\nolimits(0,d)\),
\(\sigma=\{0\}\) and \(V=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}\nolimits(d/2,-d/2)\). At
equality the open interval \(O_{d/2}(\sigma)\) excludes the eigenvalue
\(d/2\) of \(A+V\) and \(\|P-Q\|=1\).

\subsection{Status check --- 2026-09-08}\label{status-check-2026-09-08}

The 2021 generic theorem retains the sufficient constant
\(c_{\mathrm{crit}}=0.4548399\ldots\); its sharp theorem at a larger
threshold assumes extra spectral separation. The 2018 paper's solved
optimization problem determines an upper angle bound, not the
conjectured threshold. Searches for ``subspace perturbation'', ``optimal
constant'', ``1/2'', ``Seelmann'', ``conjecture'', and 2024--2026 found
no resolution. No recent explicit reaffirmation of the exact endpoint
conjecture was located; the status evidence is bounded.
