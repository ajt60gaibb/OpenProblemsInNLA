\textbf{Topic:} Hermitian eigenproblems; deterministic regularization

\textbf{Rating rationale:} Challenging reflects derandomizing eigenvalue

are historical and refer to the original question, which is resolved

\subsection{Resolution --- 16 November
2026}\label{resolution-16-november-2026}

\textbf{Resolved affirmatively.} Wenqi Zhu (University of Oxford) and
Ping Nie (University of Waterloo).

For every \(n\geq2\), every \(\delta\in(0,1/2)\) and every Hermitian
tridiagonal \(T\in\mathbb C^{n\times n}\) with \(\|T\|_2\leq1\), a
deterministic algorithm computes \(t\in[0,\delta/n]\) and returns the
real diagonal matrix \(D=tX\), where \(X=\mathrm{diag}(1,\ldots,n)\),
with

\[\|D\|_2\leq\delta,
\qquad
\mathop{\mathrm{gap}}\nolimits(T+D)>2^{-34}\frac{\delta^2}{n^{12}}
\geq2^{-34}(\delta/n)^{12},\]

using \(O(n[1+\log(n/\delta)]^3)\) arithmetic operations, comparisons
and square roots. The model of computation is exact real arithmetic and
comparisons, with unit-cost square roots of nonnegative real numbers and
complex numbers represented as pairs of real numbers; no logarithm,
floor or bit extraction is used as a primitive operation, and the
integers the algorithm needs are computed by doubling and comparisons,
at counted cost. This is Theorem 1.1, stated on p.~2 of the 28-page
paper
\href{https://github.com/erenup/tridiagonal-spectral-separation/blob/660debbcdb3bb81a1965e2c1ade82db80889abb7/paper/main.pdf}{\emph{A
Deterministic Algorithm for Eigenvalue Separation in Tridiagonal
Matrices}} at the immutable
\href{https://github.com/erenup/tridiagonal-spectral-separation/tree/660debbcdb3bb81a1965e2c1ade82db80889abb7}{revision
\textit{660debb}}, made public on 16 November 2026. It answers the
question with \(a=12\), \(c_0=2^{-34}\) and \(q=3\). The algorithm is
Algorithm 2 (p.~17), which evaluates the spectral function
\(F(t)=\sum_{j=1}^nj\lambda_j(T+tX)\) through the oracle of Lemma 4.3
(p.~19); the proof of Theorem 1.1 is completed in Section 4.3 (p.~20).

\textbf{Comparison with the target.} Theorem 1.1 assumes exactly
\(n\geq2\), \(0<\delta<1/2\) and a Hermitian tridiagonal
\(T\in\mathbb C^{n\times n}\) with \(\|T\|_2\leq1\), the quantifiers of
the problem statement below, with no further condition on the entries.
The algorithm is deterministic: each of its choices is a comparison of
computed numbers. It reads only the three diagonals; the oracle uses the
diagonal entries and the squared moduli of the off-diagonal entries,
computed from their real and imaginary parts (Appendix A, p.~27). The
output \(D=tX\) is a real diagonal matrix with \(\|D\|_2=nt\leq\delta\),
formed with \(n\) further multiplications. The paper's gap (2) (p.~2) is
the least difference of consecutive ordered eigenvalues listed with
multiplicity, which equals \(\min_{i\ne j}|\lambda_i-\lambda_j|\) in the
problem statement. Since \(\delta<1\), the bound
\(2^{-34}\delta^2n^{-12}\) is at least
\(2^{-34}(\delta/n)^{12}=c_0(\delta/n)^a\) with \(a=12\) and
\(c_0=2^{-34}\), the second inequality of (5). The operation bound holds
uniformly in \(n\), \(\delta\) and \(T\), so its implied constant serves
as \(C\), with \(q=3\). It counts exact arithmetic operations,
comparisons and unit-cost square roots of nonnegative reals, and no bits
are extracted from the inputs, as the question requires. The bound is
for this unit-cost exact-arithmetic model, in which the numbers grow:
the Graeffe coefficients of Appendix A can be exponentially large, and
their bit lengths are not charged. As the paper states (pp.~20 and 28),
no bit-complexity or finite-precision bound is claimed; the target asks
for none.

\textbf{Relation to the partial result.} Theorem 1.1 covers every input
of the target, including the subclasses of the retained partial result
below. On those subclasses the partial result gives larger gap bounds:
at least \(\delta/(n-1)\), in \(O(n\log n)\) operations, under the
weak-coupling condition of its Theorem A, and at least
\(\delta/n^3\ge(\delta/n)^3\), in \(O(n)\) operations, for the
constant-diagonal, equal-magnitude inputs of its Theorem B. The paper
cites these special classes (p.~2) and notes that they do not give the
requested construction for the unrestricted tridiagonal case.

\textbf{Proof and verification scope.} Theorem 1.1 is proved in the
written paper: the convex spectral function in Section 2 (pp.~5--8); the
local gap certificate in Section 3 (pp.~8--13), where Proposition 3.1
(p.~8) shows that a centered slope variation \(\mathcal S_h(t)<1/2\)
with \(0< h\leq1\) implies
\(\mathop{\mathrm{gap}}\nolimits(T+tX)>h^2/(2^{20}n^4)\); the selectors
and their iteration bounds in Section 4 (pp.~13--20), with Theorem 4.2
(p.~18) for Algorithm 2; and the oracle in Lemma 4.3 (p.~19) and
Appendix A (pp.~27--28). A Lean formalization at the same revision
proves only the finite and scalar core of the argument, as the single
theorem \textit{KE02.coreCertificate}, which depends only on the
standard axioms \textit{propext}, \textit{Classical.choice} and
\textit{Quot.sound}. Its
\href{https://github.com/erenup/tridiagonal-spectral-separation/blob/660debbcdb3bb81a1965e2c1ade82db80889abb7/lean/README.md}{Lean
guide} and
\href{https://github.com/erenup/tridiagonal-spectral-separation/blob/660debbcdb3bb81a1965e2c1ade82db80889abb7/lean/SCOPE.md}{formal
scope} list its nine components: inequality (34) and Lemma 4.2; the
constants of Theorem 4.2 and the run of Algorithm 2 on every reply
function meeting the requested accuracies; Lemma 4.1, one refinement of
Algorithm 1 and the constants of Theorem 4.1 and Remark 4.1; the algebra
of Appendix A, namely the continuant recurrence for real tridiagonal
matrices, the Graeffe iterates, the coefficient and ratio bounds, root
extraction by square roots and the index-wise matching; and the scalar
inequalities of Section 3 with the discrete Wronskian identity (22).
There \(F\) is an arbitrary real function, and its convexity and secant
bounds (9) are premises. Spectral theory, including Lemma 2.1, the
localization of Section 3 as matrix statements (Lemmas 3.1 and 3.2 and
Proposition 3.1), the gap conclusions, the matrix statement
\(\|D\|_2=nt\leq\delta\), the oracle of Lemma 4.3 and Appendix A as an
algorithm, the model of computation and every operation count are proved
in the text only. Theorem 1.1 as a whole is not a Lean theorem, so this
is not a Lean verification of the target. The
\href{https://github.com/erenup/tridiagonal-spectral-separation/blob/660debbcdb3bb81a1965e2c1ade82db80889abb7/VERIFICATION.md}{verification
record} also describes standard-library exact checks in
\href{https://github.com/erenup/tridiagonal-spectral-separation/blob/660debbcdb3bb81a1965e2c1ade82db80889abb7/verification/core/README.md}{verification/core},
which recompute the constants and inequality chains of Sections 2--4 and
the algebra of Appendix A on finite ranges and fixed instances and do
not establish the theorem. The numerical illustrations of Section 5,
with code and data in
\href{https://github.com/erenup/tridiagonal-spectral-separation/blob/660debbcdb3bb81a1965e2c1ade82db80889abb7/verification/numerics/README.md}{verification/numerics},
are not part of the proof.

\subsection{Partial result --- 12 September
2026}\label{partial-result-12-september-2026}

Sidney Holden (Center for Computational Biology, Flatiron Institute,
Simons Foundation) gives two deterministic constructions in
\href{https://github.com/ajt60gaibb/OpenProblemsInNLA/blob/main/references/holden-spectral-2026-09-12/KE-02/proof.md}{Theorems
A and B}. Writing \(T=\mathrm{diag}(t_i)+R\) and
\(\eta=\max_i\sum_{j\ne i}|R_{ij}|\), Theorem A covers
\(\eta\le\delta/[2(n-1)]\) with gap at least \(\delta/(n-1)\) in
\(O(n\log n)\) operations. Theorem B covers every constant-diagonal
tridiagonal input with equal off-diagonal magnitudes, allowing arbitrary
complex phases and every coupling strength, with gap at least
\(\delta/n^3\ge(\delta/n)^3\) in \(O(n)\) operations. Section 5 also
covers every order-two input. Each construction returns a real diagonal
perturbation of norm at most \(\delta\) in the original arithmetic
model.

These subclasses passed an
\href{https://github.com/ajt60gaibb/OpenProblemsInNLA/blob/main/references/holden-spectral-2026-09-12/verification/KE-02-review.md}{independent
informal Codex AI-agent audit}. The all-dimensional Toeplitz starting
class suggested in the workshop is included, supporting Partially
resolved. Arbitrary normalized Hermitian tridiagonal inputs outside
these classes remain open.
\href{https://github.com/ajt60gaibb/OpenProblemsInNLA/blob/main/references/holden-spectral-2026-09-12/README.md}{Submission
and verified affiliation}; no novelty, external human peer review or
formal verification is claimed.

\subsection{Context and notation}\label{context-and-notation}

All norms are Euclidean vector norms or their induced matrix norms.

\subsection{Problem statement}\label{problem-statement}

For a Hermitian matrix \(H\) with eigenvalues listed with multiplicity,
define

\[\mathop{\mathrm{gap}}\nolimits(H)=\min_{i\ne j}|\lambda_i(H)-\lambda_j(H)|.\]

Do universal constants \(a,c_0,C,q>0\) and a deterministic algorithm
exist with the following property? For every \(n\geq2\), Hermitian
tridiagonal \(T\in\mathbb C^{n\times n}\) with \(\|T\|_2\leq1\), and
\(\delta\in(0,1/2)\), the algorithm receives the three diagonals and
returns a real diagonal matrix \(D\) such that

\[\|D\|_2\leq\delta,
\qquad
\mathop{\mathrm{gap}}\nolimits(T+D)\geq c_0(\delta/n)^a,\]

using at most \(Cn[1+\log(n/\delta)]^q\) exact arithmetic operations and
comparisons? Square roots of nonnegative real numbers are allowed at
unit cost; extracting bits from exact real inputs is not.

This selects the explicitly proposed nearly linear version of the
deterministic Minami problem. It preserves tridiagonal structure and
requires only the perturbation as output. Its diagonal restriction and
cost target make it distinct from the general, cubic-cost regularization
question
\href{https://github.com/ajt60gaibb/OpenProblemsInNLA/blob/main/eigenvalues-and-inverse-problems/IE-07/README.md}{IE-07}.

\subsection{Earlier status check ---
2026-09-08}\label{earlier-status-check-2026-09-08}

Searches for \textit{deterministic Minami perturbation},
\textit{Minami deterministic eigenvalue gap algorithm 2026}, and
\textit{Minami derandomization matrix} found no construction meeting
this bound. Sobczyk's April 2025 revision gives a deterministic full
tridiagonal diagonalization in nearly quadratic time; it does not
provide the requested nearly linear diagonal perturbation. The workshop
retains Problem 3.2 in its August 2026 update. The normalization and a
bounded perturbation are stated explicitly here, making the scale of its
gap guarantee unambiguous.
