\subsection{Problem statement}\label{problem-statement}

For normal matrices \(A,B\in\mathbb C^{n\times n}\), list their
eigenvalues \(\alpha_1,\ldots,\alpha_n\) and \(\beta_1,\ldots,\beta_n\)
with algebraic multiplicity. Define the bottleneck matching distance

\[d_\infty(A,B)=\min_{\pi\in S_n}\max_{1\le j\le n}
|\alpha_j-\beta_{\pi(j)}|,\]

where \(S_n\) is the permutation group. Determine the exact value of

\[C_{\rm normal}=\sup_{n\ge2}\ \sup_{\substack{A,B\in\mathbb C^{n\times n}\ \mathrm{normal}\\ A\ne B}}
\frac{d_\infty(A,B)}{\|A-B\|_2}.\]

Here normal means \(AA^*=A^*A\), and \(\|\cdot\|_2\) is the spectral
norm. The target is a single sharp constant valid in every dimension.
Equivalently, find the smallest \(C\) for which
\(d_\infty(A,B)\le C\|A-B\|_2\) for all such pairs, with matching
sharpness evidence. The conjectured value \(C=1\) has already been
refuted; it is not the question being posed.

\subsection{Improved singular-value bound ---
2026-11-28}\label{improved-singular-value-bound-2026-11-28}

\textbf{Partial result (AI-derived).} Write \(s_1\ge s_2\ge\cdots\) for
the singular values of \(A-B\) and put \(r_n=\lfloor(n+1)/2\rfloor\).
Then

\[d_\infty(A,B)^2\le s_1^2+\frac12\sum_{i=2}^{r_n}s_i^2.\]

This halves the contribution of the smaller singular values in
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/2609.09177v2}{Tang's truncated bound,
Theorem 1.1}. For the constant restricted to order \(n\), it gives
\(C_n\le \sqrt{(r_n+1)/2}\). This improves the minimum of Tang's bound
and the classical dimension-independent bound of \(2.9038872828\) in
orders 3 through 30. The argument below also gives \(C_3\le\sqrt{7/5}\).
The exact dimension-independent constant \(C_{\rm normal}\) remains
open.

\textbf{Proof.} Let \(\delta=d_\infty(A,B)>0\) and \(m=\|A-B\|_2\). In
the bipartite graph that joins eigenvalue labels at distance less than
\(\delta\), there is no perfect matching. Hall's theorem provides two
sets of labels with combined size \(n+1\) and every cross distance at
least \(\delta\). Let \(U,V\) be their spans in orthonormal eigenbases
of \(A,B\), respectively. Swap the two matrices if necessary so that
\(\dim U=r\le \dim V\). Then \(r\le r_n\) and there is a common unit
vector \(z\in U\cap V\).

Write \(P_U,P_V\) for the orthogonal projections and put
\(C=P_U(A-B)|_V\) and \(H=\sum_{i=1}^r s_i^2\). Let \(\gamma\) be the
norm of \(P_U\) from \(V\cap z^\perp\) to \(U\cap z^\perp\). In the
chosen eigenbases the entries of \(C\) are the corresponding cross
eigenvalue differences times the entries of \(P_U|_V\). The common
direction contributes one to the squared Frobenius norm of \(P_U|_V\),
and its orthogonal part contributes at least \(\gamma^2\). Thus

\[\|C\|_F^2\ge\delta^2(1+\gamma^2).\]

Set \(a=\|(I-zz^*)Az\|_2\) and \(b=\|(I-zz^*)Bz\|_2\). The average of
\(|\alpha_i-\beta_j|^2\) with the independent weights given by the
squared coordinates of \(z\) in the two eigenbases is
\(|z^*Az-z^*Bz|^2+a^2+b^2\), at least \(\delta^2\). The inner product of
the two orthogonal components of \(Az,Bz\) has modulus at most
\(\gamma ab\). Also, projecting \((A-B)z\) onto \(U^\perp\) leaves only
the contribution from \(Bz\). It follows that

\[m^2\ge\delta^2-2\gamma ab,\qquad m^2\ge(1-\gamma^2)b^2.\]

For an orthonormal basis of \(U\) starting with \(z\), the sum of
squared norms of \((A-B)^*\) on the basis is at most \(H\) by the Ky Fan
maximum principle. Computing \(\|C^*\|_F^2\) projects each of these
vectors onto \(V\). On the first vector the projection loses squared
norm at least \((1-\gamma^2)a^2\), since \(B^*z\in V\) and normality
gives \(\|(I-zz^*)A^*z\|_2=a\). Therefore

\[H\ge\delta^2(1+\gamma^2)+(1-\gamma^2)a^2.\]

The desired estimate holds if \(\delta\le m\) since \(H\ge m^2\), and
the inequalities above cover \(\gamma=0\) and \(\gamma=1\). In the
remaining case set \(D=\delta^2-m^2>0\). The first two inequalities give
\(a^2\ge (1-\gamma^2)D^2/(4\gamma^2m^2)\). By the arithmetic--geometric
mean inequality,

\[\gamma^2m^2+\frac{(1-\gamma^2)^2D^2}{4\gamma^2m^2}
\ge (1-\gamma^2)D.\]

Substituting into the bound for \(H\) gives \(H\ge\delta^2+D\), which
proves the result with \(r\) in place of \(r_n\).

For \(n=3\) either \(r=1\), which gives \(\delta\le m\), or both
subspaces have dimension two. In the latter case project the two basis
vectors of \(V\) starting with \(z\) onto \(U\) after applying \(A-B\).
This gives the second Frobenius bound
\(2m^2\ge\delta^2(1+\gamma^2)+(1-\gamma^2)b^2\). Averaging it with the
first bound and using \(D\le 2\gamma ab\le\gamma(a^2+b^2)\) shows

\[\frac{m^2}{\delta^2}\ge
\frac{1+\gamma^2+c}{2+c},\qquad c=\frac{1-\gamma^2}{2\gamma}.\]

The right-hand side is at least \(5/7\), since the equivalent inequality
\(7\gamma^3-\gamma^2-3\gamma+1\ge0\) has minimum \(4/49\) over
\([0,1]\), attained at \(3/7\). This yields the stated order-three
bound.

\subsection{Why it matters}\label{why-it-matters}

This is a sharp conditioning question for the complete spectrum of a
normal matrix. Matching counts repeated eigenvalues, so a
Hausdorff-distance estimate that ignores multiplicity does not answer
it.

historical; the recent bound is context, not an explicit reaffirmation
