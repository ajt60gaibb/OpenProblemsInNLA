\subsection{Problem statement}\label{problem-statement}

Let \(\mathcal D_n\) consist of the real entrywise-nonnegative
\(n\times n\) matrices whose row and column sums are all one, and define
the single-eigenvalue region

\[D_n=\{z\in\mathbb C:z\in\sigma(A)\text{ for some }A\in\mathcal D_n\}.\]

Prove or disprove that, for every integer \(n\geq1\) and every
\(z\in\partial D_n\), there exist \(n\times n\) permutation matrices
\(P,Q\) and \(t\in[0,1]\) such that

\[\det\bigl(zI-tP-(1-t)Q\bigr)=0.\]

The boundary is taken in the usual topology of \(\mathbb C\), and
\(P=Q\) is allowed. The task concerns one eigenvalue, not simultaneous
realization of a prescribed full spectrum. It would reduce boundary
computation to a finite collection of one-parameter matrix families.
Counterexamples for convex hulls of other matrix groups do not
automatically apply to all permutation matrices.

\subsection{Orders covered by the November 2026
preprint}\label{orders-covered-by-the-november-2026-preprint}

Verbeken and Ginis's revised preprint proves results that imply the
boundary conjecture for \(1\le n\le 6\) and \(n\ge 450\). The remaining
orders are \(7\le n\le 449\).
\href{https://arxiv.org/html/2611.30905v2}{\emph{Eigenvalues of doubly
stochastic matrices in large orders}, arXiv:2611.30905v2}.

Write \(\Pi_k\) for the convex hull of the \(k\)th roots of unity and
\(U_n=\bigcup_{k=1}^n\Pi_k\). The preprint recalls \(D_n=U_n\) for
\(n\le 4\) in §1 and proves this equality for \(n=6\) and \(n\ge 450\).
If \(z\in\partial U_n\), closedness places \(z\) in one of the
constituent polygons, and \(z\) cannot be in that polygon's interior.
Thus it is a vertex or lies on a segment joining two roots of unity of
some order \(k\le n\). Two powers of a \(k\)-cycle, extended by \(n-k\)
fixed points, have those two roots as eigenvalues on the same Fourier
eigenvector. Their convex combinations therefore realize the segment.
This also covers the point and segment for \(k=1,2\).

Appendix B describes the boundary at order five. Besides the polygonal
parts, it consists of a quartic arc and its conjugate. The matrix family
\(A(t)\) in §B.1 attains the arc and is \(tP+(1-t)Q\). With permutations
specified by the column of the one in each row, the lists for \(P\) and
\(Q\) are \((2,3,1,5,4)\) and \((2,3,4,1,5)\), respectively. The same
real matrices attain the conjugate arc.
\href{https://arxiv.org/src/2611.30905v2/anc/small-orders/order5/proof.pdf}{Order-five
supplement, Corollary 1.2}.

In the radial description in Appendix B, each strict contraction of a
nonreal arc eigenvalue is an interior point. For \(0< s<1\), the
positive matrix \(sA(t)+(1-s)ee^T/5\) realizes the contracted eigenvalue
on the same sum-zero eigenvector. The real and imaginary parts of that
eigenvector span an invariant real plane perpendicular to \(e\). A small
real perturbation on this plane, extended by zero on its orthogonal
complement, preserves both row and column sums and keeps the matrix
positive. Varying the two parts of the eigenvalue on this plane gives an
open neighborhood. Real eigenvalues are already covered by \(U_5\).

Theorem 5 proves \(D_n=U_n\) analytically for \(n\ge 10^{30}\). Theorem
6 and Appendix A lower the threshold to 450 by a computer-assisted
proof. The order-five and order-six results in Appendix B also use
computer-assisted certificates.
