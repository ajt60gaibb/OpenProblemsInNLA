## Proof path and attempted failure cases

1. **Normalization.** `ColumnNormalization`, `FactorNormalization`, and `LowRankFactorObstruction` correctly handle zero columns. A zero mass implies the entire nonnegative column is zero. At least one positive-mass column is derived from product rank three, including the contradiction when the inner index type is empty. Inactive columns are filled with an existing normalized column, so their added convex weight is zero and their span does not enlarge. The actual weights are `columnMass W r * H r j / columnMass M j`; positivity of every denominator is proved from the sign pattern. Normalization preserves the product's rank and bounds the left-factor rank. No normalization hypothesis escapes into C10 or the final results.

2. **Actual affine section.** `FiniteSection` proves finiteness of the actual extreme points through positive coefficient supports, then uses compactness and Mathlib's Krein–Milman theorem to obtain their exact convex-hull representation. The argument includes empty sections, repeated generators, and nonminimal convex representations. `SectionContact` enumerates these actual extreme points, not a surrogate vertex list.

3. **Contact count.** `SectionLinearAlgebra` proves every principal three-minor with the sign pattern is positive. Rank three then makes those three coordinates injective on the column span; an affine-span vector cannot have three zero coordinates because its coordinate sum is one. `IncidenceCount` proves the weighted bipartite count internally: positive left degree, right degree at most two, and an isolated right neighbor for each degree-one left vertex. The degree-one condition is derived from the actual nonnegative product and strict off-diagonal entries. This supplies C09 without assuming an unformalized polygon contact theorem.

4. **Section upper bound.** For outer rank at most three, span equality puts the whole outer hull in the column plane, yielding at most `k` extreme points. For outer rank four, `RankFourHeight` constructs the actual separating linear functional from subspace dimensions. `SliceRecombination` and `FiniteHeightSlice` prove the entire slice equals the convex hull of zero-height columns and crossings of opposite-height columns. Both zero balanced mass and positive mass are handled, including empty or one-sided families. Counting gives `z + p*q <= 8` except the actual 3+3 split; repeated crossings make that exceptional case smaller immediately.

5. **Distinct nine-grid exception.** This exception is genuinely discharged. `CrossingRectangles` constructs a common point in each pair of open diagonals with positive reciprocal-height weights. The rank-three normalized column span is parametrized by an injective affine copy of the real plane. `PlaneSeparation` chooses a shear outside a finite bad set; the strict minimum becomes the origin, with positive first coordinate on all other points. Actual extremality forces distinct slopes and positive orientation for increasing constructed keys. The crossing/orientation lemmas imply odd comparison parity for every rectangle. `RectangleParity` combines the nine concrete rectangle equations to contradiction with exact integer arithmetic. No cyclic ordering, planar embedding, no-collinearity condition, or parity array is postulated at the application site. Coincident crossings were already handled separately. 6. **Rank contradiction.** `matrix_sylvester_rank_bound` applies rank-nullity to the actual map induced by W restricted to the range of H, with an explicitly injective map from its kernel into the kernel of W. This proves the required inequality and forces at least one factor rank to be at most four. It is not a finite enumeration of plausible ranks standing in for Sylvester's inequality.
