# TR-26 exact algebraic target

`ORIGINAL.md` is the byte-identical permanent canonical README. This is a pre-implementation statement specification, not a proof of the two degree formulas.

For **every** integer `d≥2`, take the fixed rational normal curve in complex projective `d`-space given by the homogeneous Veronese coordinates `[a^d:a^(d−1)b:…:b^d]`, including the point at infinity. The parameter space is the projective space of **all nonzero complex symmetric (d+1)×(d+1) matrices**, modulo scalar. The bilinear quadratic forms use `zᵀHz` and `zᵀz` with **no complex conjugation**, and the coordinate embedding has **no binomial or Frobenius weights**.

The rational function `ρ_H=zᵀHz/(zᵀz)` is defined on the nonisotropic part of the curve. Form the incidence variety `𝓡` as the exact Zariski closure in the product of curve and projective matrix space of pairs where the curve point is critical for `ρ_H`. Criticality means vanishing of the differential along the curve, not stationarity in all ambient coordinate directions. Its closure may include points where the quotient is undefined; do not drop or arbitrarily include those points by division-by-zero conventions.

Choose homogeneous generators of the **full defining ideal** of `𝓡`; form their Jacobian in the `d+1` curve-coordinate variables `z_j`. The ramification locus `𝓑` consists of incidence pairs with Jacobian rank at most `d−1`, as stated. Avoid replacing the ideal with a set of convenient equations if the replacement changes the Jacobian rank or scheme structure. Define `Q` by the bilinear equation `zᵀz=0`. The isotropic set is the matrix-factor projection `Σ_iso=π(𝓑∩Q)`; the nonisotropic set is `Σ_noniso=ZariskiClosure(π(𝓑\Q))`. The source writes no additional closure around `Σ_iso`; a formalization may prove it closed or state the needed hypersurface property with its exact projected set. Both parts use reduced algebraic-set degree, counting all components and with no multiplicity from an eliminant.

`Target` asserts, simultaneously and for every `d≥2`, that both sets are projective hypersurfaces and `deg Σ_noniso=6(d−1)` and `deg Σ_iso=2d`. In particular `d=2` gives degrees six and four. The known total `2(4d−3)` is context, not a replacement for either part. The two formulas are exact natural-number equalities; no probability, numerical tolerance, asymptotic estimate, or generic-metric qualifier is present. The target must retain the complete fixed embedding and bilinear model even if an implementation uses a coordinate/elimination description equivalent to the geometric one.

A Lean boundary needs genuine projective algebraic sets, incidence closure, Jacobian-rank locus, projections, and reduced degree (or explicitly proved equivalent concrete polynomial constructions). Opaque predicates such as `IsRamification` or `Degree` with no mathematical definition would make the theorem vacuous and are not an acceptable target.
