## Original mathematical target

For every integer `n ≥ 1` and every ordered input `λ : Fin n → ℝ` with `λ i ≥ 0` for all `i`, let `D = diag(λ)` be a complex diagonal matrix. Let `U(n) = {U : Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) ℂ | Uᴴ U = I}`, where `ᴴ` is conjugate transpose. For any complex square matrix `A`, define

```text
per(A) = ∑ σ : Equiv.Perm (Fin n), ∏ i : Fin n, A(i, σ(i)).
```

On the positive-semidefinite Hermitian orbit `A = Uᴴ D U`, this value is real and nonnegative. The maximum exists by compactness. The requested result must determine its exact real value uniformly from `λ`, including repeated eigenvalues, zero eigenvalues and `n = 1`. There is no normalization such as `∑ λ = 1`, no distinct-spectrum or positive-definite premise, and no assumption that equal diagonal entries characterize a maximizer.

In Lean, the final proposition should assert that the explicit spectral selector below is attained by **some** unitary and is an upper bound for **every** unitary. It should also account for the imaginary part of `per(Uᴴ D U)` being zero, or use a proven real-valued permanent definition for this orbit. Merely defining `M(λ)` as `sup U, per(Uᴴ D U)` and asserting that `M` exists is insufficient. An uninterpreted `spectralAnswer`, critical-value oracle, moment oracle, or free proposition parameter would likewise lose the exact-value target.

## Finite critical-value polynomial

Work over the exact coefficient field `F = ℚ(λ₁,…,λₙ)`, the smallest subfield of `ℝ` containing the rational numbers and the actual input entries. Specialize the spectrum **before** forming the ideal. Let

```text
qλ(t) = ∏ θ in the finite set of distinct values of λ, (t − θ).
```

There is one factor per distinct value, even when the input list repeats it; zero is treated like every other eigenvalue. In the polynomial ring `F[aᵢⱼ for i,j : Fin n, z]`, let `A = (aᵢⱼ)`. Define `C(A)ᵢⱼ` as the permanent of the matrix obtained by deleting row `j` and column `i` of `A`. Thus `C` has the transposed deleted-minor indices needed for `d(per)_A(E) = tr(C(A) E)`. Define `Iλ` to be the ideal generated by **all** of the following:

```text
tr(A^k) − ∑ i, (λ i)^k,                    1 ≤ k ≤ n;
each entry of qλ(A);
each entry of A C(A) − C(A) A;
z − per(A).
```

These are equations on the entire complex similarity orbit and its permanent critical locus. The trace equations alone are insufficient at repeated eigenvalues; `qλ(A) = 0` excludes Jordan blocks. The commutator equation is a necessary stationarity condition, not an assumption that every complex critical point is Hermitian-feasible.

The elimination ideal `Iλ ∩ F[z]` is nonzero and proper. Take its unique monic generator and then its monic square-free part; call the result `Pλ(z)`. Its degree is positive. A lexicographic Gröbner basis with every matrix-entry variable before `z` is the source's finite way to compute this same polynomial. A Lean definition may characterize the canonical generator algebraically rather than execute Gröbner elimination during elaboration, provided the ideal and the generator are explicit, their existence and uniqueness are included in the target or separately proved, and no arbitrary polynomial can be chosen to favor the desired answer. The polynomial is formed over `F`, then its roots are interpreted in `ℝ`.

## Completely finite spectral moment

For every positive natural `p`, set `m = n p` and arrange the `m` tensor positions as `(a,i)` with `a : Fin p`, `i : Fin n`. Let `H = (Sₙ)^p` permute within each row `a` and `K = (Sₚ)^n` permute within each column `i`; both embed in `Sₘ` through this fixed position bijection. For every partition `ν ⊢ m` with `length(ν) ≤ n`, let `χ^ν` be the ordinary irreducible character of `Sₘ`, `f^ν = χ^ν(e)`, and `s_ν` the Schur polynomial. Define the **finite rational** weight and **finite real polynomial** moment by

```text
w(n,p,ν) = f^ν / m! · ∑ h∈H ∑ k∈K χ^ν(kh),
T(n,p,λ) = ∑ ν⊢m, length(ν)≤n,
                 w(n,p,ν) · s_ν(λ₁,…,λₙ) / s_ν(1,…,1).
```

The denominator `s_ν(1ⁿ)` is a strictly positive integer; weights are nonnegative rationals summing to one. The character is evaluated at the **product `k h`** with the specified embeddings. Every sum is finite, including for `p` much larger than `n`. `s_ν(λ)` can be defined without dividing by eigenvalue gaps by the Jacobi–Trudi determinant: `h₀ = 1`, `h_j = 0` for `j < 0`, `∑_{j≥0} h_j(λ)t^j = ∏_i (1−λ_i t)⁻¹`, and `s_ν(λ) = det(h_{ν_i−i+j}(λ))`. Hook-length and Murnaghan–Nakayama formulas give finite dimensions and characters. The source proves `T(n,p,λ) = ∫_{U(n)} per(Uᴴ D U)^p dU`, but **the expression used by the selector is the finite character/Schur sum**, not an unevaluated Haar integral or an `Lᵖ` limit.

The eventual Lean statement must bind these mathematical ingredients to concrete finite combinatorial definitions or vetted pinned library definitions. Treating `T` as a parameter with a moment equality assumption would omit the source's exact spectral answer. Proving the moment equality belongs to the later proof, not to a premise supplied to the target.

## Finite root selector and exact answer

If every input eigenvalue is zero, define `Answer(n,λ) = 0` and do not form a positive-gap branch. Otherwise set `L = max_i λ_i` and `B = L^n > 0`. List **without multiplicity**, in strict increasing order, every real root of `Pλ` in the closed interval `[0,B]`:

```text
r₁ < r₂ < ⋯ < rₛ.
```

The list is finite and nonempty; the actual orbit maximum is among its entries. If `s = 1`, return `r₁` without any high-order moment. If `s ≥ 2`, define the positive minimum adjacent gap and finite integer order exactly as in the source:

```text
ρ = min {rⱼ₊₁ − rⱼ | 1 ≤ j < s},
b = min {j : ℕ | 2^j ≥ 1 + 16 n² B / ρ},
h = ceil(4 B / ρ),
p = 2 n² b h.
```

Here `ceil` is the least natural integer at least its positive real argument, `b` is the least natural satisfying the displayed weak inequality, and `p > 0`. All operations use exact ordered-real arithmetic and comparisons. The selector is

```text
Answer(n,λ) = r₁ + ∑_{1≤j<s} (rⱼ₊₁ − rⱼ) · 1[T(n,p,λ) > rⱼ^p].
```

The strict `>` is essential. Equivalently, the answer is the **first** root whose `p`th power is at least `T(n,p,λ)`. The root list may contain real complex-critical values that do not occur on the Hermitian unitary orbit; these cannot simply be maximized. The moment selector is precisely what chooses the orbit maximum among those candidates. No `max` over matrices, feasibility test on critical roots, unspecified limit, or infinite-precision numerical approximation remains inside `Answer`.

The exact target is, for all specified `n,λ`, that `Answer` and its defining branches exist and

```text
(∃ U₀ ∈ U(n), per(U₀ᴴ D U₀) = (Answer(n,λ) : ℂ))
∧ (∀ U ∈ U(n), Im(per(Uᴴ D U)) = 0
                   ∧ Re(per(Uᴴ D U)) ≤ Answer(n,λ)).
```

The mathematical assertion is equivalent to `Mₙ(λ) = Answer(n,λ)`. A formal implementation may package well-definedness in separate concrete helper results, but must not assume critical-value finiteness, nonempty root list, moment identity, or selector correctness as hypotheses of the main statement. The existential optimizer is included to preclude a mere upper bound.

## Endpoint and computational checks

The zero spectrum returns `0`. A scalar spectrum `(β,…,β)` returns `β^n`. For `n=1`, this is the sole value `λ₁`. For `n=2`, the source gives `Pλ(z) = (z−αβ)(z−(α²+β²)/2)` when `α≠β` and returns `(α²+β²)/2`; for `(α,β)=(1,3)`, the candidates are `3,5`, the first moment is `13/3`, and the answer is `5`. These are review controls, not replacements for arbitrary `n,λ`. Rank-one spectra, repeated positive eigenvalues and mixed zero/repeated spectra stay in scope. The source's exceptional-spectrum two-candidate formula is an optional further statement, not the all-spectrum target.

The moment order can be enormous. This is an exact finite mathematical prescription, **not** a claim of efficient general evaluation. Exact arbitrary real inputs are treated as ordered-field data; rational or algebraic inputs admit effective exact arithmetic. Reference code accepting only rational inputs, resource guards, and the stopped `(0,1,2)` order-three elimination trial do not restrict the theorem.
