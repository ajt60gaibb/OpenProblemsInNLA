## Independent analytic checks

1. **Trimming and integrality.** Interchanging the finite row-set minimum and compact directional minimum is valid, including rank-deficient rectangular maps. The variational singular value is zero when the map has a kernel. The threshold identity has slope `k/m-F_m(t)` and attains the sum of the k smallest observations. It remains valid at ties and when `k=0`. The population selector formulation has the claimed coupling Lipschitz bound because conditional expectation reduces a joint selector to a selector of either marginal without changing its constraint or objective.

2. **Pointwise concentration.** For `Y=n u_1^2`, the theta-quantile `b` satisfies `b(1-theta)<=E Y=1`. The two Hoeffding applications have total failure at most `4 exp(-2m epsilon^2)`. Markov and a third Hoeffding application ensure that at least k observations are at most `L=2/(1-theta)`, with the claimed additional failure bound. The count discrepancy is at most `m epsilon+1`. In either direction of replacement, all added or removed terms are at most L. Adding the truncated-sum error gives at most `2L epsilon+L/m`. For `k=0`, `h_{theta,n}<=theta<1/m` supplies the separate elementary bound. There is no hidden dependence on n in these constants.

3. **Covariance constants and uniformity.** The stated spherical moment formula is exact. The bound on centered moments follows, for example, from `|Y-1|^r<=2^(r-1)(Y^r+1)`. For `|lambda|<=1/8`, the terms of order at least two in the exponential series sum to at most `16 lambda^2/(1-4|lambda|)<=32 lambda^2`. Chernoff with `lambda=u/64` gives `2 exp(-m u^2/128)`. Applying this at `u=t/2` on a 1/4-net gives precisely `2*9^n exp(-m t^2/512)`. Every retained covariance matrix is bounded above by C, so each quadratic form, and then their minimum, has Lipschitz constant `2(1+t)` on the sphere. This justifies using the deterministic delta-net and a union bound, without asserting independence between different directions.

4. **Gaussian comparison and all aspect-ratio sequences.** In the independent radius-direction coupling, `G_1^2=(R^2/n)Y`, so `E|Y-G_1^2|=E|1-R^2/n|<=sqrt(2/n)`. The covariance event implies both `|(n/m)||A||^2-1|<=t` and a denominator at least `1-t`. This gives exactly the displayed ratio error. With `Q=m/n` and `t=epsilon=delta=32 sqrt(log(Q)/Q)`, the first failure exponent is `n log 9-2n log Q`; the second is `n log(1+2/delta)-2048n log Q`. Both tend to minus infinity for every sequence in the canonical statement. Parameter restrictions hold eventually for each fixed theta. Thus no extra assumption such as `m/(n log n)` tending to infinity has entered the proof. The error stated in Section 5 is valid on an event whose explicitly bounded probability tends to one; it is not an almost-sure assertion.

5. **Arbitrary matrices and correlated projections.** Removing the r largest eigenvectors of `A^T A` leaves `||B||^2<=m/(r+1)` by the trace m. Projected row norms are at most one. For each projected Gaussian coordinate, variance at most one makes `E(t-Y_i)_+` at least its standard-Gaussian value; combined with `k/m<=theta` this yields `E Psi_t<=h_theta`. The gradient is `m^-1 B^T v` up to sign, with each `|v_i|<=2 sqrt(t)`. Gaussian Poincare therefore gives `Var(Psi_t)<=4t/(r+1)` despite arbitrary dependence between rows. The bounded derivative makes the function globally Lipschitz, so the indicated smooth approximation is legitimate. No independence of projected rows is assumed.

6. **A single simultaneous successful direction.** A grid of size at most `L/delta+2`, Chebyshev, and the 1-Lipschitz dependence on t yield the stated grid failure and `sup_[0,L] Psi_t<=h_theta+2delta`. For `Z=||Bg||^2/m`, the trace formula gives `Var Z<=2/(r+1)` and `E Z<=1`, hence `P(Z>2)<=2/(r+1)`. If Z is at most 2 and k is positive, then `(m-k+1)Y_(k)<=2m`, so `Y_(k)<=L`; a maximizer of the trimming identity lies in the grid interval. The chi-square norm bound contributes exactly `2/((n-r)delta^2)`. A sum strictly below one guarantees a realization satisfying all three requirements, including positive norm. Normalization gives the boxed bound with factor `n/((n-r)(1-delta))`.

7. **Uniform constants and sharpness quantifiers.** With `r=floor(n^(2/3))` and `delta=n^(-1/6)`, the grid failure is `O_theta(n^(-1/6))`; all other failures also vanish. The normalization factor is `1+O(n^(-1/6))`, giving the asserted squared bound `h_theta+O_theta(n^(-1/6))` for all m and all unit-row A once n is large. Since h_theta is strictly positive for fixed theta, taking square roots gives `c_theta+O_theta(n^(-1/6))`. This is stronger than the requested upper bound, which also permits an aspect-ratio threshold. Theorem 1 gives positive-probability realizations arbitrarily close to h_theta at every sufficiently late pair in any admissible high-aspect sequence. Their existence proves the matching lower limit of the supremum and rules out every smaller eventual uniform constant. No expectation is interchanged with a directional minimum.

## Coverage and limits

All claims in the two numbered theorems and their explicit error and existence estimates pass. No remaining case of either canonical target was identified.
