# MD-01 proof audit — 8 October 2026

**Decision:** retain `Solution claimed`. The statement correspondence and
expectation bridge pass, but this review did not independently certify the
decisive graph-matrix combinatorics. This is not a counterexample to the paper.

## Source and target

Aaron Potechin and Jeff Xu,
[*Sharp Lovász-Theta Bounds on Random Graphs*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30064v1),
arXiv:2609.30064v1. Theorem 1.2 gives the upper bound
`θ(G(n,1/2)) ≤ (1+o(1))√n` with high probability. Appendix F identifies
the paper's SDP convention with the retained trace-normalized MD-01 SDP.
The canonical page's random-sign-matrix bound supplies a uniform second
moment for `θ(G)/√n`, making this high-probability upper bound an expectation
upper bound. For the matching expectation lower bound, the deterministic
inequality `θ(G) θ(complement G) ≥ n`, distributional symmetry and the
arithmetic-geometric mean inequality give `E θ(G) ≥ √n`.

## Scope of the proof review

The reviewer checked the conversion of a positive semidefinite witness to the
dual upper bound (Definitions 2.1 and 2.5), the positivity and nonedge
assembly (Appendix B and Claim 2.49), and the coefficient normalization and
truncation scaling (Appendices C–D). The dependency chain reaches three major
estimates whose exhaustive trace-walk and intersection-pattern enumeration
was not independently certified: Theorem 3.27/Corollary 3.28, Theorem 4.7,
and Theorem 4.17. Downstream algebra alone cannot validate those inputs.

Two displayed precision issues merit author clarification. Corollary 1.3
prints an exact `√n ≤ θ(G)` lower inequality, while Appendix A supplies an
asymptotic high-probability lower bound; the original expectation target has
the independent lower argument above. In Claim 4.15's displayed proof, an
upper bound containing `sqrt(n/ε)` is replaced by one containing
`sqrt(log(n/ε))` without a valid inequality at that line. The cited earlier
graph-matrix theorem appears to provide the intended logarithmic factor, so
this may be a transcription repair rather than a substantive gap. The audit
does not depend on resolving it because the central combinatorial inputs
remain unchecked.
