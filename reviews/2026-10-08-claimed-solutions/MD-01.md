# MD-01 proof audit — 8 October 2026

**Decision:** retain `Solution claimed`. The statement correspondence and
expectation bridge pass, but this review did not independently certify the
decisive graph-matrix combinatorics. An auxiliary theorem is false as printed;
this is not a counterexample to the paper's main theorem.

## Source and target

Aaron Potechin and Jeff Xu,
[*Sharp Lovász-Theta Bounds on Random Graphs*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.30064v1),
arXiv:2609.30064v1. Theorem 1.2 gives the upper bound
`θ(G(n,1/2)) ≤ (1+o(1))√n` with high probability. Appendix F identifies
the paper's SDP convention with the retained trace-normalized MD-01 SDP.
The canonical page's random-sign-matrix bound supplies a uniform second
moment for `θ(G)/√n`, making this high-probability upper bound an expectation
upper bound. For the matching expectation lower bound, the deterministic
inequality `θ(G) θ(complement G) ≥ n`, distributional symmetry and the
arithmetic-geometric mean inequality give `E θ(G) ≥ √n`.

## Scope of the proof review

The reviewer checked the conversion of a positive semidefinite witness to the
dual upper bound (Definitions 2.1 and 2.5), the positivity and nonedge
assembly (Appendix B and Claim 2.49), and the coefficient normalization and
truncation scaling (Appendices C–D). The dependency chain reaches three major
estimates whose exhaustive trace-walk and intersection-pattern enumeration
was not independently certified: Theorem 3.27/Corollary 3.28, Theorem 4.7,
and Theorem 4.17. Downstream algebra alone cannot validate those inputs.

Two displayed issues were confirmed by coauthor Jeff Xu in correspondence
supplied by the maintainer on 8 October 2026. Corollary 1.3 in v1 prints the
exact high-probability lower bound `√n ≤ θ(G)`; the intended lower bound is
`(1-o_n(1))√n ≤ θ(G)`. The original expectation target has the independent
exact expectation lower argument above. In Claim 4.15's proof, a factor
`sqrt(n/ε)` should contain `log(n/ε)` in place of `n/ε` under the square root,
as in the cited graph-matrix norm theorem. Both corrections preserve the
paper's claimed leading constant. A corrected arXiv version was not yet
available at this review's date. The central combinatorial inputs remain
subject to independent checking.

## Further check of the published proof

Theorem 3.24's general `O_{j,D_V}(1)` remainder for fixed mixed trace
moments is false as printed. Take the four backbone-correction shapes
`P_1`, `P_1`, `overline(overline(P_2) ∘ P_1)`, and `overline(P_2)` in that
order. All four occur with nonzero coefficients in the iterative construction.
For distinct vertex labels `a,b,c,d`, assign the successive blocks the
labels `(a,b)`, `(b,c)`, `(c,b,a,d)`, and `(d,c,a)` along their backbones.
The resulting trace closes, every assignment is injective within its block,
and each of the five graph edges `ab, bc, ac, ad, cd` occurs twice. Thus this
family alone contributes `(n)_4 / n^(7/2) = Θ(√n)` to the expected trace;
all nonzero parity expectations are nonnegative. The theorem's `N_{F/R}`
term is zero: pairing the two `P_1` blocks leaves two unequal shapes that
cannot form a forward/return pair. The reproducible
[exact small-order check](md01_moment_check.py) enumerates all sign graphs
and obtains the unnormalized expected trace `(n)_4` for `n=4,5`,
corroborating the family.

The proof's final slack estimate drops a factor `1/2` in its exponent.

This contradicts the stated auxiliary theorem, but does not by itself refute
Theorem 1.2: `Θ(√n)=o(n)`, and the later norm and correction arguments invoke
Theorems 3.25/3.27 and Corollary 3.28 with the weaker half-exponent slack
bound. Checks of 267 small parity-even trace patterns and 37,858 small
product-intersection patterns found no additional counterexample to their
structural classifications; these finite checks do not prove the
arbitrary-size cases. Other printed discrepancies occur in Definition 4.3
(the earlier-vertex exception should refer to the starting vertex),
Definition 4.6 (the second boundary is labeled `U` instead of `V`),
Theorem 4.7 (the degenerate `P_1` case needs a convention for its absent
second return partner), and Corollary 3.28 (a displayed middle bound drops
`2^(2q)`). Definition 2.39 and Theorem 3.27 also use inconsistent
normalizations for `Var(M)`; their main application has `Var(Q)=1`. These
appear locally repairable, but the general combinatorial
lemmas still need independent proof before promotion to `Solved`.
