## Proof checks

1. **Moment algebra and minimal apolar degree (lines 145–227).** The projective coordinate change acts through an invertible degree-`q` representation on each mode. Choosing a chart without support at infinity is legitimate for every nonzero binary apolar polynomial. The recurrence extends the first `r` moments through degree `D`. The pairing radical is an ideal; quotienting by a nonzero radical produces a proper divisor recurrence valid throughout the required moment interval, contradicting minimality. This proves the Frobenius condition without assuming squarefreeness. Both middle polynomial spaces surject onto the quotient because `r-1 <= floor(D/2)`, proving that `r` equals the middle catalecticant rank. The two-dimensional balanced apolar kernel and its choice independence are accounted for.

2. **Symmetric upper bounds (lines 231–328).** Locally, Frobenius nondegeneracy makes the coefficient multiplier a unit, and it has an `m`th root in the truncated polynomial algebra over the complex numbers. The Fourier period `(m-1)(ell-1)+1` admits no extraneous exponent congruent to `ell-1` in the possible product degree range. Every summand uses the same linear functional in all modes, so this is a symmetric decomposition even when a local algebra is larger than a mode. For the binary bound, the top-remainder pairing is nondegenerate, the inverse unit gives an apolar polynomial coprime to `G`, and the coprime pencil has a squarefree member outside finitely many critical values. Its leading coefficient is nonzero for nonzero pencil parameter. The Vandermonde recurrence reconstructs all moments. The exceptional balanced case and rank-one/zero cases cause no gap.

3. **Transformation lemma (lines 346–369).** I checked the induction, dimension identity, containment `U_e V_e ⊆ UV`, preservation of the designated subspace, and undoing the unit scalings. In particular, commutativity makes the cross product containment valid. Units span any subspace containing a unit because nonunits lie in finitely many residue hyperplanes. This is the finite-dimensional commutative specialization of Beck–Lecouvey Lemma 4.2.

4. **Contextual product inequality (lines 375–451).** This is the important exceptional-case step. For a nilpotent auxiliary stabilizer element, its decomposition-coordinate components vanish; multiplication of `aVQ` puts the result inside the annihilated product space. The all-but-one projection is the whole moment algebra, and `a_A` is a unit, so the Frobenius pairing forces the remaining component to vanish. Thus every auxiliary algebra is reduced. A reduced finite-dimensional complex subalgebra is generated by primitive idempotents, each constant zero or one on each ambient local factor. Consequently there are finitely many possibilities, indexed by support partitions. The polynomial basis curve and Vandermonde selection therefore give a common auxiliary algebra for a basis, yielding the stated inequality. The same nilpotent argument applies to the final stabilizer. Prefix stabilizers are contained in the final stabilizer by multiplying by the remaining factors. No ambient nonreduced-algebra Kneser theorem with an unmet finiteness hypothesis is being imported.

5. **Unrestricted exact-rank lower bound (lines 463–550).** The graph spaces encode arbitrary mode-dependent ordinary summands. Minimum-length summands are linearly independent: a dependence eliminates a summand after absorbing scalars into another mode. This establishes surjectivity onto the decomposition-coordinate space. Units exist by avoiding proper hyperplanes; their normalization preserves all needed dimensions, Frobenius nondegeneracy, and all-but-one surjectivity. Reduced stabilizer idempotents partition whole local factors. A block without a moment-algebra factor would, by coordinate surjectivity, be the entire corresponding scalar block and contradict annihilation by a functional with nonzero coordinate coefficients. Within each block the stabilizer is exactly scalar, and the projected mode dimension is at least `min(n,r_j)`. The resulting inequality
   `R_j >= m min(n,r_j) - r_j - m + 2`
   is valid. If a block has `r_j >= n`, its bound is at least `D-r+2`; otherwise summing blocks and using their number at most `s` gives `(m-1)r-(m-2)s`. These alternatives prove the minimum of the two symmetric upper bounds for an arbitrary ordinary decomposition. This handles `r>n` and multiple repeated support points without treating rank as monotone in the wrong direction under restriction.

6. **Border ranks and consequences (lines 572–744).** The squarefree recurrence perturbation is a genuine convergent Vandermonde upper-bound construction. The even-order flattening contains the middle catalecticant. For odd order I checked the displayed block factorization entrywise: `b+c=q` gives the shifted Hankel blocks and cancellation of the middle diagonal block. The matrix `E` is injective, including `q=1`, so left/right multiplication by `E,E^T` preserves the skew middle matrix rank. The Koszul map sends every grouped ordinary rank-one tensor to a matrix of rank at most two, giving the unrestricted border lower bound by closedness. Exact and border ranks are kept distinct throughout. The maximum-rank formula, examples, projective root-count formula, and terminal-sequence substitution appendix follow as stated.
