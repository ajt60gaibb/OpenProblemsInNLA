## Independent reconstruction of the proof

The result does not rely on the bundled PASS reports or on a finite computation. I reconstructed the following logical chain from the actual manuscript.

### 1. Simultaneous reconstruction gives many small singular values

If `m` center columns are approximated using a disjoint common reservoir, assemble the dependence columns into `V = [I; -X; 0]`. Then `V^T V = I + X^T X >= I` regardless of coefficient sizes or reuse of reservoir columns. Thus `Q = V(V^TV)^(-1/2)` has orthonormal columns and `||AQ||_F^2 <= ||AV||_F^2 <= m eta^2/4`. The lower Ky Fan sum is therefore at most `m eta^2/4`. If fewer than `m/2` Gram eigenvalues were at most `eta^2`, more than half of the first `m` eigenvalues would exceed `eta^2`, a contradiction. This argument is simultaneous: the identity block prevents dependencies among the coefficient witnesses from invalidating the count. Shared reservoir columns are harmless.

Column deletion produces a principal Gram submatrix, so interlacing changes the count below the closed threshold by zero or one. Two matrices differing in `h` columns both have counts within `[N(C), N(C)+h]` for their common submatrix. The replacement bound is consequently `h`, not the weaker rank-perturbation bound `2h`. Boundary eigenvalues at `eta^2` are included consistently.

### 2. Pruning supplies uniform incidence without destroying much mass

Delete all currently remaining atoms incident to a coordinate whose raw, unnormalized incidence lies in `(0,1/(4k))`. Each deletion charges a coordinate only once because its incidence becomes permanently zero. At most `k` charges remove total mass less than `1/4`. The retained law has mass at least `3/4`, and every active coordinate has raw incidence at least `1/(4k)`. Nothing here assumes a uniform law on distinct vector values; repeated original columns are naturally represented by atom masses.

### 3. The covariance filter is valid despite nonnormality

On the active subspace, write `D = diag(p_i)`, `Sigma = E uu^T`, and `T = Sigma D^{-1}`. Cauchy–Schwarz applied to each signed support gives `Sigma <= sD`; also `tr D = s`. Only D is inverted. With `R = D^{-1/2} Sigma D^{-1/2}`, we have `0 <= R <= sI` and `T = D^{1/2} R D^{-1/2}`. Substitution gives the exact identity

`E ||(I-T/s)^L u||^2 = tr(D R (I-R/s)^(2L))`.

The scalar maximum of `lambda (1-lambda/s)^(2L)` on `[0,s]` is no more than `s/(2L+1)`. Positivity and the spectral theorem then bound the trace by `s^2/(2L+1)`. This does not require D and R to commute, and it does not treat the nonnormal operator `I-T/s` as a Euclidean contraction. Singular covariance causes no problem because it is never inverted.

### 4. Signed conditional paths realize the polynomial filter

Given a state `v` equal to a signed copy of a retained vector, choose one of its `s` support coordinates uniformly and sample `w` from the retained law conditioned on that coordinate being nonzero. The next state `v_i w_i w` still has norm `sqrt(s)`, still has an admissible support, and has conditional mean `Tv/s`. Sign symmetry of the retained distribution is unnecessary: signs reside in the dependence coefficients. Repeated states are allowed.

The binomial combination of states along a single length-L trajectory has conditional mean `[I-(I-T/s)^L]u` and norm at most `sqrt(s)(2^L-1)`. Average b trajectories independent conditional on their common center. The centered error's cross terms vanish conditionally, yielding variance at most `s(2^L-1)^2/b`. Its cross term with the bias also vanishes. The specified ceilings make the bias contribution at most `epsilon^2/8` and variance at most `epsilon^2/8`, hence total mean square at most `epsilon^2/4`. Markov gives reconstruction error at most epsilon with probability at least `3/4`, averaged over the center and trajectories. No independence of successive states is asserted or needed.

### 5. Fresh chunks give a lower bound on the full successful path law

There are `m=floor(r/2)` centers and `J0=bL` disjoint reservoir chunks of size `ell=floor((r-m)/J0)`. Since `r >= 4J0`, `ell >= r/(4J0)`. For every requested active coordinate, the raw acceptable mass is at least `1/(4k)`, and therefore `ell*pi >= beta/(16J0)`. Its hit probability is at least `ell*pi/(1+ell*pi) >= beta/(16J0+beta)=a`.

For each fixed requested coordinate, the first accepted atom in a fresh chunk has unnormalized law equal to its hit probability times the ideal conditional atom law. Including the coordinate choice, every successful transition kernel dominates a times the ideal kernel. These are nonnegative kernels; composition preserves domination by `a^J0` for the complete path history, including all vectors used in the reconstruction. The event is not merely a terminal-state event. Conditional on all hits, the path law can be biased; the proof correctly uses unnormalized domination and never claims equality of those conditional laws.

The retained center contributes mass at least `3/4`, and ideal success contributes another `3/4`, giving each center success probability at least `(9/16)a^J0`. Every center can use the same reservoir because only its own marginal probability is analyzed. Linearity of expectation then gives `E Z >= (9/16)a^J0 m`. For each realization the simultaneous-witness lemma gives `N_eta >= Z/2`. Since `m >= r/3`, `E N_eta >= (3/32)a^J0 r = rho r`. Dependence among different centers' successes is immaterial.

### 6. Coupling and concentration give the stated exponential tail

Couple iid indices with distinct indices by retaining an iid draw if unused, and otherwise choosing uniformly among unused indices. Given the complete past, each unused index has probability `1/n + ((t-1)/n)/(n-t+1) = 1/(n-t+1)`, so the repaired ordered sample is exactly uniform without replacement. The expected number of altered positions is `r(r-1)/(2n)`. The one-column count bound transfers the mean with this loss. The condition `n >= r/rho` leaves mean at least `rho r/2`.

For a one-exchange-Lipschitz subset statistic, exposing ordered distinct draws gives Doob increments whose conditional range has length at most one. Indeed, transposing two alternative next labels couples the completions; final subsets either agree or exchange those two labels. Hoeffding's conditional exponential-moment argument yields `P(F-EF <= -t) <= exp(-2t^2/r)`. With `t=rho r/4` this gives exactly the asserted `exp(-rho^2 r/8)` tail. All random variables here have finite support, so there are no hidden integrability issues.

### 7. Quantifiers and edge cases

For every fixed `(s,beta,eta)`, all displayed constants are finite, `J0 >= 1`, `a` lies in `(0,1)`, and `rho` lies in `(0,1)`. Very large eta still gives positive ceilings. With `beta=1/(2C)`, the original asymptotic hypotheses eventually satisfy all finite assumptions, while `r_k` tends to infinity. The event of least singular value exceeding eta means `N_eta=0` and is contained in the finite bad-count event. Thus its probability tends to zero. All constants precede the varying matrix dimensions and values. No assertion for growing sparsity is inferred.
