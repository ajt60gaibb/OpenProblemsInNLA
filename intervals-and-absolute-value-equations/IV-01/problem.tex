\subsection{Resolution --- 15 October
2026}\label{resolution-15-october-2026}

\textbf{Resolved affirmatively.} Wenqi Zhu (University of Oxford), Kai
Zou (King's College London), and Ping Nie (University of Waterloo).

The answer is \textbf{yes}. For every positive integer dimension,
arbitrary real entrywise bounds, and every common signature, nonsingular
sign regularity at the two checker vertices implies nonsingular sign
regularity throughout the closed matrix interval. The result allows
arbitrary fixed entries, including fixed zeros of both checker parities,
and vanishing proper minors. The range \(n\ge1\) includes the original
question's \(n\ge5\), with no additional hypothesis on the interval or
its signature.

The primary formal result is
\href{https://github.com/erenup/two-vertex-sign-regularity/blob/22fedcfb6940fd943d67f1582068ed5b9b91a83e/lean/IV01/Statement.lean}{\textit{IV01.twoCheckerVertices}}.
Theorem 1.1 of the
\href{https://github.com/erenup/two-vertex-sign-regularity/blob/22fedcfb6940fd943d67f1582068ed5b9b91a83e/paper/main.pdf}{paper,
\emph{Two-vertex certification of nonsingular sign-regular matrix
intervals}} states the equivalent two-vertex characterization. The
\href{https://github.com/ajt60gaibb/OpenProblemsInNLA/blob/main/intervals-and-absolute-value-equations/IV-01\#lean-proof-and-verification-evidence}{Lean
proof and verification evidence} below gives the exact statement

and literature records below predate this resolution.

\subsubsection{Declarations and statement

All predicates used by the target are defined in

correspondence} compares them with the original catalogue statement:

\begin{itemize}
\tightlist
\item
  \textbf{Matrices and dimensions:} \textit{Square n} is
  \textit{Matrix (Fin n) (Fin n) Real}. The theorem quantifies over
  every \(n\ge1\) and all real matrices of that dimension. This includes
  every \(n\ge5\) in the original question.
\item
  \textbf{Signature:} \textit{IsSignature n ε} requires
  \textit{ε k = 1} or \textit{ε k = -1} for every \(1\le k\le n\).
  The function \textit{ε : Nat → Real} represents the finite
  signature by minor order. Unused values are unrestricted; every
  original signature extends to this representation.
\item
  \textbf{Interval:} \textit{EntrywiseLE L U} is the ordinary
  pointwise inequality, and \textit{InBox L U M} means
  \(L\le M\le U\). The interval is closed, with no strict-width
  condition. Fixed coordinates may occur anywhere.
\item
  \textbf{Checker vertices:} \textit{checkerMinus} chooses the lower
  entry at even row-plus-column parity and the upper entry at odd
  parity; \textit{checkerPlus} reverses those choices. Lean's zero-based
  row and column labels preserve the original checker parity when both
  are shifted to one-based labels.
\item
  \textbf{All minors and weak signs:} \textit{SignRegular ε M}
  quantifies over every order \(1\le k\le n\) and every pair of strictly
  increasing row and column maps. Its inequality is
  \(0\le\epsilon_k\det M[I,J]\). These are all minors, with row and
  column indices in increasing order; proper zero minors are allowed.
\item
  \textbf{Nonsingularity and common signature:}
  \textit{NonsingularSignRegular ε M} is the conjunction of
  \textit{Matrix.det M ≠ 0} and \textit{SignRegular ε M}. Both
  checker-vertex assumptions and the conclusion use the same \textit{ε}.
  Nonsingularity is assumed only at the two checker vertices and proved
  for every matrix in the box.
\end{itemize}

\href{https://github.com/erenup/two-vertex-sign-regularity/blob/22fedcfb6940fd943d67f1582068ed5b9b91a83e/lean/IV01/Statement.lean}{\textit{IV01.twoCheckerVertices}}

\subsection{Problem statement}\label{problem-statement}

For \(\epsilon\in\{-1,1\}^n\), call a real \(n\times n\) matrix \(M\)
\textbf{nonsingular sign regular with signature} \(\epsilon\) if
\(\det M\ne0\) and every minor of order \(k\) has determinant \(d\)
satisfying \(\epsilon_k d\ge0\), for every \(1\le k\le n\).

Let \(n\ge5\) and let \(L,U\in\mathbb R^{n\times n}\) satisfy \(L\le U\)
entrywise. Define the two checkerboard vertex matrices

\[A^-_{ij}=\begin{cases}L_{ij},&i+j\text{ even},\\U_{ij},&i+j\text{ odd},\end{cases}
\qquad
A^+_{ij}=\begin{cases}U_{ij},&i+j\text{ even},\\L_{ij},&i+j\text{ odd}.\end{cases}\]

\subsubsection{Question}\label{question}

If \(A^-\) and \(A^+\) are nonsingular sign regular with the same
signature \(\epsilon\), must every real matrix \(M\) with
\(L\le M\le U\) be nonsingular sign regular with signature \(\epsilon\)?

The implication would certify a property of an entire interval of
matrices from only two of its vertices. Allowing zero minors is
essential to the remaining problem.

\subsection{Earlier status evidence ---
2026-09-08}\label{earlier-status-evidence-2026-09-08}

The 2026 survey explicitly retains this question. It records positive
results for strictly sign regular matrices, totally nonnegative
matrices, tridiagonal matrices, and certain signatures covering all
orders at most four. Theorem 3.3 also covers intervals whose fixed
entries \(L_{ij}=U_{ij}\), if any, all have the same parity of \(i+j\);
fixed entries of both parities are allowed in the general question. It
also records counterexamples when nonsingularity is omitted. Searches
for sign-regular interval conjectures and subsequent 2026 results found
no resolution of the displayed implication.

\subsection{Audit update --- 2026-09-10}\label{audit-update-2026-09-10}

Rechecked the
\href{https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s44146-026-00223-y}{2026
survey}, §3, Theorems 3.2--3.3 and the intervening open question.
Tridiagonal and certain signature classes, as well as the specified
restrictions on fixed-entry parity, prove substantive subclasses even
for \(n\ge5\). The unrestricted nonsingular case remains explicitly open
there. Searches for subsequent sign-regular interval certification
results found no general resolution; the status now exposes these
partial results.
