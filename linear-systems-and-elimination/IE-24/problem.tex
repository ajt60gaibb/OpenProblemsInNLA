\textbf{Rating rationale:} Uniform cut-cell estimates on arbitrary
smooth domains are challenging beyond rectangles; effective Neumann
preconditioning has community importance.

\subsection{Statement}\label{statement}

Let \(\Omega\subset\mathbb R^2\) be a fixed bounded connected domain
with smooth boundary. For grid width \(h>0\), retain the points
\(p\in h\mathbb Z^2\) whose square control cells \(C_p=p+[-h/2,h/2]^2\)
intersect \(\Omega\). Order them lexicographically and identify them
with \(1,\ldots,N_h\). For cells sharing a face, put

\[w_{pq}=\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{length}}\nolimits(C_p\cap C_q\cap\Omega)}h,\]

and set other weights to zero. Define the Purvis--Burkhalter Neumann
matrix by \(a_p=\sum_qw_{pq}\), \(A_{pp}=a_p\), and \(A_{pq}=-w_{pq}\)
for \(p\ne q\). Write \(L\) for its strictly lower triangular part.

For \(0< r<1\), the source's relaxed incomplete LU preconditioner uses
\(E=\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}\nolimits(e_1,\ldots,e_{N_h})\), where
\(e_1=a_1\) and, in increasing order,

\[e_p=a_p-\sum_{\substack{q< p\\w_{pq}>0}}
\frac{w_{pq}}{e_q}
\left(w_{pq}+(1-r)\sum_{\substack{s>q\\s\ne p}}w_{qs}\right),
\qquad M=(L+E)E^{-1}(E+L^T).\]

This is Definition 4 written with numbered grid points; sums contain
only neighboring cells. Define \(\kappa_+(M^{-1}A)\) as the largest
divided by the smallest \textbf{positive} eigenvalue of
\(M^{-1/2}AM^{-1/2}\), excluding the Neumann zero eigenvalue.

\textbf{Conjecture.} There are constants
\(c_\Omega,K_\Omega,h_\Omega>0\), independent of \(h\), such that with
\(r=c_\Omega h^2\), for all \(0< h< h_\Omega\) the pivots are positive
and

\[\kappa_+(M^{-1}A)\le K_\Omega h^{-1}.\]

Here \(h_\Omega\) is small enough that \(r<1\). This makes the source's
``some moderate constant'' explicit as an existence quantifier; no
numerical meaning is assigned to ``moderate.'' The authors recommend
\(c_\Omega=1\) in practice.

\subsection{Numerical significance}\label{numerical-significance}

This asks whether a specified sparse preconditioner gives the observed
improvement in conditioning for general curved Neumann boundaries. The
source proves the rectangular case, while its three-dimensional
experiments do not support the same RILU claim.

\subsection{Positive pivots and a uniform lower spectral
bound}\label{positive-pivots-and-a-uniform-lower-spectral-bound}

\textbf{Partial result (AI-derived).} On every smooth domain in the
statement, the RILU pivots are positive for sufficiently small \(h\) and
every \(0< r<1\). For \(r=c h^2\) with any fixed \(c>0\), the smallest
positive generalized eigenvalue stays bounded away from zero uniformly
in \(h\). The upper spectral bound needed for the conjectured condition
number remains open.

\textbf{Pivots.} The positive-weight face graph is connected. Each
retained cell has an interior point in \(\Omega\) by openness. A
polygonal path in \(\Omega\) between two such points can be perturbed to
avoid the grid faces of codimension at least two. Its face crossings
then give a chain of positive weights. For small \(h\) there is a cell
with two forward neighbors of weight one, by choosing a grid stencil
inside a ball contained in \(\Omega\).

Orient each positive-weight edge forward in the ordering. Put
\(s_p=\sum_{q>p}w_{pq}\) and \(k_p=e_p-s_p\). The recurrence gives

\[k_1=0,\qquad
k_p=\sum_{q< p,\,w_{pq}>0}\frac{w_{pq}}{e_q}
       \bigl(k_q+r(s_q-w_{pq})\bigr).\]

Inductively \(k_p\ge0\), so a vertex with a forward edge has \(e_p>0\).
For a sink, consider it and all its directed ancestors. If no ancestor
has two forward neighbors, each ancestor other than the sink has its
only forward neighbor in this set. Any edge entering the set would make
its starting vertex an ancestor. The set would then have no undirected
edge to its complement. Connectedness would make it the whole graph,
contrary to the interior stencil. A branch at an ancestor thus gives a
strictly positive term on the first edge of a path to the sink. The
recurrence propagates this positivity along the path and gives positive
pivot at the sink too.

\subsubsection{Weighted Poincaré estimate for the cut-cell
graph}\label{weighted-poincaruxe9-estimate-for-the-cut-cell-graph}

The estimate below holds in both dimensions two and three with the face
fractions in
\href{https://github.com/ajt60gaibb/OpenProblemsInNLA/blob/main/linear-systems-and-elimination/IE-25/README.md}{IE-25}.
For some \(C_\Omega\) and all sufficiently small \(h\), every array of
grid values satisfies

\[\min_{b\in\mathbb R}\sum_p a_p|x_p-b|^2\le C_\Omega h^{-2}x^TAx.\]

For the boundary estimate, use a finite cover of the smooth boundary by
graph charts with a coordinate axis directed inward. Such an axis exists
at every boundary point because some coordinate component of its normal
is nonzero. Reflect that coordinate when necessary and write the section
of the domain as \(x_d< f(x')\) with Lipschitz constant \(L\) bounded
uniformly over the charts. Call a center deep if it lies in \(\Omega\)
at distance greater than \(4\sqrt d\,h\) from its boundary. For small
\(h\) each remaining retained cell and a bounded number of its
neighboring cells along the inward axis fit in one chart, by compactness
and by taking the initial charts slightly smaller than their graph
neighborhoods.

In such a cell, let \(W_0\) be the fraction of its inward face in
\(\Omega\). The opposite face fraction is at most \(W_0\). On a side
face, fix the other tangential coordinates and let \(\ell\) be the
vertical length in \(\Omega\) at those coordinates. Moving from the side
into the full tangential footprint by at most \(\ell/(2\max\{1,L\})\)
keeps the graph strictly above the inward face height when \(\ell>0\).
Integrating over the other tangential coordinates shows that the side
face fraction is at most \(2\max\{1,L\}W_0\). Summing the faces gives
\(W_0\ge c_0a_p\) for a uniform \(c_0>0\).

Moving along the inward axis keeps the same tangential footprint and
moves the face height down, so the successive face fractions are
nondecreasing. After a uniformly bounded number of steps the center is
deep. Indeed the initial excess of its height above \(f\) at the
tangential center is \(O(h)\) since the cell meets \(\Omega\), and a
vertical depth \(s>0\) below a Lipschitz graph gives distance at least
\(s/\sqrt{1+L^2}\) from that graph. For small \(h\) any nearest boundary
point remains in the chart over the bounded path. Each path therefore
joins the original center to a deep center \(p_*\) along edges of weight
at least \(c_0a_p\). Each edge and each deep endpoint are used by only a
bounded number of these paths, since all paths have bounded length in
grid steps. Cauchy--Schwarz along the paths gives

\[\sum_{p\ \mathrm{not\ deep}}a_p|x_p-x_{p_*}|^2\le C x^TAx.\]

For the deep values, interpolate the nodal values by multilinear grid
interpolation on the inner offset of \(\Omega\) at distance
\(2\sqrt d\,h\) from the boundary. The interpolation cubes that meet
this offset have fully interior nodal control cells. Local scaling on
the cubes and the unit weights of their lattice edges bound the integral
of the squared gradient by \(C h^{d-2}x^TAx\). The smooth boundary has a
tubular neighborhood. Moving its boundary inward by \(2\sqrt d\,h\)
inside this neighborhood with a fixed smooth cutoff gives uniformly
bi-Lipschitz maps from \(\Omega\) to the inner offsets for small \(h\).
Thus the continuum Poincaré constant on the inner offset is uniform.
With \(b\) the mean of the interpolant there, the integral of the
squared difference from \(b\) is at most \(C h^{d-2}x^TAx\). Each deep
center is the lower lattice vertex of a distinct interpolation cube
entirely inside this offset. On a cube the squared nodal value times
\(h^d\) is controlled by the squared \(L^2\) norm of the interpolant by
finite-dimensional norm equivalence on the unit cube. Consequently the
deep squared nodal differences from \(b\) sum to at most
\(C h^{-2}x^TAx\). Combine this with the path estimate, bounded endpoint
congestion, and \(a_p\le2d\) to obtain the claimed weighted inequality.

\textbf{Generalized eigenvalues.} Expansion of the factors gives
\(M=A+\mathop{\mathrm{diag}}\nolimits(M\mathbf1)-L_F\), where \(L_F\) is
the positive semidefinite graph Laplacian with off-diagonal fill weights
\(F_{ps}=\sum_{q<\min(p,s)} w_{pq}w_{sq}/e_q\) for \(p\ne s\). The RILU
recurrence gives the row sums

\[0\le (M\mathbf1)_p=r\sum_{q< p,\,w_{pq}>0}
       \frac{w_{pq}(s_q-w_{pq})}{e_q}\le r a_p,\]

using \(e_q\ge s_q\). On the \(M\)-orthogonal complement of the constant
vector, \(x^TMx\le (x-b\mathbf1)^T M(x-b\mathbf1)\) for every constant
\(b\). Choose \(b\) by the weighted Poincaré estimate. The factor
identity and \(r=c h^2\) give \(x^TMx\le (1+cC_\Omega)x^TAx\). The graph
has just the constants in its kernel and \(M\) is positive definite by
the pivot result. The generalized Rayleigh principle gives
\(\lambda_{\min,+}(M^{-1}A)\ge(1+c C_\Omega)^{-1}\).

2026-09-10}\label{independent-audit-2026-09-10}

The recurrence and smooth-domain quantifiers were independently compared
with Lee--Min, Definition 4 and the §3 conjecture. The §5 proof treats
rectangles, whose corners exclude them from the displayed
smooth-boundary class; it therefore does not justify a
partial-resolution label for this exact target. The full author PDF and
the later MILU preprint were checked. The latter assumes positive
definiteness in §2.1 and leaves Neumann conditions to future work in §5.
Targeted subsequent searches found no smooth-domain resolution.
