## Common definitions and conventions

Let $G$ have the already defined nested product Gaussian law. Every norm $\|\cdot\|_2$ below is the Euclidean operator norm, every $\|\cdot\|_F$ the Frobenius norm, and vector norms are Euclidean unless labeled otherwise. Do not accidentally use the entrywise maximum norm or the default function-space sup norm in spectral estimates.

For an $a\times b$ real matrix, write singular values in decreasing order, $\sigma_1\ge\cdots\ge\sigma_{\min(a,b)}\ge0$. Mathlib's linear-map singular values use zero-based natural indices, so source $\sigma_j$ corresponds to index $j-1$. Every use needs this translation and valid-index evidence; alternatively deliberately zero-extend the sequence.

For a $t\times t$ matrix $T$ and natural $r$,

$$
\Sigma_r(T)=
 \begin{cases}
 \displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{t-r}\sigma_i(T)^{-2},&r<t,\\
 0,&r\ge t.
 \end{cases}
$$

This removes the largest $r$ inverse singular values, hence the smallest $r$ original singular values. It must not sum the opposite end of the spectrum. Use the displayed finite reciprocal sum as a total real definition, but every reciprocal comparison must be guarded by positivity of the retained singular values; real division by zero is not an infinite reciprocal. The selected blocks $T_t$ are nonsingular. In A5, $M$ itself may be singular, but its top $s-k$ retained singular values are positive under the stated hypothesis.

For the deterministic measurable smallest-current-index PP rule, let $\pi_t$ be the ordered first $t$ original pivot rows, $T_t=G_{\pi_t,[t]}$, and $\pi_t^c$ the remaining original rows in increasing order. Let $P_t$ reorder rows as $(\pi_t,\pi_t^c)$ and

$$
E_t=[-G_{\pi_t^c,[t]}T_t^{-1}\ \ I]P_t.
$$

At $t=0$, define $E_0=I$. The active complement is $E_tG_{:,[t]^c}$. Every row of $E_t$ has a surviving identity coordinate, so its norm is at least one. The existing padded trajectory must be proved equivalent to this block/elimination-matrix description.

Let $\mathcal F_t$ record all first $t$ columns and $\mathcal H_t$ record $(\pi_t,T_t)$. Both are explicit sigma-algebras generated by measurable maps. Conditional statements mean almost-everywhere statements for regular conditional laws/kernels, or equivalent integrated fiber inequalities. Conditioning on a particular matrix value of probability zero is not elementary event conditioning.

Write $\lambda=\log n$. All Section 5 uses may assume $n\ge2$; strengthen the final dimension threshold as needed. All rank counts $r,k,d,t,m,j$ are natural numbers, with explicitly positive counts wherever they occur in denominators.

## Required theorem contracts

### F: foundational probability and elimination facts

F1. For each finite family of atomless real probability measures and each nonzero multivariate real polynomial, its zero set has product measure zero, including the zero-variable case.

F2. Flattening the actual nested Gaussian matrix law gives the product of $n^2$ standard normal laws. Evaluating the determinant polynomial then proves Gaussian singularity has measure zero for every $n$, including $n=0$ where the determinant is one.

F3. On nonsingular positive-dimensional matrices there is an admissible PP path, its pivots are nonzero, every resulting leading selected block is invertible, and each PP multiplier has absolute value at most one.

F4. The deterministic tie rule is measurable. The first $t$ completed pivots, $E_t$ and $T_t$ depend only on the first $t$ columns, hence are $\mathcal F_t$-measurable. In the zero-based padded trajectory, selecting pivot $k$ uses column $k$, hence the first $k+1$ columns and $\mathcal F_{k+1}$. Almost surely its pivot magnitude is unique at each nontrivial stage. The null set can be obtained from finitely many guarded rational equalities between two candidate residual magnitudes: clear denominators, square both sides, and show each resulting polynomial is nonzero. All admissible paths coincide outside this null set. A factorial union bound with a nonzero failure probability is not an acceptable substitute.

F5. Deleting a fixed set of rows not selected in the first $m$ steps leaves those first $m$ pivots unchanged, at least on the full-measure no-tie set. This is the deletion consistency used in Lemma 5.4. It must also be proved for the rectangular matrix obtained after deletion.

F6. Conditional on $\mathcal F_t$, every unrevealed column is an independent standard Gaussian vector. Consequently a row $e^T$ of the $\mathcal F_t$-measurable $E_t$ satisfies $e^TG_{:,j}\mid\mathcal F_t\sim N(0,\|e\|_2^2)$ for $j>t$.

F7. Conditional on $\mathcal H_t$, each unpivoted original row restricted to its first $t$ entries has standard Gaussian law restricted and normalized on

$$
\mathcal K_t=\{x:\|x\widehat U^{-1}\|_\infty\le1\},
\qquad T_t=\widehat L\widehat U.
$$

This set is closed, convex, centrally symmetric and has positive Gaussian measure. A direct proof can factor the finite pivot-event density over fixed ordered pivot indices and pivot-block entries. No independence after conditioning on success at all stages is assumed.

F8. For $Z\sim N(0,1)$ and $h\ge0$, $\Pr(|Z|>h)\le2e^{-h^2/2}$. If $q=\Pr(|Z|<1)$, then $0<q<1$ and $\Pr(\|G\|_{\max}<1)=q^{n^2}$. In particular this denominator event is eventually smaller than every prescribed inverse polynomial.

F9. Elimination row norms obey the deterministic bound $\|(E_u)_{i,:}\|_1\le2^u$. A block of $s$ further PP steps gives a row-operation matrix $J$ with $\max_i\|J_{i,:}\|_1\le2^s$, $JE_t=E_{t+s}$, and $JE_tG_\star=0$ for the next $s$ columns.

### A1: centered Lemma 4.1

For every fixed $M\in\mathbb R^{b\times p}$, Gaussian $G\in\mathbb R^{a\times b}$ and $x>0$,

$$
\Pr\{\|GM\|_F^2>2a\|M\|_F^2+4x\|M\|_2^2\}\le e^{-x}.
$$

For $1\le k\le\min(a,p)$,

$$
\Pr\{\sigma_k(GM)>
 \|M\|_F+(\sqrt a+\sqrt{2x/k})\|M\|_2\}\le e^{-x}.
$$

The source proof uses Gaussian Frobenius/spectral expected-norm estimates, Gaussian Lipschitz concentration, and the Ky Fan Euclidean norm $f_k(A)=(\sum_{i=1}^k\sigma_i(A)^2)^{1/2}$. Required deterministic facts include its Lipschitz constant and $\sqrt k\,\sigma_k\le f_k$.

### A2: centered Lemma 4.2

For $r\ge1$, $0\le a\le r$, $b\ge3r$, and Gaussian $G\in\mathbb R^{a\times b}$, for every $x\ge1$,

$$
\Pr\{\|G^\dagger\|_2^2>2e^{2x/r+2}/r\}\le e^{-x}.
$$

The zero-row case is trivial and should be separated. In the remaining cases, prove full row rank almost surely before identifying the norm of the pseudoinverse with the reciprocal least singular value. One may restrict to $2r$ columns and add independent rows to obtain an $r\times2r$ matrix. For $r=1$ the proof is a two-dimensional Gaussian radial calculation. For $r\ge2$ the cited estimate is HMT A.3:

$$
\Pr\{\|H^\dagger\|_2>u\}
\le\frac1{\sqrt{2\pi(b-a+1)}}
 \left(\frac{e\sqrt b}{b-a+1}\right)^{b-a+1}u^{-(b-a+1)}
$$

for $b\ge a\ge2,u>0$. This is a separate probabilistic theorem, not a consequence of nonsingularity alone.

### A3: centered Lemma 4.3

There exists an absolute real $c$ with $0<c\le1$ such that, for every $s$, $4\le j<s$, and $0<\theta\le1$, a Gaussian $s\times s$ matrix satisfies

$$
\Pr\{\sigma_{s-j}(G)\le cj\theta/\sqrt s\}
 \le s^{j+1}\theta^{j^2/4}.
$$

The same fixed $c$ must work uniformly in all dimensions and indices. The source reduction uses Nguyen (7) with $\gamma=1/2$, $k=j+1$, $\varepsilon=cj\theta/(j+1)$, and the scalar concentration-function estimate $p(\varepsilon)\le\sqrt{2/\pi}\varepsilon$. Nguyen's exponent is $(1-\gamma)k^2$ and its prefactor is $s^{\lfloor(1-\gamma/2)k\rfloor}$. These imply the weaker displayed source bound after choosing $c$ once. The general perturbation theorem is unnecessary; a Gaussian-specific proof of this exact contract would suffice.

### A4: centered Lemma 4.4

For every closed convex centrally symmetric $\mathcal K\subset\mathbb R^a$ of positive standard Gaussian measure, let $\nu$ be that Gaussian measure restricted to $\mathcal K$ and normalized. Then

$$
\int X\,d\nu=0,\qquad
\forall u\in\mathbb R^a,\quad
\int e^{u^TX}\,d\nu\le e^{\|u\|_2^2/2}.
$$

For fixed $M\in\mathbb R^{a\times p}$ and $x>0$,

$$
\nu\{\|X^TM\|_2^2>2\|M\|_F^2+4x\|M\|_2^2\}\le e^{-x}.
$$

Only the zero-mean restriction is needed. The source proves the MGF claim using marginal log-concavity and a Hessian bound, then the quadratic claim using Hsu–Kakade–Zhang. A shorter centered route could use the Gaussian shift inequality for symmetric convex sets (Anderson's inequality), but that theorem itself must be proved; it is not currently a discovered verified shortcut.

### A4-min: sufficient centered specialization that avoids unused machinery

For IE-06, B1 only uses the weaker conclusion

$$
\nu\{\|X^TM\|_2^2>(2+4x)\|M\|_F^2\}\le e^{-x}.
$$

This exact version is sufficient because $\|M\|_F^2\le\tau$. It can replace A4's operator/Frobenius refinement without changing any later target or B1's constants. The nonzero-mean, Hessian and covariance claims need not be formalized.

A proposed proof requiring independent review is:

1. Prove the Gaussian shift inequality $\gamma_a(\mathcal K-u)\le\gamma_a(\mathcal K)$ for centrally symmetric convex $\mathcal K$. Prékopa marginal log-concavity implies the shift probability is log-concave; symmetry makes it even, hence maximized at zero.
2. Complete the square to get the centered vector MGF bound. This avoids differentiating an integral or proving a covariance Hessian bound.
3. For any real scalar $Y$ with $\mathbb E e^{tY}\le e^{t^2/2}$ for every $t$, introduce an independent standard normal $Z$. Tonelli and the Gaussian MGF give
   $\mathbb E e^{Y^2/4}=\mathbb E_Z\mathbb E_Y e^{ZY/\sqrt2}\le\mathbb E_Ze^{Z^2/4}=\sqrt2$.
4. If $F^2=\|M\|_F^2>0$, write $\|X^TM\|_2^2/F^2$ as a convex combination of squared normalized scalar projections, one per nonzero column of $M$. Finite Jensen gives $\mathbb E e^{\|X^TM\|_2^2/(4F^2)}\le\sqrt2$. No independence of those projections is required. If $F=0$, $M=0$ and the tail event is empty.
5. Exponential Markov yields the claimed tail because $\sqrt2e^{-(2+4x)/4}\le e^{-x}$, equivalently $\log2\le1$.

This route removes Hsu–Kakade–Zhang and measurable SVD from this particular dependency, while retaining the substantive Gaussian convex-shift theorem. It is a proof plan, not a completed Lean result; no listed input may become an assumed axiom.

### A5: centered Lemma 4.5

There exists an absolute $C\ge1$ such that the following holds. Let $1\le k<s$, let $M\in\mathbb R^{s\times s}$ be deterministic with

$$
0<\mu\le\sqrt k,\qquad
\sigma_{s-k}(M)\ge\mu,\qquad
\Sigma_k(M)\le2k\mu^{-2}.
$$

For Gaussian $G\in\mathbb R^{s\times4k}$, let $B=[M\ G]$. For every $x\ge1$, outside an event of probability at most $3e^{-x}$, simultaneously

$$
\|B^\dagger\|_2^2\le C\mu^{-2}e^{Cx/k},
\qquad
\|B^\dagger\|_F^2\le Ck\mu^{-2}e^{Cx/k}.
$$

Source proof: rotate $M$ to singular diagonal form, split the Gaussian rows into $k$ and $s-k$, normalize the first Gaussian block to an orthonormal $Q$, compare $BB^T$ to an invertible block-triangular matrix, and apply A1/A2. Measurability of the chosen rotations and rank-exception handling are required when this is used conditionally.

### A6: deterministic Lemma 4.6, with an explicit denominator guard

Let $M\in\mathbb R^{s\times(s+k)}$ have rank $s$, let $Q\in\mathbb R^{(s+k)\times k}$ be an orthonormal basis of its nullspace, and let $B\in\mathbb R^{k\times(s+k)}$. For $j\in\mathbb N$ with $2j<k$ and $j<s$, assume additionally $\sigma_{k-j}(BQ)>0$. Then

$$
\sigma_{s+k-2j}\begin{pmatrix}M\\B\end{pmatrix}
\ge\left(
 \|M^\dagger\|_2+
 \frac{1+\sigma_{j+1}(BM^\dagger)}{\sigma_{k-j}(BQ)}
 \right)^{-1}.
$$

The positivity guard is essential with Lean real division. Without it, choose $B=0$: total division would make the displayed right side positive while the left side vanishes. The intended extended-inverse convention in the informal inequality must not be translated as total real division. All applications in Lemma 5.4 supply the positive denominator. The index guard $j<s$ is also satisfied there, or can be omitted only if the singular sequence is deliberately zero-extended.

Proof obligations include singular-value interlacing under row projection, simultaneous removal of $j$ bad left directions from each of two matrices, and the right-inverse solution bound.

### B1: Lemma 5.2, truncated inverse decomposition

For every stage $t<n$ and $r\ge1$, construct $\mathcal F_t$-measurable $X_t,Y_t,Q_t$ with

$$
E_t=X_t+Y_tQ_t^T,\qquad
Q_t\in\mathbb R^{n\times\min(t,r)},\quad Q_t^TQ_t=I.
$$

If $t\le r$, every row of $X_t$ has norm one. If $t>r$, on the $\mathcal H_t$-measurable event $\Sigma_r(T_t)\le\tau$, for every deterministic $\tau>0,x>0$,

$$
\Pr\left\{\max_i\|(X_t)_{i,:}\|_2^2>
 1+(2+4x)\tau\mid\mathcal H_t\right\}\le ne^{-x}.
$$

For $t>r$, use a measurable spectral truncation $T_t^{-1}=Z_t+HV^T$ with $V^TV=I_r$ and $\|Z_t\|_F^2=\Sigma_r(T_t)$, then apply A4 through F7. A bare classical choice of an SVD does not establish measurability.

### B2: Lemma 5.3, smoothing the discarded inverse directions

There is an absolute $C_0>0$ and, for every $q\ge1$, a $C_q>0$ such that, for $r\ge1$ and $n-t>4r$, the following conditional bound holds. Given $\mathcal F_t$ and the B1 decomposition whose rows satisfy $\|(X_t)_{i,:}\|_2\le\zeta$ for $\zeta\ge0$, with conditional failure probability at most $C_0n^{-q}$,

$$
\max_i\|(E_{t+4r})_{i,:}\|_2
 \le\zeta e^{C_q(r+\lambda/r)}.
$$

The constants are independent of $\|Y_t\|_2$, $n,t,r,\zeta$ and the revealed past. The $t=0$ empty-rank case can be separated using F9. For $t>0$, fresh Gaussian columns $G_\star$ give

$$
E_{t+4r}
 =J\bigl(X_t-X_tG_\star(Q_t^TG_\star)^\dagger Q_t^T\bigr),
\qquad \max_i\|J_{i,:}\|_1\le2^{4r}.
$$

Use A1 and A2 with $x=(q+1)\lambda$ and a row union bound. The $X_tG_\star$ and $Q_t^TG_\star$ factors need not be independent; a union bound does not require independence.

### B3: Proposition 5.1 strengthened to all Schur entries

For every $p>0$, there are $C_p>0,N_p\ge2$ such that, for all $n\ge N_p$, integers $1\le r<n/8$, and deterministic $\tau,\delta>0$, if

$$
\Pr\{\forall t<n,\ \Sigma_r(T_t)\le\tau\}\ge1-\delta,
$$

then

$$
\Pr\left\{\rho_{\rm PP}(G)>
 \sqrt{1+\tau}\,e^{C_p(r+\lambda/r)}\right\}
 \le\delta+n^{-p}.
$$

Here $\rho_{\rm PP}$ means the existing all-admissible-path growth event, after F4 transfers the deterministic rule almost surely. The strengthened numerator statement is obtained in the same proof before dividing by the input maximum.

Take $q=p+4$. B1 over starting stages costs at most $n^{2-q}$. B2 costs at most $C_0n^{1-q}$. Stages $u\le4r$ use F9. Each Schur entry has conditional Gaussian tail from F6, with $h=\sqrt{2q\lambda}$; the total number of entries is $\sum_{u=0}^{n-1}(n-u)^2\le n^3$. This costs at most $2n^{3-q}$. Add the denominator event from F8 and absorb all constants by increasing $N_p,C_p$.

All bounds are stagewise conditional bounds integrated and then unioned. Never condition the fresh-column law on the success of every stage.

### B4: Lemma 5.4, extending a selected block

For every $\beta\ge1$, there exists $C_\beta>0$ such that the following holds uniformly. Let natural $m,k,d,n$ satisfy

$$
m+4k\le n,\quad k<m,\quad d\ge16,\quad k\ge100d,
$$

and let $0<\mu<\sqrt k$. Then

$$
\begin{split}
\Pr\{&
 \sigma_{m-k}(T_m)\ge\mu,\quad
 \Sigma_k(T_m)\le2k\mu^{-2},\\
&\sigma_{m+4k-d}(T_{m+4k})
 \le(\mu d/k)e^{-C_\beta(1+k\lambda/d^2)}
\}\le n^{-\beta}.
\end{split}
$$

First expose the first $m$ columns and use A5 to extend the already selected rows with $4k$ fresh columns. Then, for each fixed candidate set $S$ of $4k$ rows, delete $S$, run PP on the other rows, and condition on all those other rows. The selected rectangular block is now fixed and the candidate rows remain Gaussian. Apply A1 to its pseudoinverse and A3 to the candidate rows projected into its nullspace. Set $j=\lfloor d/2\rfloor$; A6 combines the two estimates. F5 identifies the actual next pivot set with one of these fixed candidates.

There are at most $n^{4k}$ candidates. Bounds must be proved for all candidates first and only then applied to the random actual candidate; conditioning on it being the next pivot set would invalidate the unconditioned Gaussian law.

### B5: Proposition 5.5, spectral recursion

For every $\gamma>0$, there exist $C_\gamma>0,N_\gamma\ge2$ such that for $n\ge N_\gamma$, with probability at least $1-n^{-\gamma}$, **simultaneously for every integer** $r\ge\lceil\sqrt\lambda\rceil$,

$$
\max_{t<n}\Sigma_r(T_t)
 \le\frac{C_\gamma n}{r}
 e^{C_\gamma(\lambda/r+\log\log n)}.
$$

The denominator is positive because $N_\gamma$ can ensure $\sqrt\lambda\ge16$. Define for integer $d\ge\lceil\sqrt\lambda\rceil$,

$$
k(d)=\max(100d,\lceil d^2/\lambda\rceil),\quad
d_0=d,\quad d_{i+1}=k(d_i),
$$

$$
h(d)=\sum_{i:d_i<n}(1+\lambda/d_i),
\qquad
g(d)=\frac d{\sqrt n}e^{-D_\gamma h(d)}.
$$

Choose $D_\gamma$ once, uniformly for all $n,d,t$. The induction proves

$$
\sigma_{t-d}(T_t)\ge g(d)
\quad\text{for all }\lceil\sqrt\lambda\rceil\le d<t\le n
$$

except with probability $n^{-\gamma}$. Base cases $k(d)\ge t/8$ use A3 and a union over at most $n^t$ choices of rows. Recursive cases set $u=t-4k(d)>t/2>k(d)$ and use B4 with $\mu=g(k(d))$. Its strict requirement $\mu<\sqrt{k(d)}$ follows from $k(d)<n$, not merely the weak bound written in the source discussion. There are fewer than $n^2$ pairs $(t,d)$; take $\beta=\gamma+2$.

Required scalar recursion facts:

- $k(d)\ge100d$, so the chain terminates below any fixed $n$ and $h$ is a finite sum.
- $h$ is nonincreasing, and $g(d)/d$ is nondecreasing.
- $h(d)=1+\lambda/d+h(k(d))$ for $d<n$.
- For $d^2\ge\lambda>0$,
  $1+k(d)\lambda/d^2\le2+100\lambda/d$.
- The step count is bounded by a constant times $\log\log n$, and
  $\sum_i\lambda/d_i\le2\lambda/d$.
- Consequently $h(d)\le C(\lambda/d+\log\log n)$.
- For $r\ge1$, $\sum_{d=r}^\infty d^{-2}\le2/r$, yielding
  $\Sigma_r(T_t)\le2r/g(r)^2$.

For the fourth inequality, use $\lceil d^2/\lambda\rceil\le d^2/\lambda+1$ and $\lambda/d^2\le100\lambda/d$ for integer $d\ge1$. Both branches of $k(d)\lambda/d^2$ are then at most $1+100\lambda/d$; the constants must be checked symbolically. No numerical evaluation of a huge threshold $n$ is needed.

### C: final assembly

Fix $\alpha>0$, take $\gamma=p=\alpha+1$ and $r=\lceil\sqrt{\log n}\rceil$. For all sufficiently large $n$, $1\le r<n/8$, and B5 supplies deterministic

$$
\tau=n e^{C_\alpha\sqrt{\log n}}
$$

as a valid upper bound. B3 then gives the required threshold, after increasing $C_\alpha$:

$$
\sqrt{1+\tau}\,e^{C_{\alpha+1}(r+\lambda/r)}
 \le\sqrt n\,e^{C'_\alpha\sqrt{\log n}}.
$$

The failure probability is at most $2n^{-(\alpha+1)}<n^{-\alpha}$ for $n>2$. This proves SchurSubpolynomialTail, unconditionally. Only then may the already kernel-checked reduction produce SquareRootUpperBound.

## Gaps, review decisions, and safe implementation order

1. **Literal A6 is false under total real division without the added guard.** Keep the positivity hypothesis; it is available on the later good event.
2. **Implicit integer/rank conventions must be explicit.** In particular $r\ge1$, nonzero pivot blocks, valid singular indices, $n\ge2$, and strict $\mu<\sqrt k$ at the B4 call.
3. **Adaptive Gaussian claims require proofs.** A matrix chosen after seeing all rows is not independent of those rows. Use the specified column filtration and row-deletion conditioning.
4. **Measurable spectral choices are not automatic.** Prove them or rewrite estimates in measurable invariant form with an explicit measurable construction.
5. **No source theorem may remain a final hypothesis.** The current conditional reduction is useful only after B3/B5 and their analytic inputs have actual kernel-clean proofs.
6. **No constant computation resolves a missing probability theorem.** LeanCert numerical fixtures establish only those exact inequalities.
7. **Minimize scope through centered specializations.** Nonzero means, smoothed analysis, limiting laws and lower bounds are unnecessary.
8. **Do not weaken an intermediate estimate without checking its recursion cost.** A factor polynomial in $n$ or in a changing rank can spoil the square-root target after iteration.
